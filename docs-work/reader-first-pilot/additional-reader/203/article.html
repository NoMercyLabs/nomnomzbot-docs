Timers and chat triggers let the bot handle messages you would otherwise repeat during a stream. A timer can remind viewers to take a break or explain a regular channel activity; a chat trigger can answer a phrase as it comes up in conversation. You manage both on Triggers and timers in the sidebar.

The difference is what starts the response. A timer waits for time and, optionally, enough chat activity. A chat trigger watches ordinary messages for the words you choose. Both can send a reply or run a command, action or pipeline, so you can start with a message and add automation later.

TabWhat it holds
TimersLines the bot posts on a schedule.
Chat triggersReplies to words in ordinary chat lines.

A chat trigger is not a command. A command starts with the prefix, such as !hello, and is made on Create a command.

Who can use this page

By default, a Moderator, Lead moderator, Editor or Broadcaster may open the page and change timers and chat triggers. The broadcaster can raise the required role on Permissions. Sign in and select your channel first; the bot must be running and connected to receive its chat and check its timers. The roles are explained on Permissions.

The Broadcaster may also let a VIP open each tab, with read access granted independently for timers and chat triggers. A VIP then sees the list only, and the editor does not open. For a VIP, the New timer and New trigger buttons show “You can view this, but your role cannot change it.”

Make a timer

For a first timer, make a reminder that rotates through two useful messages. Use a name such as Break reminder, keep Reaction on Simple reply, and enter one message on each line:

Take a moment to stretch and get some water.
New here? You are welcome to join the conversation or just watch.

The bot posts one line each time the timer fires, then moves to the next. This lets one timer vary its reminder without posting several messages at once.

  1. Open Triggers and timers in the sidebar, then the Timers tab.
  2. Choose New timer. The New timer dialog opens.
  3. Type a Name, for example Follow reminder.
  4. Under Reaction, choose Simple reply or Pipeline (see the table below).
  5. Set Minutes between posts. The default is 30 minutes.
  6. Choose Create timer. The dialog closes and the new timer shows in the list, switched on.
ReactionThe field to fill in
Simple replyMessages: one line per message. Blank lines are dropped.
PipelinePipeline: a command, a pipeline or an action for the timer to run.

A message can hold template helpers, such as {channel}. They are explained on Variables and templates.

When the list is empty, the tab shows “No timers yet.” Its Make the first timer button opens the same dialog.

The dialog stops with a reason in red when a field is wrong:

ProblemWhat the dialog shows
No name“Give the timer a name.”
Simple reply with no message“Write at least one message.”
Pipeline with nothing picked“Pick the command, action or pipeline the timer runs.”
Minutes outside the limits“Use a whole number from 1 to 1440 minutes.”

When a timer posts

The bot checks its timers about every 30 seconds. A timer posts when both of these are true:

  • Minutes between posts have passed since its last counted firing.
  • Chat has at least Chat lines needed new lines since its last counted firing.

A timer that has never posted is due at once. A new timer starts with Chat lines needed at 0, so it posts at the next check, within about 30 seconds.

A copy made with Duplicate keeps the Chat lines needed of the timer it copies. Once you switch the copy on, it posts at the next check after chat has had that many lines since the bot began watching your channel. After the first counted firing, it waits Minutes between posts between firings, and counts new chat lines from that firing.

When Chat lines needed is 0, a timer posts on time alone. The bot posts only in a channel it is connected to.

A timer with several messages posts them in turn, one per post. After the last message, it starts again at the first. Changing a simple-reply timer to Pipeline in the dashboard clears its messages, so it runs its command, pipeline or action each time. Choose an existing suitable command, pipeline or action for that reaction; a simple reply needs none.

A simple reply goes to Twitch chat by default. Send the reply to can send it to Chat overlay, Both or Nowhere instead; overlay delivery needs its browser source loaded in OBS. The shared delivery and appearance controls are explained on Create a command. A simple reply counts as a firing when chat accepts it for Twitch chat, an open overlay receives it for Chat overlay, or either receives it for Both. Nowhere counts without visible output. When that requested simple-reply delivery fails, the timer keeps the same line and timing state, and tries again at the next check. A bound command or action counts the recorded attempt even when it reports failure. A pipeline attempt also counts when it reports failure or the pipeline is missing, disabled or has no executable graph. A repeating timer then waits for its interval and required new chat lines; a recorded firing does not prove the action succeeded. An unexpected error before the reaction returns leaves that firing unrecorded.

Timers are not restricted to a live broadcast: the bot checks channels it is connected to. If a reminder is useful only while you stream, switch it off when you finish.

Choose a timer in the list to open it in the editor. The list shows each timer’s name and how often it posts.

Duplicate makes a copy named after the timer with “copy” added, such as Follow reminder copy. The copy uses the saved settings, is switched off, and opens in the editor.

Delete a timer

  1. Open the timer and choose Delete. A box asks “Delete name?”
  2. Choose Delete timer.

The timer is removed and stops posting. The editor closes and shows “Pick a timer to edit it.”

Make a chat trigger

For example, you could welcome a morning greeting without asking viewers to remember a command. Set Words to watch for to good morning and Reply to Good morning! Settle in and enjoy the stream. With the default matching mode, a message such as good morning everyone starts the reply too.

  1. Open Triggers and timers in the sidebar, then the Chat triggers tab.
  2. Choose New trigger. The New chat trigger dialog opens.
  3. Type the Words to watch for, for example good morning.
  4. Type the Reply, the line the bot sends. The reply may hold template helpers from Variables and templates.
  5. Choose Create trigger. The dialog closes and the new trigger shows in the list, switched on.

The dialog shows “Give the trigger some words to watch for.” or “Write the reply.” when a field is empty. When the list is empty, the tab shows “No chat triggers yet.” with a Make the first trigger button.

A new trigger starts with these settings, which you change in the editor:

SettingStarts at
How chat must matchContains the words
Match capital lettersOff
Seconds between replies30
Who can set it offEveryone

How chat must match

How chat must match decides how the words compare to a chat line.

ChoiceThe trigger fires when the chat line
Contains the wordsHolds the words anywhere, even inside a longer word.
Is exactly the wordsIs the words and nothing else.
Starts with the wordsBegins with the words.
Matches a patternMatches a regular expression, a pattern language for text.

With Contains the words, the words hi also match this. Use Is exactly the words or Matches a pattern when that is too wide.

Match capital letters off means Hello and hello are the same. A pattern that does not work as a regular expression is refused when you save.

When a chat trigger fires

The bot checks chat triggers only on ordinary chat lines:

  • A line that starts with the command prefix, or that runs a command, never sets off a chat trigger.
  • A line that plays a sound from the soundboard never sets off a chat trigger.

One chat line sets off one chat trigger at most. The bot checks a matching trigger’s Who can set it off before Seconds between replies. It can skip a match whose role requirement the sender does not meet. A matching trigger that is cooling down stops the search, so another trigger does not answer instead.

Who can set it off is the lowest role that may fire the trigger. The choices run from Everyone up to Broadcaster, as on Permissions.

Seconds between replies holds the whole channel. Once an eligible match fires, the trigger stays quiet for that many seconds. The broadcaster and moderators identified on the incoming platform message are not held back by it; a role stored only in the bot does not grant this bypass.

The bot keeps the value between 0 and 86400 seconds, one day.

Troubleshooting

When something is not right

A timer never posts. Check these, in order:

  1. Enabled. A timer marked Off posts nothing, and Post once switches it off after one counted firing, which can be a failed bound-reaction attempt.
  2. Chat lines needed. A quiet chat holds the timer until enough new lines arrive.
  3. Minutes between posts. After a post, the next one can come up to 30 seconds later than the number you set.

A chat trigger does not answer. Check these, in order:

  1. Enabled. A trigger marked Off answers nothing.
  2. The chat line. A line that starts with the command prefix never sets off a trigger.
  3. Who can set it off. The viewer’s role must be at least the role chosen.
  4. Seconds between replies. The trigger stays quiet after an answer.
