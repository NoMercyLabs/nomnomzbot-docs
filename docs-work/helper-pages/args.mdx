title: Command word helpers
description: The 64 helpers that read the words a viewer types after a command, with what each gives when a word is missing.
---

These helpers work in a command reply, and in a pipeline that a chat command starts.
They read the text after the command name, split on spaces.
Words count from 1, so in `!remind @ViewerTwo 10m`, `{args.1}` is `@ViewerTwo` and `{args.2}` is `10m`.
An event response or a timer message has no command text, so these helpers do not belong there.

A pipeline that a **Run pipeline** action calls gets the words that action passes as its positional arguments, and reads them as `{args.1}`, `{args.2}` and so on.
When something other than a chat command starts the pipeline, such as an event or a timer, every other form on this page stays in the message as you wrote it.

## Words after the command

These examples use the message `!example @ViewerTwo hello big world`, unless the row names another.

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{args}` | Everything typed after the command, as one text | `{args}` gives `@ViewerTwo hello big world` |
| `{args.<a>-<b>}` | The words from the a-th to the b-th, for example args.2-3 | `{args.2-3}` gives `hello big` |
| `{args.<n>}` | The n-th word after the command, as typed | `{args.1}` gives `@ViewerTwo` |
| `{args.<n>+}` | Every word from the n-th one to the end, for example args.2+ | `{args.2+}` gives `hello big world` |
| `{args.<n>.clean}` | The n-th word without a leading @ | `{args.1.clean}` gives `ViewerTwo` |
| `{args.<n>.isMention}` | True when the n-th word is an @mention | `{args.1.isMention}` gives `true` |
| `{args.<n>.isNumber}` | True when the n-th word is a number | `{args.1.isNumber}` gives `false` |
| `{args.<n>.length}` | How many characters the n-th word has | `{args.2.length}` gives `5` |
| `{args.<n>.number}` | The n-th word when it is a number, else the default after the colon or nothing | `{args.3.number:1}` gives `1` |
| `{args.<n>.upper}` | The n-th word in UPPERCASE | `{args.2.upper}` gives `HELLO` |
| `{args.<n>.urlEncoded}` | The n-th word made safe for a web address | `{args.1.urlEncoded}` gives `%40ViewerTwo` |
| `{args.<n>:<default>}` | The n-th word, or the default after the colon when the command has fewer words | `{args.5:nobody}` gives `nobody` |
| `{args.all.quoted}` | All the words, in double quotes | `{args.all.quoted}` gives `"@ViewerTwo hello big world"` |
| `{args.all.urlEncoded}` | All the words, made safe for a web address | `{args.all.urlEncoded}` gives `%40ViewerTwo%20hello%20big%20world` |
| `{args.count}` | How many words follow the command | `{args.count}` gives `4` |
| `{args.duration:<n>}` | The n-th word read as a time (5m, 1h30m, 90), in seconds | For `!remind @ViewerTwo 5m`, `{args.duration:2}` gives `300` |
| `{args.first}` | The first word after the command, with a leading @ kept | `{args.first}` gives `@ViewerTwo` |
| `{args.isEmpty}` | True when no words follow the command, false when some do | `{args.isEmpty}` gives `false` |
| `{args.last}` | The last word after the command | `{args.last}` gives `world` |
| `{args.mentions}` | Every @mention among the words, separated by a space | For `!hug @ViewerTwo @ViewerThree`, `{args.mentions}` gives `@ViewerTwo @ViewerThree` |
| `{args.mentions.count}` | How many @mentions are among the words | `{args.mentions.count}` gives `1` |
| `{args.numbers}` | Every word that is a number, separated by a space | For `!roll 42 7`, `{args.numbers}` gives `42 7` |
| `{args.rest}` | Every word after the first one | `{args.rest}` gives `hello big world` |

## When a word is missing

A missing word does not always give an empty result.
`{args.3}` stays in the reply as you wrote it, `{args.3:nobody}` gives `nobody`, and the person helpers give nothing.

| You write | The word is missing, so you get |
| --- | --- |
| `{args.3}` | `{args.3}`, as written |
| `{args.3:nobody}` | `nobody`, the text after the colon |
| `{args.3.number}` | Nothing. With `{args.3.number:1}` you get `1`, which you also get when the word is not a number |
| `{args.3+}` or `{args.3-4}` | Nothing |
| `{args.3.upper}`, `{args.3.clean}`, `{args.3.length}` or `{args.3.urlEncoded}` | The helper, as written |
| `{args.3.isNumber}` or `{args.3.isMention}` | `false` |
| `{args.first}`, `{args.last}` or `{args.rest}` | Nothing |
| `{args.duration:3}` | `{args.duration:3}`, as written |
| `{args.3.user.display}` and every other person helper | Nothing |

When the viewer types no words at all, `{args}`, `{args.mentions}`, `{args.numbers}` and `{args.all.urlEncoded}` give nothing, `{args.count}` and `{args.mentions.count}` give `0`, `{args.isEmpty}` gives `true`, and `{args.all.quoted}` gives `""`.

So a reply that may run without a word should use a form with a fallback, such as `{args.1:friend}`.

## Read a duration

`{args.duration:2}` reads the second word as a time and gives it in seconds.
Here the number after the colon is the position of the word, not a fallback as in `{args.2:friend}`.
A word can use `s`, `m`, `h`, `d` and `w` for seconds, minutes, hours, days and weeks, chain them as in `1h30m`, or be a plain number of seconds such as `90`.
Capital letters work too, so `5m` and `5M` both give `300`, and `1h30m` gives `5400`.

A missing word, or a word that is not a time such as `soon` or `1h30`, leaves `{args.duration:2}` in the reply as you wrote it.
For a command that takes a viewer name and then a duration, the reply `{args.1.user.display}, see you in {args.duration:2} seconds` turns `!remind @ViewerTwo 10m` into `ViewerTwo, see you in 600 seconds`.
`!remind @ViewerTwo` alone gives `ViewerTwo, see you in {args.duration:2} seconds`.

There is no fallback for a duration.
When you need one, ask viewers for a plain number of seconds, such as `600`, and read it with `{args.2.number:60}`.
That gives `60` when the word is missing, and also when it is not a number, so a viewer who types `10m` gets `60` with no warning.

## Read the person named in a word

`{args.1.user.display}` and the other person helpers read the word as a viewer name, with a leading `@` dropped, and then give a fact about that viewer.
When the word is missing they give nothing, so `!hug` alone makes `{args.1.user.display}` empty.

Use `{args.1.user.display}` when the reply names a viewer.
It gives the display name the bot knows, capital letters included, or the word without the `@` when the viewer is unknown.
`{args.1.clean}` gives the word as typed, minus the `@`, and unlike the person helper it stays as written when the word is missing.

For example, the reply `{args.1.user.display} has followed for {args.1.user.followAge}` for `!since @ViewerTwo` gives `ViewerTwo has followed for 9 months and 21 days`.
For a viewer who does not follow, `{args.1.user.followAge}` gives `not following`.

The points helpers read the named viewer's balance in this bot's [channel currency](/reference/economy/currency-and-balances), not their Twitch Channel Points.
A viewer with no balance yet reads as `0`.
A viewer who is not on the currency ranking gets nothing from `{args.1.user.points.rank}`, and the top viewer gets nothing from the two next-rank helpers, because nobody is above them.

## When viewers type helpers

With the **Viewer Templates** setting on, a viewer who holds the template permission can type helpers in the text after a command, and the bot fills them in once, when the message arrives.
The setting is on by default, and its switch is on the [Features](/reference/settings/features) page.

The permission has the key `reward:input:template`, because the same permission covers the text a viewer types into a reward.
Twitch subscribers and higher hold it by default.
To change that, open the **Permissions** settings page and go to the **Actions** tab.
Type `reward:input:template` into **Search actions**, open the **Rewards** area, and pick the lowest role that may use it in the list beside the action.

Once the bot has filled in the viewer's helpers, only `{args.1}`, `{args.2}` and the other plain numbered words, and `{args.count}`, still show what the viewer actually typed.
Every other form on this page reads the filled-in text, including `{args}`, `{args.first}` and a word with a fallback such as `{args.1:friend}`.
