---
title: AutoMod and chat filters
description: Set the four AutoMod filters and the Twitch AutoMod levels, and make, try, switch off and remove your own chat filters.
---

Filters let you handle recurring chat problems without asking a moderator to respond to every message.
NomNomzBot offers simple switches for common problems, custom rules for your channel, and access to Twitch's own AutoMod settings.
These systems act differently, so choose the one that fits the result you want.

Use **AutoMod filters** for the bot's link, capitals, phrase and emote checks.
Use **Chat filters** when you need your own matching rule or a choice between deleting, timing out and asking for review.
Both pages are in the **Access** group of **Settings**; their page heading also calls them **Moderation**.

| Page | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **AutoMod filters** | Four fixed filters of the bot, and the Twitch AutoMod levels. |
| **Chat filters** | The filters you make: a pattern, a list of blocked terms or a link rule. |

## Who can use these pages

These are the default channel roles; the broadcaster can adjust action access on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

| Who | What they may do |
| --- | --- |
| A **Moderator** | Open both pages and see the settings. |
| A **Lead moderator** | Change the four AutoMod filters, and make, change and remove chat filters. |
| The **Broadcaster** | All of the above, and change the Twitch AutoMod levels. |

On **AutoMod filters**, someone who may only look sees the settings but cannot change them.
On **Chat filters**, that person sees only the list: each filter's name, what it checks, its action, its match count, and **On** or **Off**.
They cannot open a filter to see its pattern, terms or other fields.

## Set the AutoMod filters

The four switches start off.
When enabled, they delete matching messages, while the broadcaster and moderators remain exempt.
The initial capitals threshold is 70 percent, and the emote limit is 10 emotes per message.

1. Open **AutoMod filters** in the settings.
   The page shows four panels: **Links**, **Capital letters**, **Banned phrases** and **Emote spam**.
2. Switch a filter on or off, and fill in its fields (see the table below).
   **Save** at the top of the page becomes active.
3. Choose **Save**.
   The page shows "AutoMod saved." The change applies to chat at once.

| Panel | Switch | Field | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Links** | **Delete links** | **Allowed domains** | Deletes a message that holds a link. A link on the allowed list passes. |
| **Capital letters** | **Delete shouting** | **Capital letters, percent** | Deletes a message with this share of capitals or more. |
| **Banned phrases** | **Delete banned phrases** | **Banned phrases** | Deletes a message that holds one of these phrases, in any letter case. |
| **Emote spam** | **Delete emote spam** | **Most emotes in a message** | Deletes a message with more emotes than this. |

**Allowed domains** and **Banned phrases** take one entry per line.
Blank lines and entries typed twice drop out when you save.

### Matching details

An allowed domain also allows its subdomains: allowing `example.com` permits `www.example.com`.
One disallowed link still makes the message match, even if it also contains an allowed link.
Banned phrases match anywhere in the message, so a short phrase can also match inside a longer word.
Blocked terms in a chat filter match the same way.

The capitals check counts capital letters as a share of letters, ignoring punctuation and numbers in that percentage.
It skips messages with fewer than five letters and, with the dashboard's normal configuration, messages shorter than ten characters.

**Capital letters, percent** takes a number from 1 to 100.
**Most emotes in a message** takes a number from 1 to 50.
A number outside its range shows "Use a number from 1 to 100." or "Use a number from 1 to 50.", and **Save** stays off.

## Set the Twitch AutoMod levels

The **Twitch AutoMod** panel sits under the four filters.
Twitch's own AutoMod holds messages before they appear in chat, giving moderators a chance to approve or deny them in the [review queue](/reference/moderation/overview).
The bot's four filters delete messages after they arrive, so approving a review cannot bring those messages back.
The Twitch levels are saved separately from the bot's switches.

1. In the **Twitch AutoMod** panel, pick a level for each category.
2. Choose **Save Twitch levels**.
   The page shows "Twitch AutoMod saved."

| Level choice |
| --- |
| **Off** |
| **Level 1, lightest** |
| **Level 2** |
| **Level 3** |
| **Level 4, strictest** |

The eight categories are **Aggression**, **Bullying**, **Disability**, **Misogyny**, **Race, ethnicity or religion**, **Sex-based terms**, **Sexuality, sex or gender** and **Swearing**.

**Save** at the top of the page does not save these levels.
Only **Save Twitch levels** does.

## Make a chat filter

A chat filter checks every message against a pattern, a list of blocked terms or a link rule.
When a message matches, the bot takes the action you pick.

1. Open **Chat filters** in the settings and choose **New filter**.
   The **New chat filter** editor takes the place of the list.
2. Type a name in **Name**.
3. Choose **What it checks**, and fill in the fields it shows (see the table below).
4. Choose **When a message matches** (see [What a filter does](#what-a-filter-does)).
5. Choose **Never filter**: the lowest role the filter skips.
6. Check the filter with **Try it** (see [Try a filter before you save it](#try-a-filter-before-you-save-it)).
7. Choose **Save filter**.
   The page shows "Chat filter saved." and the list comes back with the new filter.
   With **Filter is on** left on, the filter acts on chat at once, live or offline.

| **What it checks** | The fields it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Blocked terms** | **Blocked terms, one per line** |
| **Pattern (regex)** | **Pattern**: a regular expression. |
| **Links** | **Allowed domains, one per line**, and **Also catch links without https** |

**Also catch links without https** treats text like `example.com/x` as a link.

For example, to review unwanted links without removing them immediately, choose **Links**, leave **Also catch links without https** on, and select **Flag for review**.
Add domains that your community is allowed to share, such as your own website, one per line.
Use **Try it** with both an allowed link and an unwanted link before saving, then check the [Home review queue](/reference/moderation/overview) when a Twitch message matches.

| Field | Default |
| --- | --- |
| **What it checks** | **Blocked terms** |
| **When a message matches** | **Delete the message** |
| **Timeout length, in seconds** | 600 seconds |
| **Never filter** | **Moderators and above** |
| **Also catch links without https** | On |
| **Match upper and lower case apart** | Off |
| **Filter is on** | On |

When the list is empty, it shows "No chat filters yet."

### What the editor refuses

The editor checks the filter when you choose **Save filter**.
A refused field shows its reason, and nothing is saved.

| Rule | What the editor shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** is empty | "Give the filter a name." |
| **Pattern** is empty for a pattern filter | "Enter a pattern." |
| No term is in the list for a blocked-terms filter | "Add at least one term." |
| The timeout length is under 1 second | "Use a length of at least 1 second." |

The bot also refuses a pattern that is not a valid regular expression.
The editor then shows "The chat filter could not be saved:" with the reason.

## What a filter does

**When a message matches** picks the bot's action.

| Choice | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Delete the message** | The bot deletes the message. |
| **Time the person out** | The bot times the sender out for **Timeout length, in seconds**. |
| **Hold for review** | The bot removes the message. On Twitch, it also puts the message in the review queue. Allowing that review does not restore the message. |
| **Flag for review** | On Twitch, the message stays in chat and goes to the review queue. This action does not flag Kick or YouTube messages. |
| **Step up the escalation ladder** | Removes the message and applies the [escalation ladder](/reference/moderation/trust-and-escalation) on Twitch. If the ladder is off or unavailable, it applies the filter's timeout instead. Kick and YouTube use that timeout directly. |

**Timeout length, in seconds** shows only when you choose **Time the person out**.
A filter made with **Step up the escalation ladder** has no length of its own, so its fallback timeout is 600 seconds.
A filter you switch from **Time the person out** keeps the length it last saved.
You cannot change that length while **Step up the escalation ladder** is chosen.

**Never filter** picks the lowest role the filter skips.
That role and every role above it are never filtered.
The choices run from **Twitch subscribers and above** to **Only the broadcaster**.

The broadcaster and moderators are always exempt from automatic chat filters, regardless of this choice.
The setting lets you exempt additional viewers, such as subscribers or VIPs; choosing a higher level does not make the bot filter moderators.
When several custom filters match, the bot handles only the first matching enabled filter, starting with the oldest one.
The list shows the newest filter first, so the filter at the bottom of the list acts first.
A filter whose **Never filter** choice skips the sender does not count as a match.
The next matching filter still acts on that message.

Chat filters run only in a channel whose broadcaster has finished onboarding.
Before that, the bot does not check chat against the custom filters.

| **Never filter** choice |
| --- |
| **Twitch subscribers and above** |
| **VIPs and above** |
| **Artists and above** |
| **Moderators and above** |
| **Lead moderators and above** |
| **Editors and above** |
| **Only the broadcaster** |

## Try a filter before you save it

The **Try it** panel sits under the editor.
It checks a message against the filter as you have it now.
Nothing is saved or sent.

1. Type a message in **A sample chat message**.
2. Choose **Try message**.
   The panel shows "This message would match." or "This message would not match."

For a pattern that is not valid, the panel shows "The pattern is not valid:" with the reason.

The test ignores **Never filter**, the broadcaster and moderator exemption, and whether the bot can act.
A match here does not promise an action in chat.

## Change a chat filter

1. Choose **Edit** on the filter's row.
   The editor opens with the filter's name in its title.
2. Change the fields.
   The **What it checks** choice of a saved filter does not change; for a different type, make a new filter.
3. Choose **Save filter**.
   The page shows "Chat filter saved."

**Cancel** closes the editor and keeps nothing you changed.

Each row shows what the filter checks, its action and how many times the filter acted, for example "Blocked terms · Delete the message · 3 matches".
A match that the platform refused is not counted.
A **Flag for review** match on a Kick or YouTube message is not counted either.

## Switch a chat filter on or off

Use the switch on the filter's row.
The change takes effect at once, with no **Save**.
A filter that is off does not check chat.

## Remove a chat filter

1. Choose **Remove** on the filter's row.
   A dialog asks "Remove" and the filter's name, with a question mark.
2. Choose **Remove filter**.
   The filter leaves the list.

The filter stops checking chat at once.
Its match count is lost.

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**Save does nothing on the AutoMod filters page.**
**Save** is active only after you change something, and only when every number is in its range.
Check **Capital letters, percent** and **Most emotes in a message** for a red message.

::

::Troubleshooting

**The Twitch levels went back after I left the page.**
**Save** at the top of the page saves only the four filters.
Choose **Save Twitch levels** in the **Twitch AutoMod** panel.

::

::Troubleshooting

**A filter never catches a moderator.**
The bot always skips the broadcaster and moderators.
Changing **Never filter** does not remove that protection.
Use the manual chat tools when a moderator's message needs attention.

::

::Troubleshooting

**A filter matched, but the message stayed in chat.**
The platform refused to delete the message or to time the person out.
The message stays in chat, and an item appears in your attention inbox: "AutoMod could not delete a message" or "The `{filter}` filter could not act on `{username}`", with the filter's name and the person's name filled in.
Read the item, then act on that message by hand.

::
