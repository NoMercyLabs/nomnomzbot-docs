title: Shoutouts
description: Set how the bot shouts out other streamers, send its suggested shoutouts, and see how it keeps within the Twitch shoutout limits.

You set the channel's shoutouts on the **Shoutouts** settings page.
You send suggested shoutouts from the **Quick actions** panel on **Home**.
A shoutout is a Twitch shoutout plus a chat announcement from the bot.

| Where | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Automatic shoutouts** on **Shoutouts** | One switch: the bot shouts out a known streamer by itself. |
| **Shoutout message** on **Shoutouts** | The line the bot announces in chat. |
| **Own shoutout line** in a person's profile | A per-streamer line for that one streamer, and a **Shout out** button. |
| **Suggested shoutouts** on **Home** | Chatters of this stream that you may want to shout out. |

The bot has no built-in shoutout command.
For chat to ask for a shoutout, make a command that runs a pipeline with a **Shoutout** step.

[Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command) explains how to make the command.
[Pipelines](/reference/commands/pipelines) explains the steps a command runs.

### Make a shoutout command

1. Open **Pipelines** in the sidebar and choose **New pipeline**.
   Type a name in **Name** and choose **Make pipeline**.
   The editor of the new pipeline opens, with "This pipeline has no steps yet."
2. Choose **Add a step at the end**, then choose **Shoutout** in **Steps to add**.
   The step joins the pipeline, and its settings open in **Step details**.
3. In **User ID**, type `{args.1}`.
   That is the first word chat types after the command: the name of the streamer to shout out.
4. Choose **Save** above the steps.
   The page shows "Pipeline saved."
5. Open **Commands** in the sidebar and choose **New command**.
   The **New command** dialog opens.
6. Type `so` in **Name**, choose **Runs a pipeline** in **What it does**, and choose this pipeline in **Pipeline**.
7. Choose **Create command**.
   The dialog closes and the new command opens in the editor, switched on (see [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command)).

To test it, type `!so` and the name of a streamer in Twitch chat.
The bot posts the shoutout announcement in chat.
When a rate limit is active, the bot tells chat that the shoutout is queued, in the channel's [personality](/reference/settings/bot-basics-and-personality).
With the default personality, the line is `Shoutout for {target.displayname} queued.`, where `{target.displayname}` is the name of the streamer who is shouted out.

A **Test run** in the editor tries the steps but sends nothing, so it gives no shoutout.

## Who can use this page

A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the **Shoutouts** page.
The same roles may change its settings and send a suggested shoutout.
Someone who may only see the page sees the switch and the message, but cannot change them.

## What a shoutout does

1. The bot sends the Twitch shoutout for the other channel.
2. When Twitch accepts it, the bot posts its announcement in chat.
3. When speech is on for the shoutout, the bot also reads the announcement aloud, in the channel's one bot voice.
   A pipeline's shoutout step turns speech on. It is on by default for a raid.

The announcement posts only after Twitch accepts the shoutout.
The one exception is a chat request inside the per-streamer wait (see [Rate limits](#rate-limits)).
When Twitch refuses it for another reason than a rate limit, nothing is posted.

When Twitch refuses it for a rate limit, or the connection to Twitch drops, the shoutout and its announcement wait in the queue together (see [Rate limits](#rate-limits)).
The announcement posts when Twitch accepts the shoutout later.

## Turn on automatic shoutouts

1. Open the settings and choose **Shoutouts** in the **Bot** group.
   The page shows the **Automatic shoutouts** card on top.
2. Switch on **Shout out a known streamer automatically**.
   The switch saves at once.

Default: off.

A known streamer is a chatter whose own channel uses this NomNomzBot server.
The bot looks at each chatter once per stream, on their first chat.
It shouts a known streamer out when all of these are true:

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| The stream has run for at least | 10 minutes |
| The time since their first chat is at least | 5 minutes |
| The time since the channel's last shoutout is at least | 8 minutes |
| The time since this streamer's last shoutout here is at least | 60 minutes |
| This streamer had no shoutout yet this stream | yes |

The 8 minutes count from any shoutout in the channel.
For an automatic shoutout, they replace the 2-minute channel wait (see [Rate limits](#rate-limits)).
An automatic shoutout also waits while another shoutout is in the queue.

The bot never shouts out the broadcaster or itself.
When the stream ends, the waiting automatic shoutouts are dropped.
When you switch the setting off, the waiting automatic shoutouts are dropped too.

## Set the shoutout message

1. Open the settings and choose **Shoutouts** in the **Bot** group.
   The **Shoutout message** card is below **Automatic shoutouts**.
2. Type the line in **Message**, for example `Go follow {target.displayname}!`.
   The bot puts the name of the streamer who is shouted out in place of `{target.displayname}`.
   The field holds up to 500 characters.
3. Choose **Save**.
   The page shows "Shoutout message saved."

**Save** stays off until the text differs from the saved message.
To go back to the built-in line, choose **Use the default**.
It clears the field and saves at once.

With no message, the bot uses its built-in line.
That line follows the bot's [personality](/reference/settings/bot-basics-and-personality).

### Which message the bot uses

The bot picks the first message that is set, in this order:

1. A message given in the shoutout step itself, in a pipeline.
2. Your per-streamer line for that one streamer, set in their profile (see [Write a line for one streamer](#write-a-line-for-one-streamer)).
3. The **Message** of the streamer who is shouted out, when their own channel uses this NomNomzBot server.
4. Your own **Message**.
5. The built-in line.

So your **Message** is also how another channel on this server announces you.
That channel's per-streamer line for you wins over it.

## Write a line for one streamer

You can give one streamer their own per-streamer line.
It is set in that person's profile dialog, on the **Data** tab, under **Own shoutout line**.

1. Open **Chat** in the sidebar ([Chat page](/reference/community/chat-page)) and choose the person's name on one of their chat lines.
   Their profile dialog opens.
   A name in **Suggested shoutouts** on **Home** opens the same dialog.
2. Choose the **Data** tab.
   Under **Own shoutout line** it shows "Uses the channel shoutout line." when no line is set.
3. Choose **Write line**, or **Edit** when a line exists.
   The **Shoutout line for** dialog opens, with the person's name.
4. Type the line in **Shoutout line**, up to 500 characters.
5. Choose **Save line**.
   The dialog closes and the line shows under **Own shoutout line**.

On the **People** page in the sidebar ([People](/reference/community/people)), the profile of the person you choose shows beside the list, with the same **Data** tab.

To drop the line, choose **Remove** under **Own shoutout line**.
A dialog asks to remove the shoutout line for the person.
Choose **Remove** again.

Shoutouts of that streamer then use the next message in the order above.
Someone whose role cannot change it sees "Your role cannot change a shoutout line."

The profile also has a **Shout out** button, which sends a shoutout for that person.
The dashboard then shows "Shouted out" with their name.

## Send a suggested shoutout

When the channel is live, **Home** shows **Suggested shoutouts** in the **Quick actions** panel.
The heading counts the suggestions you have not sent yet.

1. Open **Home** in the sidebar and find the chatter under **Suggested shoutouts**.
   Each row shows how many streams they had in the last 30 days, and their last category.
2. Choose **Shoutout** to send it.
   The row shows "Shoutout sent."
3. Or choose **Not now**.
   The chatter is hidden for the rest of this stream.

When the shoutout does not go out, the row shows "The shoutout did not go out. Try again in a moment."

The list holds up to 10 chatters, the most active streamers first.
It shows only chatters who streamed in the last 30 days.

It leaves out the broadcaster, the bot, and chatters who already had a shoutout this stream.
When the channel is offline, the list is empty and the **Suggested shoutouts** heading is hidden.

## Rate limits

Twitch limits how often a channel may give a shoutout.
By default, the bot waits 2 minutes between shoutouts in the channel.
It waits 60 minutes between two shoutouts of the same streamer, the per-streamer wait.

The bot keeps within these limits with one queue per channel.

- A shoutout asked for while a limit is active waits in the queue, with its announcement.
  The announcement posts when the shoutout goes out.
- A queued shoutout is also held back while an earlier one waits, so the order stays fair.
- A raid shoutout goes ahead of the other waiting shoutouts.
- When a chat request waits, the bot tells chat that the shoutout is queued, in the channel's personality (see [Make a shoutout command](#make-a-shoutout-command)).

A chat request for a streamer inside their per-streamer wait posts the announcement only, without a new Twitch shoutout.
The bot also reads that announcement aloud.
It posts that announcement at most once every 2 minutes for the same streamer.

The **Shoutout** step of a pipeline can set its own wait times, in its **Step details**.
Default: 60 minutes per streamer and 2 minutes for the channel.

::Troubleshooting
**A shoutout from chat did not show at once.**
It waits in the queue for a Twitch limit.
It goes out when the limit ends.
::
