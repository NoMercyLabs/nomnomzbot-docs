You tune two moderation policies on two settings pages.
Both pages sit in the **Access** group of the settings sidebar.
**Trust policy** sets how the bot scores each viewer's trust and heat.
**Escalation** sets how the bot punishes a viewer who breaks the rules again and again.

## Who can use these pages

The table shows the default roles.
You can change who may do what on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

| Page | Who may open it | Who may change it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trust policy** | A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** | The **Broadcaster** |
| **Escalation** | A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** | A **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** |

Someone who may only open a page sees every value but cannot change it.
The **Save** and **Reset to defaults** buttons show only to someone who may change the page.

## Change a policy

Both pages work the same way.

1. Open the page in the **Access** group of the settings sidebar.
   Each field shows its default next to its name, for example "Default 168".
2. Change one or more fields.
   **Save** becomes active.
3. Choose **Save**.
   The bot stores all fields at once.

If the bot refuses a value, the reason shows under the field it names.
**Reset to defaults** puts every field back to its default.
You still choose **Save** to store the reset.

## Trust policy

The bot keeps two numbers for each viewer in your channel: a trust score and heat.
Both run from 0 to 100.
The bot works them out again after each moderation action against the viewer.
The profile dialog shows the score as **Trust score** under **Bot standing**.

The **Trust policy** page holds every number behind the score and the heat.
The fields sit in six panels.

::Callout

The trust score uses only five facts about a viewer today.
They are how long the bot has known the viewer, the Twitch affiliate or partner status, a verified YouTube channel, the number of timeouts and the number of bans.
The page shows more fields than these.
Only the fields named below as used change the score today.

::

### How the score is made

1. The bot takes how long it has known the viewer, in months.
   **Account age growth** sets how fast this signal climbs to 100.
   A higher number climbs faster.
2. The bot multiplies the signal by the **Account age** weight.
3. The bot multiplies the result by the **Not following multiplier**.
   Today this happens for every viewer.
4. The bot adds the **Affiliate boost** or **Partner boost** for a Twitch affiliate or partner, and the **Verified YouTube boost** for a verified YouTube channel.
   The score does not go above 100.
5. The bot takes off the **Timeout penalty** once for each timeout, and the **Ban penalty** once for each ban.
   The score does not go below 0.

With the defaults, a viewer with no special status and no punishments reaches at most 18.75.

| Field | Panel | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Account age** | Score weights | 0.25 |
| **Account age growth** | Growth speed | 0.499 |
| **Not following multiplier** | Boosts and multipliers | 0.75 |
| **Affiliate boost** | Boosts and multipliers | 5 |
| **Partner boost** | Boosts and multipliers | 10 |
| **Verified YouTube boost** | Boosts and multipliers | 10 |
| **Timeout penalty** | Penalties | 10 |
| **Ban penalty** | Penalties | 30 |

### Allowed values

Each weight is between 0 and 1.
The four weights must add up to exactly 1.
The **Score weights** panel shows the live total, for example "Total 1 of 1".
Each growth speed is above 0 and at most 10.
A boost is between 0 and 100 points.
A multiplier is between 0 and 1.
A penalty is between 0 and 100 points.
Each tier ceiling is between 0 and 100, and higher than the one before it.

### Heat

Heat measures recent trouble.
Each event in the table adds heat.
Heat then fades by half for each half-life that passes.
Heat never goes above 100.

These fields sit in the **Heat** panel.

| Field | Default |
| --- | --- |
| **Heat half-life (hours)** | 24 |
| **Heat for a ban** | 40 |
| **Heat for a timeout** | 15 |
| **Heat for a confirmed report** | 10 |
| **Heat for a denied AutoMod message** | 5 |
| **Heat for a filter hit** | 5 |

When a viewer's heat rises to 80 or more, the bot puts the viewer in the review queue on the Home page.
A moderator then decides what to do.
No field on this page changes the 80.

The half-life is above 0 and at most one year (8760 hours).
A heat amount is between 0 and 1000.
A denied AutoMod message is a message that AutoMod holds back.
A filter hit is a message that trips one of your [chat filters](/reference/moderation/automod-and-chat-filters).
A confirmed report is explained on [Reports](/reference/moderation/reports).

## Escalation

The **Escalation** page sets the escalation ladder.
The ladder counts each viewer's offenses.
Each new offense moves the viewer one step up the ladder.
Each step takes an action: a warning, a timeout or a ban.

### What counts as an offense

These events count as an offense:

- A Twitch message trips a [chat filter](/reference/moderation/automod-and-chat-filters) whose action is **Step up the escalation ladder**.
- With **Count AutoMod holds** on, AutoMod or spam defense would time out or ban the viewer.
  The ladder step takes the place of that punishment, so the viewer is punished once.

A timeout or ban that a moderator gives by hand does not count.

### What each action does

- **Warn** sends the viewer a Twitch warning with the reason.
- **Timeout** times the viewer out for the step's length.
- **Ban** bans the viewer.

The bot takes the action in the name of the channel owner.

### Policy

| Field | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Escalation ladder** | Punishes repeat offenders with a stronger action each time. | Off |
| **Count AutoMod holds** | AutoMod and spam defense actions count as offenses. | Off |
| **Offense window (hours)** | How long a run of offenses lasts, counted from the first offense of the run. | 168 (one week) |
| **Default timeout** | The length a timeout step takes when it has no length of its own. | 10:00 (10 minutes) |

The ladder ships switched off.
It punishes nobody until you switch on **Escalation ladder** and choose **Save**.
**Count AutoMod holds** works only while **Escalation ladder** is on.

The offense window is 1 to 8760 hours.

The window starts at a viewer's first offense.
Each offense inside the window adds 1 to the count.
The first offense after the window ends sets the count back to 1 and starts a new window.

### Type a length of time

**Default timeout** and each **Timeout length** box take minutes and seconds.

- Type minutes, a colon and two digits of seconds, for example `4:30` for four and a half minutes.
- Or type a whole number, which counts as minutes, for example `1` for one minute.

The shortest length is `0:01`, one second.
The longest is `20160:00`, which is 14 days.
A length outside that range, or text that is not a length, marks the box as invalid.

The default next to **Default timeout** shows in seconds: "Default 600" is 10:00.

### Ladder

Each step starts at an offense number.
The numbers must go up from step to step.

| **At offense** | **Action** | **Timeout length** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Warn** | |
| 2 | **Timeout** | 1:00 (one minute) |
| 3 | **Timeout** | 10:00 (ten minutes) |
| 4 | **Timeout** | 60:00 (one hour) |
| 5 | **Timeout** | 1440:00 (one day) |
| 6 | **Ban** | |

The table shows the default ladder.
When a viewer's offense count is past the last step, the last step applies again.

### Edit the ladder

1. Type a number in **At offense** to set where a step starts.
2. Choose **Warn**, **Timeout** or **Ban** in **Action**.
   A **Timeout** step shows a **Timeout length** box, filled with the **Default timeout**.
3. To change the length, type it in the **Timeout length** box.
   Use minutes and seconds, as in [Type a length of time](#type-a-length-of-time).
4. Choose **Add step** to add a step at the bottom.
   The new step starts one offense after the last step and is a **Warn** step.
5. Choose **Remove step** on a row to delete that step.
6. Choose **Save**.

The ladder holds 1 to 50 steps.
**Add step** is inactive at 50 steps.
**Remove step** is inactive when only one step is left.

The saved ladder applies from the next offense.
A viewer keeps the offense count they already have.

::Troubleshooting

**The ladder does nothing.**
**Escalation ladder** is off by default.
Switch it on and choose **Save**.

::

::Troubleshooting

**A viewer's old offenses are not counted.**
The window started at the viewer's first offense, and it has ended.
The next offense sets the count back to 1, as in [Policy](#policy).
To count offenses for longer, set a higher **Offense window (hours)** and choose **Save**.

::
