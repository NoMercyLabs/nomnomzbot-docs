---
title: White-label bot
description: Connect your own bot account on Twitch so the bot chats as that account, and go back to the shared bot.
---

A separate Twitch account gives your channel's bot its own identity.
You connect it on **Settings**, then **White-label bot** in the **Bot** group.
Your channel's commands and settings still belong to the same channel.

You can use the bot without making a separate account.
When the bot sends a chat message, it uses your own bot account when one is connected.
Without one, it uses the shared bot when one is configured, and otherwise the broadcaster's account.
[Bot account](/reference/getting-started/bot-account) explains the accounts in more detail.

## Who can use this page

The broadcaster may open the page.
Only the broadcaster may connect, change or disconnect the bot account.

The broadcaster can give a named person permission to view the bot account through **Per person** on the [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions) page.
That person can inspect the status; connecting or disconnecting an account remains broadcaster-only.

## Read the status

The card **Your channel's own bot** shows which account chats for your channel.

| Badge | What the card shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Connected** | Your own bot account: its name, its `@` handle and its picture. |
| **Shared bot** | "Your channel uses the shared bot" with the shared bot's name, or **Shared bot** when it has no name. |

On a self-hosted bot without a shared account, the card still says **Shared bot**, but your own Twitch account types the replies.

When the status cannot load, the card shows "The bot account could not be loaded." and lets you try again.

## Connect your own bot

Use a Twitch account that you made for the bot.
Sign in to Twitch as that account, not as yourself.
Use a private window if you are signed in to Twitch as yourself.
You cannot use the shared bot's own account for this.

A hosted channel needs a plan that includes its own bot account.
Self-hosted channels always have it.

1. Open the **White-label bot** page and choose **Connect your own bot**.
   The card shows "Starting the sign-in...", and then a sign-in code with "Waiting for you to approve the code..."
2. Choose **Open** followed by the Twitch site name.
   The Twitch page opens in a new tab.
3. Sign in there as the bot account and type the code.
4. Approve the code on Twitch.
   A message pops up: "Your bot account is connected."
   The badge changes to **Connected**.

If the dashboard reports that it is not a moderator, follow the instructions on [Bot account](/reference/getting-started/bot-account#keep-the-bot-a-moderator).

Choose **Cancel** to stop the sign-in.
The button shows next to the code, and under a sign-in that failed.

<details>
<summary>Sign in with the browser instead of the code</summary>

Under the code, the card shows "Can't use the code? Sign in on the Twitch page in this browser instead."
Choose **Sign in with the browser**.
The Twitch sign-in page opens in a new tab.
If it cannot start, a message pops up: "The Twitch sign-in could not be started."

</details>

## Use another bot account

1. On the **White-label bot** page, find the row **Use another account**.
2. Choose **Change account**.
   The same sign-in code steps start as in [Connect your own bot](#connect-your-own-bot).
3. Approve the code as the new bot account.
   The new account replaces the current one.

## Go back to the shared bot

1. On the **White-label bot** page, find the row **Go back to the shared bot**.
2. Choose **Disconnect**.
   A dialog asks "Disconnect" followed by the bot's name.
3. Choose **Disconnect** in the dialog.
   A message pops up: "Your bot account is disconnected. The shared bot speaks again."

The bot goes back to the next available account in the fallback order.
On a self-hosted bot without a shared account, that is the broadcaster's account.
The Twitch account itself stays as it is.
The stored sign-in of the bot account is revoked, unless another channel still uses that bot.
To use the account again, connect it again with the sign-in steps.
If the disconnect fails, a message pops up: "The bot account could not be disconnected."

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**The card shows "The code ran out of time. Start again to get a new one."**
The code was not approved in time.
Choose **Try again** for a new code.

::

::Troubleshooting

**The card shows "The sign-in was denied on Twitch."**
The approval was refused on Twitch.
Choose **Try again** and approve the code.

::

::Troubleshooting

**The card shows "The sign-in did not finish. Try again."**
Two causes are common.
You signed in with the shared bot's own account, which a channel cannot use as its own bot.
Or your hosted channel's plan does not include its own bot account.
Sign in with a different account, or check your plan.

::

When the sign-in stops for another reason, the card shows "The sign-in did not finish. Try again." and a **Try again** button.
