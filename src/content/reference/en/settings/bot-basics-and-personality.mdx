Your commands give the bot things to do; these settings shape how it fits into your channel.
You can change how viewers call commands, choose the wording of built-in replies, and decide when the bot welcomes people chatting during a stream.
Open the three pages from the settings sidebar, described in the [dashboard tour](/reference/getting-started/dashboard-tour).

| Sidebar entry | Card on the page | What you set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bot basics** | **Commands and language** | The command prefix, the language, the timezone and the viewer activity levels. |
| **Bot personality** | **Reply voice** | The tone of the bot's built-in commands. |
| **Engagement triggers** | **Greetings and streaks** | First-time and returning greetings, plus saved streak controls whose current limitation is explained below. |

## Who can use these pages

| Page | Who may open it | Who may change it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bot basics** | A **Moderator** and every role above it. | The **Broadcaster** only. |
| **Bot personality** | A **Moderator** and every role above it. | The **Broadcaster** only. |
| **Engagement triggers** | A **Moderator** and every role above it. | A **Moderator** and every role above it. |

The **Broadcaster** may also let a **VIP** open **Bot basics** and **Bot personality**.
Someone who may not change a page sees the values, but the fields are switched off and the save buttons do not show.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

## Change the bot basics

Start here when the bot works but does not yet behave the way you want.
For example, changing the command prefix changes what viewers type, while the bot line marker helps them recognize automated messages sent through your own account.
Those settings serve different purposes: a marker does not become part of a command.

1. Open **Bot basics** in the sidebar.
   The **Commands and language** card opens.
2. Change one or more fields (see the table below).
   **Save** and **Discard changes** become active.
3. Choose **Save**.
   The page shows "Basics saved."

A new prefix and a new language work in chat at once, with no restart.
**Discard changes** puts back the values that are saved.

| Field | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Command prefix** | The first characters of a command, such as `!`. One to five characters, no spaces. | `!` |
| **Bot line marker** | Shown before lines the bot posts through your own account. Leave it empty for none. Up to four characters, no spaces. A marker with a space lets **Save** stay on, but the save fails with an error. | Empty (none) |
| **Language** | The default language of the bot's replies. The list names each language in its own language. | Not set |
| **Timezone** | Used for schedules and for times shown in replies. | Not set |
| **Join chat automatically** | The bot joins your chat by itself when it starts. | On |
| **Keep commands and rewards on while offline** | Off: when your stream ends, viewer commands stop and your rewards turn off. They come back when you go live. Mods and you can always use commands. | Off |

The prefix is the one your [built-in commands](/reference/commands/built-in-commands) and commands using the channel prefix start with.
An individual command can use a different trigger, as explained in [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command).
When you switch **Join chat automatically** off, the bot leaves your channel's events at once.
When you switch it on, the bot joins them at once.

### Set the viewer activity levels

Under **Viewer activity levels**, you set how many chat messages a viewer needs before the bot calls them casual, regular or veteran.
Below the first step, a viewer reads as new.

| Field | Meaning | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Casual from** | Messages before a viewer stops reading as new. | 10 messages |
| **Regular from** | Messages before a casual viewer reads as regular. | 100 messages |
| **Veteran from** | Messages before a regular viewer reads as veteran. | 500 messages |

Each step is a whole number from 1 to 1,000,000.
Each step must be higher than the one before it.
A wrong step shows its message under the field, and **Save** stays off until you fix it.

## Pick the reply voice

The reply voice changes the wording of built-in command replies.
It also words the chat lines of [Event responses](/reference/automation/event-responses) that use the default text, such as follows, subs, cheers, raids and bans.
An event response with your own text keeps it.
Choose a tone that fits your chat: **Informative** gives straightforward answers, while **Hype** adds a more energetic style.
This choice does not select a spoken voice or change the text you wrote for a custom command; spoken voices belong to [Text to speech](/reference/audio/text-to-speech).

1. Open **Bot personality** in the sidebar.
   The **Reply voice** card lists the tones.
2. Choose a tone.
   The choice is saved at once. There is no save button.

| Tone | What it sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| **Informative** | Clear, helpful and polite. The default voice. |
| **Friendly** | Warm, wholesome and encouraging. |
| **Sassy** | Witty, teasing and playful, never mean. |
| **Hype** | High energy, capital letters and emotes. |
| **Chill** | Laid back and minimal. |

A new pick applies to the next reply.
Custom commands keep their own text.

## Set greetings and streaks

Greetings let the bot recognize a viewer's first tracked live-stream message or their first message in a later stream without requiring a command.
They start off, so you can choose how much attention the bot draws to people joining the conversation.
Both greetings operate while your stream is live; offline messages do not trigger them.
The greeting interval limits how often those messages appear across viewers.
For example, with a five-second interval, two people arriving a second apart do not both receive a greeting.

1. Open **Engagement triggers** in the sidebar.
   The **Greetings and streaks** card opens.
2. Switch on the moments you want the bot to detect, and set the numbers (see the tables below).
3. Choose **Save changes**.
4. Open [Event responses](/reference/automation/event-responses) and turn on the matching greeting, as the note under the table explains.

| Moment | What the bot does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First time the bot sees a viewer** | Detects the moment the bot first records a viewer chatting during a live stream. | Off |
| **Back after a break** | Detects a previously recorded viewer on their first message in a new stream. There is no minimum number of days away. | Off |
| **Watch streaks** | The watch-streak response does not consult this switch. See below. Any switch on this card makes the bot start recording viewers' live-stream chat, and that record decides who counts as first-time or returning later. | Off |

These switches only detect the moment.
The bot posts nothing until you also turn on the matching entry on [Event responses](/reference/automation/event-responses): **Engagement first time chatter** or **Engagement returning chatter**.
Every channel starts with those entries off, and you choose the reply or pipeline there.
The returning response can use `\{engagement.daysSinceLastSeen\}`, the number of days since the viewer last chatted.

| Field | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Time between greetings** | The bot greets at most one viewer in this time, in seconds. Use 0 for no limit. | 5 seconds |
| **Streak milestones** | Saves your chosen counts; these currently do not filter watch-streak responses. | None |

Each field on the page also shows its own default.
**Reset to defaults** puts the defaults in the form.
Nothing is saved until you choose **Save changes**.

### How watch-streak responses actually work

A watch-streak response begins with a viewer submitting Twitch's own watch-streak notification.
It is not an automatic announcement based on the bot counting attendance.
Configure **Engagement watch streak** on [Event responses](/reference/automation/event-responses) to choose the reply or pipeline for that notification.

The **Watch streaks** switch and **Streak milestones** values on this settings page are saved, but the current notification handler does not use them.
In particular, an empty milestone list does not cause a celebration every stream, and adding milestones does not restrict incoming Twitch notifications.
The page's help text describes that filtering, but it is not implemented in the response path.

You can still edit the stored milestone list:

1. Choose **Add milestone**.
   A new row opens with a count 5 higher than the last one.
2. Change the count if you want. A count is a whole number of 1 or more.
3. To remove a milestone, choose the trash button on its row.
4. Choose **Save changes**.

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**Save changes stays off on Greetings and streaks.**
A number is marked in red, and the page shows "Fix the numbers marked in red to save."
Use a whole number of 0 or more for **Time between greetings**, and 1 or more for each milestone.

::

::Troubleshooting

**Save stays off on Commands and language.**
A field breaks a rule.
The prefix needs one to five characters with no spaces, and the bot line marker takes up to four characters, no spaces.
A marker with a space leaves **Save** on, but the save shows an error.

::
