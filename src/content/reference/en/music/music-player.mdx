The **Music** page lets you manage the soundtrack while viewers contribute [song requests](/reference/music/song-requests).
You can see what is playing, pause or skip it, and keep the next requests in order without leaving the dashboard.
The music can come from [Spotify](/reference/integrations/spotify) or [YouTube](/reference/integrations/youtube), but the two sources reach your stream differently.

YouTube requests play through the **Music Player** browser source in OBS.
Spotify plays on the active playback device in your connected Spotify account.
[Pick the Spotify device](#pick-the-spotify-device) explains how to choose that device.

## Add the Music Player to your streaming software

The **Music Player** holds a hidden YouTube player and can add a Spotify playback device named "NomNomzBot" followed by the widget name.
Select it in Spotify, or with the device button on the Music page, when you want Spotify audio to play through this browser source.
Until a Spotify song plays, the device is not in the list.

1. In the dashboard, open the **OBS** page, choose **Overlays in OBS**, and find **Music Player** under **Required overlays**.
2. Choose **Connect**. A dialog opens with a **Copy address** button. Choose it, then paste the address into a **Browser** source in the OBS app, as shown on [Add an overlay to OBS](/reference/overlays/obs-browser-source).
3. Follow the dialog: size the source 1 by 1 pixel, keep **Control audio via OBS** on, and leave **Shutdown source when not visible** off.
   This gives the source its own audio channel in the OBS mixer.
4. Check the status line at the top of the same dialog. **Loaded in OBS** confirms the player connected. **Not loaded in OBS** means OBS has not loaded the source yet.

The browser source shows nothing while the music plays well.
It shows a short message only when audio needs a click or the Spotify device has a problem (see the table below).

| Message in the browser source | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "Click to enable audio playback" | Click the message in the browser source, so it may play sound. |
| "Reconnect Spotify with streaming permission to enable this device (Integrations page)." | The Music Player device cannot start. Either the Spotify connection lacks streaming permission, or the Spotify account does not have Premium. Choose **Reconnect** on the **Spotify** row of the Integrations page once. If the message stays, play Spotify on a regular Spotify device and capture that device in OBS, as shown on [Spotify](/reference/integrations/spotify#spotify-audio-in-an-obs-browser-source). |
| "Couldn't start playback — check Spotify Premium and try reloading this source." | Check that the Spotify account has Premium, then reload the browser source. |

A YouTube request that waits for this browser source shows "Waiting for the Music Player in OBS" in the **Request queue**.
Its row has a button that opens the same setup steps.
When the **Music Player** loads in OBS and nothing else is playing, the first song in the queue starts.

### Silent stream

**Control audio via OBS** is an OBS setting that routes the browser source's sound through the OBS mixer.
Check that mixer channel when the browser source is playing but your stream is silent.

## Get your first song playing

Connect Spotify if you want to use it; the **Music Player** device needs Spotify Premium and a connection with streaming permission.
Then open Spotify on the device where you want the audio to play.

For YouTube, do these steps once:

1. Add the **Music Player** to OBS, as shown in [Add the Music Player to your streaming software](#add-the-music-player-to-your-streaming-software).
2. On the **Music** page, choose **Request rules** in the page header and open the **Sources** tab.
3. Turn on **Allow YouTube songs** and choose **Save**.

YouTube requests start disabled, and are restricted to the broadcaster by default.
You do not need to connect a YouTube channel account just to request YouTube music.

Open **Music**, check that the **Playback** switch in the **Now playing** header is on, then request a song in chat with `!sr` followed by a single-track link or song name.
The [Song requests](/reference/music/song-requests) guide explains the source permissions and limits.
Check both **Now playing** and the OBS audio mixer to confirm that the sound is reaching your stream.

## Who can use this page

| Role | What they may do |
| --- | --- |
| **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor**, **Broadcaster** | Open the page, play, pause and skip, add and edit player buttons, switch **Playback**, and open **Request rules** to change the rules. |
| **VIP** | Open the page when the **Broadcaster** allows it. |

The **Broadcaster** lets a VIP in on the **Permissions** settings page.
A VIP can only view the page. The playback controls need the **Moderator** role or higher.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).
A YouTube song request in chat works only for the **Broadcaster** until the rules allow more roles.

## Control the song that plays now

The **Now playing** panel shows the cover, the song name, the artist and the viewer who requested it.
When nothing plays, it shows "Nothing is playing right now."

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Play** | Plays the paused song. |
| **Pause** | Pauses the song. |
| **Next song** | Skips to the next song. |

On this panel, **Play**, **Pause** and **Next song** are the controls that act.
For previous song, shuffle, repeat, seeking and volume, pin a player button, as shown in [Keep useful controls close](#keep-useful-controls-close).
**Previous Song**, the shuffle and repeat buttons and **Switch Playback Device** work only with Spotify.
With only YouTube, these buttons do nothing.

A badge under the song name shows a special state.

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Paused by you** | You paused the song. |
| **Blocked: the player cannot start** | The player cannot start the song. For a Spotify song, open Spotify on a device, or pick one as shown in [Pick the Spotify device](#pick-the-spotify-device). If it stays, choose **Reconnect** on the **Spotify** row of the Integrations page, and check that the Spotify account has Premium. For a YouTube song, add or refresh the **Music Player** in OBS. |
| **Starting** | The song is starting. |
| **Music muted** | The music is muted. |

### Pick the Spotify device

The device button sits in the **Request queue** header when Spotify has a device you can move playback to.
It shows the active device by name, or **Choose a device** when no device is active.
Open it and choose another device to move playback there.
When a request waits because Spotify has no active device, the **Request queue** shows "Spotify has no active device. Open Spotify on a device to play requests."
Choosing a device then also starts the music on it.
In every other case, choosing a device only moves playback there.
The button is hidden when there is no device to move to.
Only people who can control playback see it: the **Moderator** role or higher.

The **Music Player** can also register as a Spotify device, which lets that audio have its own OBS source.
It registers only after the bot reports a Spotify song as now playing.
The bot never makes the **Music Player** your Spotify device on its own.
You choose it in Spotify or with the device button.
When Spotify has no active device, the bot first tries the last Spotify device that played during this stream.
That is the **Music Player** only if you chose it before.
If that fails, the **Request queue** shows the "Spotify has no active device" line and you pick a device yourself.

### Turn song requests off

The **Playback** switch sits in the header of the **Now playing** panel.
It is on by default.
When it is off, the bot refuses every new song request.

## Keep useful controls close

Player buttons put the controls you use most often under the current song.
For example, you can pin pause, skip and a fixed volume instead of looking for those controls during a busy moment.
They use the same actions as the Stream Deck music plugin, and you can keep up to 12 in the channel.

1. In the **Now playing** panel header, choose **Add button**.
   The **Add a player button** dialog opens.
2. Pick one of the buttons in the list.
   The dialog shows what it does, and the fields it needs.
3. Fill in the fields the button needs.
   For **Set Volume**, enter the fixed level from 0 (silent) to 100 (full volume).
   For **Seek**, enter the position in seconds from the start of the track.
   For **Set Repeat Mode**, pick Off, Track or Context. Context repeats the current playlist or album.
   Buttons such as **Play** or **Pause** need no fields.
4. Choose an **Icon**.
   An icon is required.
5. Change the **Label** if you want, at most 24 characters.
   It starts as the button's name.
   Clear it for a button with only an icon.
6. Choose **Add button**.
   The dialog closes and the new button shows under the song.

A channel can pin at most 12 buttons. With 12 buttons, **Add button** is off.

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Play** | Resumes playback. |
| **Pause** | Pauses playback. |
| **Play/Pause** | Toggles playback: pauses when a song plays, plays when it is paused. |
| **Now Playing** | Does the same as **Play/Pause**. Only the starting icon differs, and you can change the icon. |
| **Next Song** | Skips to the next track. |
| **Previous Song** | Goes back to the previous track. |
| **Set Volume** | Sets playback volume to a fixed level. |
| **Volume Up** | Raises playback volume by a step. |
| **Volume Down** | Lowers playback volume by a step. |
| **Volume Mute/Unmute** | Mutes if audible, or unmutes to a set level if muted. |
| **Seek** | Jumps to a set point in the current track. |
| **Set Shuffle** | Sets shuffle to a fixed state that you choose: on or off. |
| **Toggle Shuffle** | Flips shuffle from its current state: on becomes off, and off becomes on. |
| **Set Repeat Mode** | Sets repeat to Off, Track or Context. Context repeats the current playlist or album. |
| **Cycle Repeat Mode** | Moves repeat to the next mode: Off, then Track, then Context, then Off again. |
| **Switch Playback Device** | Moves playback to a chosen device. |
| **Save Track** | Adds the current track to your Liked Songs. |
| **Remove Saved Track** | Removes the current track from your Liked Songs. |
| **Favorite Toggle** | Adds or removes the current track from your Liked Songs. |
| **Add to Playlist** | Adds the current track to a chosen playlist. |
| **Remove from Playlist** | Removes the current track from a chosen playlist. |
| **Follow Artist** | Follows the current track's artist. |
| **Unfollow Artist** | Unfollows the current track's artist. |

The list holds all 23 buttons.

### Use, move and edit a button

- Choose a button to run it.
- Drag a button to move it.
  The new order is saved at once.
- Right-click a button to edit it.
  The **Edit player button** dialog opens.
  Choose **Save button** to keep the change, or **Remove button** to delete it.
  The dialog closes and the player buttons show the change.

## Read the song history

The **History** panel lists the 50 latest counted song requests, newest played first.
Each row shows the song, the artist, the viewer who requested it and when.
A row with no known title shows "Unknown song".

The **Thanks for the music** panel lists the 10 viewers with the most counted requests, with their rank beside their name.
A bar and a count show how many of their requests played.
**Open people** opens the **People** page.

A newly accepted request counts when playback starts, rather than when it enters the queue.
Removing it before it plays does not add to history or the requester ranking, and replaying an already counted request does not count it again.
The **Songs per person per stream** limit is separate: a request removed before it plays still counted toward that limit when it was accepted.
Imported historical requests can also appear in these totals.
