title: Sign in
description: Sign in to the dashboard as broadcaster, moderator or viewer, with a button or with a code, and read what a closed sign-in page shows.
---

You sign in on the address of the bot you were given, such as `bot.example.com`.
Any dashboard page sends you to the sign-in page while you are signed out.
On Twitch you pick a role as you sign in, and only the broadcaster role opens a channel for the bot.

The public NomNomzBot website has its sign-in closed: its card says **Sign-in is coming soon** and "You can self-host your own bot today. It is free."
A bot you or your streamer run has its sign-in open.

## Before you start

You need the address of a running NomNomzBot and an account on a platform that bot has switched on for sign-in.
A new bot you run yourself shows the [first-run setup](/reference/self-hosting/first-run-setup) before any sign-in.

To help in a friend's channel as a moderator, you must be a moderator of that channel on Twitch.
You also need the address of the bot that runs it.

The role buttons show only on the bot's own address when it starts with `https://`, or on `localhost` on the computer the bot runs on.
On any other address the page offers only a code; [When the page offers only a code](#when-the-page-offers-only-a-code) covers that case.

## Sign in with Twitch

The **Welcome** card shows the bot at the top and the three role buttons under it.
If this is your own channel, choose **Continue as broadcaster**: it creates or opens your channel, so the bot can work in it.
The other two role buttons sign you in as yourself and create no channel.

| Role button | Choose it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Continue as broadcaster** | You own the channel and run the bot in it. |
| **Continue as moderator** | You moderate a channel from the dashboard. |
| **Continue as viewer** | You watch a channel. |

1. Check the bot address at the top of the **Welcome** card.
   The line under it says which bot that is:
   - **The bot this page belongs to** is the bot at the address you opened.
   - **The official NomNomzBot** is the bot NoMercy Labs hosts at `nomnomz.bot`.
   - **Used on this browser** is another bot you picked earlier in this browser. For that bot the page offers only a code.

   If it is not the address you were given, [choose a different bot](#choose-a-different-bot) first.
2. Choose the role button for your role.
   The browser goes to Twitch, which asks you to approve the sign-in.
3. Approve it on Twitch.
   The browser comes back to the bot, and you are signed in.

As a broadcaster, go on to [Connect platforms](/reference/getting-started/connect-platforms) to check your channel connection.

As a moderator, the dashboard opens the channel you picked last in this browser.
The first time, it opens your own channel if you have one on this bot, or else a channel on this bot that you moderate.
To open your friend's channel, open the channel switcher at the top of the sidebar and pick it under **Channels you moderate**.

If you open the sign-in page while you are already signed in, it sends you straight on to the dashboard.
Under the **Welcome** card, links to the terms of service and the privacy policy of the site you have open let you read them before you sign in.

### What Twitch is asked for

Each role button asks Twitch for only what that role uses.

**Continue as broadcaster** asks to read chat, send chat as you, and list the channels you moderate.
That is what the bot needs to work in your channel.
The bot asks for more later, when a feature needs it.

**Continue as moderator** also asks to read your emotes, and it asks for the moderation tools, such as ban, time out and delete message, right away.
Those tools act on Twitch as you, not as the bot, so they need your own permission.

**Continue as viewer** asks to read chat, send chat as you, and read your emotes.

A later sign-in as moderator or viewer, from a signed-out page, asks only for that role's permissions.
They replace the ones you gave before, so a narrower role can narrow your permissions.

## Sign in with another platform

Twitch sign-in is always on, and the person who runs the bot cannot switch it off.
They can switch on YouTube, Kick and Twitter / X.
Kick and Twitter / X then add a button such as **Continue with Kick**.
A YouTube account signs in with a code: choose **Sign in with a code instead**.
This page does not list a YouTube button as a way in.
A platform shows only when it is switched on and the bot has that platform's app details.

| Platform | Sign in with a button | Sign in with a code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Twitch | Yes | Yes |
| YouTube | Not listed | Yes |
| Kick | Yes | No |
| Twitter / X | Yes | No |

A platform that is off is named in a line at the bottom of the **Welcome** card, such as "Kick is off on this bot."

## Sign in with a code

A code works where the platform cannot send you back to this page.
It has no role choice:

- A Twitch code signs you in as the broadcaster of your own channel, so it creates your channel on this bot if you have none.
  It does not ask for the moderation tools, so to ban, time out or delete messages in a friend's channel, sign in with **Continue as moderator**.
  Chatting there as yourself works after a code sign-in too.
- A YouTube code signs you in as yourself and creates no channel.

1. Under the role buttons, choose **Sign in with a code instead**.
   If only one platform offers a code, the code shows at once.
   If more than one does, the page asks "Which account do you want a code for?"
2. If the page asks, choose the button for your platform, such as **Get a Twitch code**.
   The page shows the code and the platform's site.
3. Choose the **Open** button under the code.
   The platform's site opens in a new tab.
4. Enter the code there and approve the sign-in.
   The page says "Waiting for you to approve. This page goes on by itself." and opens the dashboard when you approve.

To leave without a code, choose **Back**.
On the account question it sits under the platform buttons; with a code on screen it is the last button on the card.
It returns to the buttons you started from.

When the code fails, the page shows one of these messages, then a **Get a new code** button.
The table quotes the Twitch wording; for YouTube the message names YouTube.

| Message | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| "The code ran out before it was used." | You did not enter the code in time. |
| "You did not approve the sign-in on Twitch." | You refused it on the platform. |
| "Twitch did not finish the sign-in." | The platform or the bot ran into an error, even after you approved. Choose **Get a new code** and try again. |
| "The bot could not start a sign-in with Twitch." | The bot could not get a code. |

### When the page offers only a code

On this page, choosing **Continue with Twitch** signs you in as the broadcaster of your own channel on that bot, as a Twitch code always does.
If you came to moderate a friend's channel, ask the person who runs the bot for its address that starts with `https://` instead.
Open that address, and the role buttons show.

The page has no role buttons and no **Sign in with a code instead** here.
Each platform with code sign-in shows one button, such as **Continue with Twitch**, so Kick and Twitter / X do not show.
A line on the **Welcome** card says why:

| The page says | Why |
| --- | --- |
| bot.example.com has its own sign-in page. From here, you sign in with a code. | The bot on the card is not the bot this page belongs to. |
| Platforms send you back only to a secure address. On this address, you sign in with a code. | The address starts with `http://` and is not this computer. |

1. Choose **Continue with Twitch**.
   The code shows at once, with no account question.
2. Choose the **Open** button under the code.
   Twitch opens in a new tab.
3. Enter the code on Twitch and approve the sign-in.
   The dashboard opens.

To reach the friend's channel after this, open the channel switcher at the top of the sidebar and pick it under **Channels you moderate**.
It is listed there as long as you are a moderator of that channel on Twitch.

## Choose a different bot

Most people keep the bot already on the card.
Change it when you belong to more than one bot, or when someone gives you another address.

1. Choose **Change** next to the bot on the **Welcome** card.
   The **Choose a bot** card opens with the bots this browser knows.
2. Pick the bot from the list; a bot in the list needs no **Check**.
   For a bot that is not in the list, type its address in **Bot address**, such as `bot.example.com`, then choose **Check** or press Enter.
   When the check finds it, the bot is added to the list and picked.
3. Choose **Use this bot** at the bottom of the card.
   A successful **Check** still needs this step.
   The **Welcome** card comes back with the new bot on it.

**Back**, beside **Use this bot**, returns to the **Welcome** card and keeps the bot you had.
A bot you added has a cross button beside it that removes it from this browser's list.

| After **Check** the page says | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 192.168.1.20:5080 is a NomNomzBot. It is added and picked. | Choose **Use this bot**. |
| Something answers at 192.168.1.20:5080, but it does not say it is a NomNomzBot. It is added and picked. | Make sure the address is the one you were given before you choose **Use this bot**. |
| That is not a web address. Type a host name or IP, with the port if it has one. | Fix the address and choose **Check** again. |
| Nothing answers at 192.168.1.20:5080. Check that the bot runs, and that the address and port are right. | Nothing is added. Fix the address or start the bot, then choose **Check** again. |
| This page is secure (https) and 192.168.1.20:5080 is not (http). Browsers block calls between them, so this page cannot use it. Open 192.168.1.20:5080 in the browser instead. | Nothing is added. Sign in on that bot's own page. |

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**The page says "This bot does not answer. Check that it runs, then try again."**
The bot did not answer the page.
If you run the bot yourself, start it, then choose **Try again**.
If someone else runs it, choose **Try again** after a moment, and ask them to check the bot when the message stays.

::

::Troubleshooting

**The page says "This bot has no sign-in switched on yet. Its owner sets one up in the bot settings."**
Something answered at the bot address, but it sent back no sign-in buttons.
A NomNomzBot always offers Twitch, so the address on the card is not a NomNomzBot, or the bot ran into an error.
Choose **Change** and check the address.
When the address is right, ask the person who runs the bot to check it.

::

::Troubleshooting

**The page says "Too many sign-in tries."**
The page counts the seconds down.
**Try again** turns on when the count reaches 0.

::
