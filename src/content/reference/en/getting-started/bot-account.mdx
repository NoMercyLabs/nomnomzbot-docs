The bot types its lines in your chat from one Twitch account.
This page explains which account that is.
A bot account is never required.

## Which account the bot types from

The bot picks the account in this order.

| Order | The bot types from | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Your channel's own bot account | You connected one on the [White-label bot](/reference/settings/white-label-bot) settings page. |
| 2 | The shared bot account | No own bot account is connected, and the install has a shared bot connected. |
| 3 | Your own Twitch account | Neither of the two above exists. |

A self-hosted bot uses the streamer's own account until a separate bot account is registered.
So after you [connect Twitch](/reference/getting-started/connect-platforms), the bot can already type in your chat.

The **White-label bot** page shows which account the channel uses now.
It shows **Connected** for your own bot account, or **Shared bot** for the shared one.

## The bot speaks in one voice

Every line the bot writes uses the channel's one bot voice.
A command reply, an announcement and an event response all come from the same account.
The bot speaks aloud only through text to speech.

## Mark lines the bot posts as you

When the bot types from your own account, viewers cannot tell its lines from yours.
A marker in front of each bot line fixes that.

1. Open **Settings** in the sidebar and choose **Bot basics**.
2. Type one to four characters in **Bot line marker**, such as `*` or an emoji.
   Spaces are not allowed.
3. Choose **Save**.
   The page shows "Basics saved."

The marker goes in front of each line the bot posts through your own account.
Leave the field empty for no marker.
The bot skips the marker when it types from a separate bot account, because that account's name already tells viewers apart.
A line you send as yourself never gets the marker.

## Keep the bot a moderator

This applies only when the bot types from a separate bot account.
Your own account needs no moderator role.

When the bot account loses the moderator role, the dashboard shows a notice: "{botName} is not a moderator in your channel".
The notice says: "Type /mod {botName} in your Twitch chat. Twitch limits what a bot without the moderator role can do."
The notice clears when the bot is a moderator again.

<details>
<summary>The shared bot can get the role back by itself</summary>

For the shared bot account, NomNomzBot tries to give the moderator role back with your own Twitch sign-in.
This needs the permission to manage moderators on your Twitch account.
If that permission is missing, the dashboard asks you for it in its notices.

</details>
