title: Bot account
description: Which Twitch account the bot types from in your chat, how to see which one it uses, and how to mark its lines when it types as you.
---

You do not need to make a bot account first.
After you [connect Twitch](/reference/getting-started/connect-platforms), the bot can already reply in your chat.
Until a shared bot or your own bot account is connected, it types those replies from your own Twitch account, so you can test a command straight away.

## Which account the bot types from

Three accounts can type the bot's lines in your chat, and the bot uses the first one in this list that is connected.

| Order | The bot types from | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Your own bot account | Your own bot account is connected on **White-label bot** and is active. |
| 2 | The shared bot | Your own bot account is not connected, and the shared bot is connected on your NomNomzBot installation. |
| 3 | Your own Twitch account | Neither your own bot account nor the shared bot is connected. |

Your own bot account is a Twitch account you made for the bot and connected for your channel only.
You connect it on the [White-label bot](/reference/settings/white-label-bot) settings page, in the card **Your channel's own bot**.
When it is connected, the bot types from it, leaves the marker off its lines, and the moderator notice checks this account.

The shared bot is one bot account for every channel on a NomNomzBot installation.
If your channel runs on someone else's installation, you do nothing for it.
Only the person who runs the installation adds the shared bot, during [first-run setup](/reference/self-hosting/first-run-setup) or later on the **Platform bot** page of the admin console.
When you run NomNomzBot yourself, that person is you.

## See which account the bot uses now

In the sidebar, choose **Settings**, the last item.
The sidebar then lists the settings pages in groups.
Choose **White-label bot** in the **Bot** group.
In the card **Your channel's own bot**, the first row shows which account is set up, and the name on that row tells you which one.
If you may change the bot, more rows with buttons follow it, to connect, replace or disconnect your own bot account.

| The first row's name | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| The bot's name, with a **Connected** badge and an `@` handle under it | Your own bot account is connected, and the bot types from it. |
| "Your channel uses the shared bot", followed by the bot's login name | The shared bot types the bot's lines. |
| **Shared bot**, and nothing after it | Your own Twitch account types the bot's lines, because neither your own bot account nor the shared bot is connected. |

## Mark the lines the bot posts as you

The **Bot line marker** is a short mark the bot puts in front of each line it posts through your own Twitch account, so viewers can tell those lines from what you type.
It shows only when no bot account is connected for your channel: neither your own bot account nor the shared bot.
When either one is connected, the bot leaves the marker off.
You can still save a marker then, but it does not show in chat.

Only the broadcaster can change the marker.

1. In the sidebar, choose **Settings**, then choose **Bot basics** in the **Bot** group.
2. In **Bot line marker**, type one to four characters with no spaces, such as `*` or an emoji.
   Most emoji count as two of the four characters, and an emoji with a skin tone counts as all four.
3. Choose **Save**.
   The page shows "Basics saved."
   If an error shows instead, the marker is not saved, so check it against step 2 and choose **Save** again.
4. In your Twitch chat, type `!commands` and press Enter.
   The bot answers with the list of your commands, with your marker in front of its line.
   As the broadcaster, you can repeat `!commands` at any time, because its wait between uses does not apply to you.
   If the line has no marker, check the card on **White-label bot**: when it shows your own bot account or the shared bot, the marker stays off.

To remove the marker, clear the field and choose **Save**.

A command or event response can also post a line as you on purpose.
Its steps are built in the editor described on [Pipelines](/reference/commands/pipelines#make-a-pipeline), where a **Send message** step has a **Sender** field.
Set to **Bot**, the default, the line comes from the account the bot types from now.
Set to **Broadcaster**, the line always comes from your own Twitch account, even when a bot account is connected.
That suits a line only you can post, such as one with a subscriber-only emote that the bot account cannot use.
Those lines never get the marker, because they are meant to read as your own words.

## Keep the bot a moderator

The moderator notice checks one bot account: your own bot account when one is connected, else the shared bot.
Your own Twitch account is the broadcaster, which already outranks a moderator.

When that bot account is not a moderator, a notice titled "BotName is not a moderator in your channel" appears.
The notice shows the bot account's Twitch login name in place of BotName.
It shows in the **Needs attention** panel on the dashboard home and in the **Action required** list.
To open that list, choose the inbox icon button at the top of the dashboard, next to the Shield mode button.
The notice says: "Type /mod BotName in your Twitch chat. Twitch limits what a bot without the moderator role can do."

1. In your Twitch chat, as the broadcaster, type `/mod` followed by a space and the login name the notice shows.
2. Press Enter.
   The notice clears once Twitch reports that the bot is a moderator again.
