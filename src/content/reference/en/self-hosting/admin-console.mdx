title: Admin console
description: Watch the health of your own installation, look after its channels and people, and set its platform-wide defaults from the admin console.

When you run NomNomzBot yourself, the admin console is where you look after the whole installation instead of one channel.
You check that Twitch is reaching every channel, see which services are healthy, suspend a channel, set what new channels start with, and rotate the encryption key.

::Callout

Running it for yourself, your friends and your community is free. You may not earn money with it: no selling it, and no paid hosting or paid service built on it.

::

## Who can open it

Only an administrator of the installation sees the console.
On a self-hosted install, the first account that signs in and finishes onboarding becomes the administrator automatically, with no setting.
To pick the account yourself, put its Twitch ID in `INITIAL_ADMIN_TWITCH_ID` in `.env`; the account with exactly that ID is promoted when it signs in.
An account that is already an administrator is never promoted again.

Each page also asks for its own platform permission, and the dashboard checks it before the page opens.
Without it, you land on the dashboard home page instead.
The sidebar and the page check use the same list, so a page you cannot open is a page you do not hold the permission for.
As the person who runs the installation, you hold all of them.
You only meet this limit when you give someone else a role on the [Access](#access) page.

## Open the console

Open the account menu at the bottom of the dashboard sidebar and choose **Platform admin**.
The item hides while you view as another role; see the [dashboard tour](/reference/getting-started/dashboard-tour).
The console has its own sidebar, with the way back to your channel at the top, then five groups: **Activity**, **People**, **Billing**, **Safety** and **Configuration**.

Start on **Overview** for a quick look, and on **System** when something seems wrong.
Leave the **Safety** and **Configuration** pages alone until you have a reason to change them, because several of them reach every channel at once.

## Activity

These pages show what the installation is doing.
Most only read; two let you act (**Webhook deliveries** and **Scheduled jobs**), and **Event replay** rebuilds data.

| Page | What it shows | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overview** | **Channels**, **Active channels**, **Users**, **System**, **Bot uptime** and **Events today**. It refreshes every 30 seconds. | Read only. |
| **EventSub health** | For each channel, whether its Twitch subscriptions work, shown as "Working" or "N of M need attention". A broken channel lists its topic, state, last confirmed time and last error. | Read only. A channel whose sign-in has expired says "Re-authorize the channel." |
| **Webhook deliveries** | The delivery log of outgoing [webhooks](/reference/integrations/webhooks-and-event-feeds): endpoint, event, status, code, duration, attempt, time sent and error. | **Replay** sends a delivery again as a new attempt. |
| **Scheduled jobs** | The job queue: channel, pipeline, state, when it is due, when it ran, and what started it. | **Retry** queues a job again as a new run. |
| **Channel usage** | The latest metering period of each channel, with its usage, **TTS characters** and **Cost**. A usage without a price shows "No price yet". | Read only. |
| **Error budget** | Webhook delivery per channel over the last 24 hours: attempts, errors, error rate and budget left. A channel shows "Budget spent" when it has used it all. | Read only. |
| **Event replay** | The history of earlier replays. | Rebuild a channel's data from its events (below). |
| **Audit log** | Every operator permission check, with its operator, permission, channel, reason and outcome (**Allowed** or **Denied**). New entries appear by themselves. | Filter by permission, outcome, operator, channel and day. |

### Replay events

A replay rebuilds one kind of stored data for one channel from its recorded events.
Use it when that data looks wrong and you know why.
Choose the **Channel**, the **Projection**, an optional **Event type** and a **From** and **To** time, then choose **Count events** to see how many events match.
Choose **Replay** and confirm.
The confirmation says it replays the count into that projection "for" the channel, and that "The projection rows in this scope are rebuilt."
When it ends, the page says how many events went into the projection, and the run appears in **Replay history**.

## People

### Tenants and the channel console

**Tenants** lists everyone who joined: streamers with their channel, and viewers who signed in.
Search by name, and filter by **Kind** (**Streamer** or **Viewer**), **Status** (**Active**, **Suspended** or **Banned**) and **Live**.
Each row shows the person, kind, platforms, channel status, live state, tier, last activity and creation date.

Open a streamer to reach their **Channel console**.
It has these tabs:

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Summary** | The runs of the last 24 hours, how many failed, and the last failure. |
| **Runs** | Every command, pipeline, script and timer run, newest first. Filter to failures or one pipeline, and open a run to read its steps. A failed run names its kind, such as **Timeout** or **Permission**. |
| **TTS** | The last 50 clips the channel spoke, and how fast the voice service answered. |
| **Billing** | The channel's plan, invoices and grants. See [Billing](#billing). |
| **People** | The channel's members and their relation to it. |
| **Limits** | Limit overrides. Add one with a key, a value, a reason and an optional expiry. Clearing it puts the channel back on its tier default. |
| **Audit** | What operators and the system did to this channel. |
| **Data** | The lifecycle actions below. |

The lifecycle actions each ask for a justification, and the bot keeps it in the audit log.

| Action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Suspend** | The bot stops serving the channel until you reinstate it. You choose the new status, **Suspended** or **Banned**. |
| **Reinstate** | The bot serves the channel again. |
| **Begin access** | Opens a time-boxed support session on the channel, with an expiry. **Break glass (emergency access)** is the emergency form. Every step is audited. |
| **Re-run migrations** | Applies the database migrations the channel is still missing. |
| **Export data** | Exports the channel's data. |
| **Erase channel** | Removes the channel and its data. It can be restored for a number of days, then it is permanent. |

The **Tenants** page also holds the **Support contact**.
It is the page the people of a suspended channel are sent to.
Leave it empty for none.

**Act as** is the one action that changes what you see.
It opens a time-boxed session in which you see and do everything as one person of a channel.
You give a reason and pick 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours or 4 hours.
It lasts until you press **Exit** or the time runs out, and the bot logs the reason and every change.

### Support

**Support** is the help desk view of one person.
Give a reason first, because the bot keeps every lookup in the audit log, then search for the person.
You read their username, platform, Twitch ID, last seen time, community, moderation and trust standing, their history, and the commands and pipelines their channel built.
It shows information only.

## Billing

**Billing** manages the plans of the installation, and the **Billing** tab of a channel does the same for one channel.
You can use it to give a friend's channel a bigger allowance.
It is not a way to charge anyone.

| Part | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **Tiers** | Add or edit a tier: a key, a name, a price in cents, its limits (one `key=value` per line) and whether it shows on the public pricing page. The confirmation says how many channels are on the tier and get the change. |
| **Invite codes** | Create an invite with a maximum number of redemptions, an optional tier for a number of months or forever, an optional founders badge and an optional expiry. Send the link to the streamer. **Revoke** stops the link at once; channels that already redeemed it keep what it gave them. See [Redeem an invite](/reference/getting-started/redeem-an-invite). |
| **Priced units** | Set a price per batch for a unit, with a currency and a batch size. This feeds the **Cost** shown on **Channel usage**. |
| **Channel billing** | Enter a channel id to see its invoices and grants. You can refund an invoice, gift a plan until a date or forever, **End now** on a gift (the channel goes back to its own tier at once), and grant the founders badge. |

A channel reads the result on its own [Billing](/reference/settings/billing) page, and the limits behind a tier on [Resource limits](/reference/settings/resource-limits).

## Safety

| Page | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Spam defaults** | The values every new channel starts with for [spam defense](/reference/moderation/spam-defense). A channel that never changed a setting keeps following them, so one edit here moves all of them. It has the same groups as the channel page, and a note on each setting about its cost. Watch-only mode is the safe start. Settings marked **Not yet active** are saved but do nothing yet. |
| **Trust and safety** | A desk for accounts that need a human. State a **Reason for access** first, and nothing loads until you do. Then work the **Review queue** (**Confirm** keeps an automatic action, **Overturn** reverses it on the real account), read **Cross-channel signals** (accounts flagged in two or more channels), and manage **Network blocks**. A network block bans an account in every channel it touches. **Preview block** first shows how many channels it reaches, and **Lift** unbans the account in those channels. |
| **Data requests** | The erasure, export and opt-out requests people made about their data. Filter by status and type. A subject shows as a hash, never by name. **Retry** on a failed erasure first counts what it erases and says "It cannot be undone." |

Read [Your data and privacy](/reference/settings/your-data-and-privacy) for the channel side of these requests.

## Configuration

### System

**System** answers "is my installation healthy?".
It lists each service with a plain meaning: **Bot API**, **Database**, **Shared cache** or **Cache**, **Database upgrades**, **Twitch events**, **Twitch events after an update**, **Restart in progress** and **Twitch bot account**.
Each one is **Healthy**, **Degraded** or **Unhealthy**.
The page also shows the running version, uptime, memory, processor, events today, live channels and known users.
The **Updates** card checks GitHub every few hours for a newer stable release; see [Updates](/reference/self-hosting/updates).

**Rotate encryption key** re-wraps every stored data key from the previous encryption key to a new one.
Set the new key on the server first.
If the server runs a different key than the one you enter, bot tokens become invalid and the bot must re-authorize.
A second run changes nothing, and the result says how many keys were re-wrapped, were already current or failed.
Keep `ENCRYPTION_KEY` safe either way, as [Updates](/reference/self-hosting/updates) explains.

### Feature flags

A flag switches a feature on or off for every channel.
Give it a key, a description, **On for every channel**, a **Rollout share (percent)** from 0 to 100, a **Lowest tier that gets it**, a **Consent the channel must give first** and an optional **Only in this deployment mode**.
Choose **Review the change** and the page counts the active channels that follow the flag.
Channels with an override keep their own state.
A channel override forces the flag on or off for one channel, with a reason and an optional end time, and beats the flag for that channel.

### Providers

**Providers** holds the OAuth apps of the services you connect, such as Twitch, Spotify and Discord.
Each shows where its client ID and secret come from: **Stored** here, **Environment**, or **Not set**.
Secrets are write only.
You type a new client secret, API key or bot token, the bot stores it sealed, and it never shows it again.
An empty box leaves that value as it is.
**Clear stored** removes every stored value for the provider, and the bot then uses the environment.

### Content

**Content** manages the definitions channels can install from the platform.
Filter by **Kind**, open a definition to see its installs ("N installed copies, N behind the current version, N edited by the channel") and draft a new version with a JSON payload.
**Retire** stops a definition being offered.
Installed copies keep running, and nothing in any channel changes.

### Access

**Access** lists the principals with platform access, employees and service accounts, and the roles that grant it.
You can **Assign role** (with an optional expiry), **Revoke** a role or **Deactivate** a principal, each with a reason.
A revoke takes effect at once.
A deactivated principal holds no active roles and cannot act until you **Reactivate** it.
Changing your own account shows "You lose this access in your own session."
That is the page to use if you let a friend help run the installation with fewer permissions than you.

### Platform bot

The shared bot account speaks for every channel that has no bot of its own.
The page shows its state: **Never connected**, **Connected and working** or **Connected, token unusable**.
To replace it, write a **Reason** and choose **Preview channels** to see how many channels speak through it.
Then choose **Reconnect for N channels**, enter the code shown on Twitch as the new bot account, and wait for approval.
If the channel count changed in between, preview again.
A channel with its own [white-label bot](/reference/settings/white-label-bot) is not affected.

### Platform defaults

These are the values a channel follows until it sets its own: the role each action asks for, the wording of built-in command replies, the response to each event, and the text-to-speech voice.
A change reaches every channel that has no setting of its own.
Before you apply one, the page counts the channels it changes and how many keep their own setting.
Lowering a protected default needs a tick on "I understand this lowers a protected default for every channel above."

Each default shows how many channels use their own value, and a changed one is marked **Changed**.
Roles run from **Everyone**, **Twitch subscribers**, **VIPs**, **Artists**, **Moderators**, **Lead moderators** and **Editors** up to **The broadcaster**.
See [built-in commands](/reference/commands/built-in-commands), [event responses](/reference/automation/event-responses) and [text-to-speech](/reference/audio/text-to-speech) for the channel side.
