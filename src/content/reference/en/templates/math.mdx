title: Math helpers
description: Twenty template helpers that calculate with numbers or draw a number as a progress bar, hearts or stars, with the input each one takes and what it gives.
---

Math helpers work out a number while the bot builds a reply. Meter helpers turn a number into a bar, hearts or stars. Put them in any reply field that takes template braces, the same way as the other [template helpers](/reference/templates/overview). The helper name is followed by a colon and its input, and pipes separate the inputs. [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates) covers this syntax.

A helper can take another helper as its input, so a command reply can calculate with what a viewer typed. If a helper has no answer, chat shows the helper text with its braces. For example, `{math.div:7|0}` shows up in chat as `{math.div:7|0}`, because 7 cannot be divided by 0.

## Where they work

Every helper on this page works in commands, event responses, timers, pipelines, Discord posts and webhook bodies. They also work in a viewer's own text, such as a reward input. For that, **Viewer Templates** must be on in [Features](/reference/settings/features), and the viewer must hold the template permission. Subscribers and higher hold that permission by default. In a viewer's text, a bar is never wider than 20 marks, even when a wider one is asked for.

## Numbers you type

Write a number with a dot for decimals and no separators between thousands, such as `2.5` or `-4`. A space before or after a number is fine. `2,5`, `1,000` and `1e3` are not numbers here, so a helper given one stays in braces.

A result has at most 10 decimals and no zeros at the end. It always uses a dot, whatever language the channel uses. `{math:10/3}` gives `3.3333333333`, and `{math:2.50*2}` gives `5`.

## Calculate a whole expression

`{math:expression}` works out a calculation written the way you would type it into a calculator. `{math:2+3*4}` gives `14`, and `{math:(2+3)*4}` gives `20`.

| You type | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `+` and `-` | Add and subtract | `{math:10-4+1}` gives `7` |
| `*` and `/` | Multiply and divide | `{math:6*7/2}` gives `21` |
| `%` | The remainder after dividing | `{math:7%3}` gives `1` |
| `^` | To the power of | `{math:2^10}` gives `1024` |
| `( )` | Work this part out first | `{math:(1+2)^2}` gives `9` |

Powers go first. Then come multiplying, dividing and remainders. Adding and subtracting go last. A minus sign in front of a number is applied after the power, so `{math:-2^2}` gives `-4`. Write `{math:(-2)^2}` to get `4`. Two powers in a row work from the right, so `{math:2^3^2}` gives `512`.

An expression can also use six functions. Put the input in brackets, and separate two inputs with a comma.

| Function | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sqrt(n)` | The square root | `{math:sqrt(81)}` gives `9` |
| `abs(n)` | The number without its minus sign | `{math:abs(3-10)}` gives `7` |
| `floor(n)` | The number rounded down to a whole number | `{math:floor(7/2)}` gives `3` |
| `ceil(n)` | The number rounded up to a whole number | `{math:ceil(7/2)}` gives `4` |
| `pow(n,p)` | `n` to the power of `p` | `{math:pow(2,3)+1}` gives `9` |
| `log(n)` or `log(n,b)` | The logarithm of `n` in base `b`, or the natural logarithm without a base | `{math:log(100,10)}` gives `2` |

The whole expression is one input, so it cannot hold a pipe. An expression longer than 200 characters stays in braces. An expression that does not finish, such as `{math:2+}`, also stays in braces. So does an expression with an unknown function. A value inside the expression counts toward the 200 characters after the bot fills it in.

A command such as `!double 21` can calculate with the word after the command: `{math:{args.1}*2}` gives `42`. When the viewer types `!double lots`, the reply shows `{math:lots*2}`. Word the reply so it still reads well if someone types a word instead of a number.

## Calculate with one helper per step

The math helpers each do one operation, and you separate the numbers with pipes.

| Helper | Takes | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `{math.add}` | Two numbers | The first plus the second | `{math.add:2\|3}` gives `5` |
| `{math.sub}` | Two numbers | The first minus the second | `{math.sub:5\|3}` gives `2` |
| `{math.mul}` | Two numbers | The first times the second | `{math.mul:4\|3}` gives `12` |
| `{math.div}` | Two numbers | The first divided by the second | `{math.div:7\|2}` gives `3.5` |
| `{math.div.int}` | Two numbers | The division with the fraction dropped | `{math.div.int:7\|2}` gives `3` |
| `{math.mod}` | Two numbers | The remainder after dividing the first by the second | `{math.mod:7\|2}` gives `1` |
| `{math.pow}` | Two numbers | The first to the power of the second | `{math.pow:3\|3}` gives `27` |
| `{math.sqrt}` | One number | The square root | `{math.sqrt:81}` gives `9` |
| `{math.abs}` | One number | The number without its minus sign | `{math.abs:-4}` gives `4` |
| `{math.floor}` | One number | The number rounded down to a whole number | `{math.floor:2.7}` gives `2` |
| `{math.ceil}` | One number | The number rounded up to a whole number | `{math.ceil:2.1}` gives `3` |
| `{math.log}` | A number, and a base if you want one | The logarithm, or the natural logarithm without a base | `{math.log:100\|10}` gives `2` |
| `{math.sum}` | One or more numbers | All the numbers added up | `{math.sum:1\|2\|3}` gives `6` |
| `{math.avg}` | One or more numbers | The average | `{math.avg:1\|2\|6}` gives `3` |
| `{math.median}` | One or more numbers | The middle number in size order | `{math.median:1\|9\|3}` gives `3` |

A helper given more or fewer numbers than it takes stays in braces, so `{math.add:1|2|3}` does not add up; use `{math.sum}` for that. With an even count of numbers, `{math.median}` gives the average of the middle two, so `{math.median:1|2|3|10}` gives `2.5`.

With a minus sign, `{math.div.int}` drops the fraction toward 0, so `{math.div.int:-7|2}` gives `-3`. The remainder keeps the sign of the first number, so `{math.mod:-7|2}` gives `-1`.

Some numbers have no answer. For these, the helper stays in braces:

- Dividing by 0, or the remainder after dividing by 0.
- The square root of a negative number.
- 0 to a negative power.
- The logarithm of 0 or of a negative number.

A logarithm base of 1, or of 0 or less, does not work either. A result too large for the bot to hold also stays in braces. The same numbers fail the same way inside `{math:expression}`.

## Draw a number as a meter

The meter helpers turn a number into a row of marks. Three of them read a percentage, and `{meter.stars}` reads a value out of a maximum.

| Helper | Takes | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `{meter}` | A percentage, then a width, then a full mark and an empty mark if you want them | A bar, a space and the percentage | `{meter:60}` gives `██████░░░░ 60%` |
| `{meter.plain}` | A percentage, then a width if you want one | A bar without the number | `{meter.plain:60}` gives `██████░░░░` |
| `{meter.hearts}` | A percentage | Five hearts, red for full and black for empty | `{meter.hearts:60}` gives `❤️❤️❤️🖤🖤` |
| `{meter.stars}` | A value and a maximum | Stars, as many as the maximum, filled up to the value | `{meter.stars:3\|5}` gives `★★★☆☆` |

A percentage below 0 counts as 0, and one above 100 counts as 100. The number printed beside the bar is limited the same way, so `{meter:150}` gives `██████████ 100%`. The count of full marks is rounded to the nearest mark, and a half rounds up, so `{meter:55}` fills 6 of 10 marks.

The width is how many marks the bar has: a whole number from 1 to 50, and 10 when you leave it out. `{meter:60|5}` gives `███░░ 60%`. A width of 0, above 50, or with a sign or decimals stays in braces, so `{meter:60|80}` shows up in chat as `{meter:60|80}`. In a viewer's text, a width above 20 becomes 20 before this check, so there a width of 80 draws 20 marks. The full mark and the empty mark can be any text, such as an emoji, and `{meter}` needs both or neither. `{meter:40|5|🟩|⬜}` gives `🟩🟩⬜⬜⬜ 40%`, but `{meter:40|5|🟩}` stays in braces.

The maximum for `{meter.stars}` is a whole number from 1 to 20. A maximum below 1, above 20, or with decimals stays in braces, so `{meter.stars:3|25}` shows up in chat as `{meter.stars:3|25}`. A value above the maximum fills every star, and a value below 0 fills none.

A command such as `!progress 45 60` can draw its own bar from the two words after the command:

```
Progress: {meter:{math:{args.1}/{args.2}*100}}
```

This gives `Progress: ████████░░ 75%`. For `!progress 45 0` there is no answer to divide by, so chat shows `Progress: {meter:{math:45/0*100}}`.
