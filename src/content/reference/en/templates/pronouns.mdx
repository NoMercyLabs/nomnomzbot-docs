One reply goes to many viewers.
This page shows how to write a line that fits every person's pronouns.
The helpers themselves are listed on [User helpers](/reference/templates/user) and [Target helpers](/reference/templates/target).

## Where pronouns come from

The bot reads pronouns from the alejo.io pronoun API, the same source the Twitch pronoun chat plugins use.
It loads the list of pronouns from `https://api.pronouns.alejo.io/v1/pronouns`.
It looks up one viewer at `https://api.pronouns.alejo.io/v1/users/` followed by the viewer's Twitch login name.
The lookup uses Twitch logins only, so it finds Twitch viewers.

The bot saves a person's pronouns when that person chats in any channel the bot is in.
The saved pronouns are the same in every channel.
A reply reads the saved pronouns. It does not look them up again.

The API lists the base pronouns, such as he, she and they.
Pairs such as he/they come from the bot's own bundled set.
If the API is down, the bot uses that bundled set.
Pronouns set by a command that runs the Set pronoun action stay set. The API does not overwrite them.

The bot adds the words that go with each pronoun.
The words below are the ones the three common pronouns give.

| Helper | he | she | they | any or other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | he | she | they | the name |
| Object | him | her | them | the name |
| Possessive | his | her | their | the name with `'s` |
| Present tense | is | is | are | is |
| Past tense | was | was | were | was |
| Gendered term | guy | gal | person | person |

Other pronouns, such as xe and zie, bring their own possessive words, and their gendered term is "person".
Only "they" takes the plural verb.
The last column is explained under [When the pronouns are "any" or "other"](#when-the-pronouns-are-any-or-other).

## Write correct sentences

If you type "he" or "his" in a reply, the line is wrong for every viewer who uses other pronouns.
Use the pronoun helpers instead.
The bot fills in the right word for the person in the line.

### Pick the helper

- `{subject}` gives he, she or they. Use it where a sentence needs "he", "she" or "they".
- `{object}` gives him, her or them. Use it after a verb or a word like "to" and "for".
- `{possessive}` gives his, her or their. Use it before a noun, as in "their cat".
- `{genderedTerm}` gives guy, gal or person.
- `{presentTense}` gives is or are. It does not change with the stream.
- `{tense}` gives is or are while your channel's stream is live, and was or were when it is offline. In a shoutout it follows the target's stream.
- `{verb:plays|play}` gives the first form for he and she, and the second form for they.

Use `{tense}` when you need was or were.

### Pick the person

A bare helper such as `{subject}` follows the person the command names.
If the command names nobody, it follows the person who used the command.
In `!hug @someone`, `{subject}` is about the person named, and in `!lurk` it is about the person who typed it.

Add `user.` or `target.` to choose a side yourself.
`{user.subject}` is always about the person who used the command.
`{target.subject}` is always about the person the command names.
The same works for the other helpers, such as `{user.possessive}`, `{target.object}`, `{target.verb:plays|play}` and `{target.tense}`.

### Start a sentence with a capital

Write the first letter of the helper name in upper case to get a capital first letter.
`{Subject}` gives He, She or They.
`{Target.Subject}`, `{User.Possessive}` and `{Tense}` work the same way.
Use it at the start of a sentence.
A verb form from `{verb:plays|play}` does not get a capital.

### Make the verb agree

A verb must match the pronoun.
"He plays" and "they play" use different forms.
Do not type the verb after `{subject}`.
Use `{verb:singular|plural}` and type the two forms with a pipe between them.
The first form is for he and she, and the second form is for they.
Without a pipe, the bot leaves the text as you typed it.

For the verb "to be", use `{presentTense}` or `{tense}`.
They give is or are.

### When a person has no pronouns

If the bot has no pronouns for a person, it uses they, them, their, person, are and were.
This holds for a person the bot has never seen chat, and who never picked pronouns in the bot.
The pronoun helpers never show as raw text.
The verb takes the plural form, so the line reads "they play".

### When the pronouns are "any" or "other"

"Any" and "other" name a choice, not words for a sentence.
A guess such as "they" can be wrong for the person.
So the bot uses the person's name where a pronoun would go.

- `{subject}` and `{object}` give the name.
- `{possessive}` gives the name with 's, such as "Alex's".
- `{presentTense}` and `{pastTense}` give is and was.
- `{verb:plays|play}` gives the first form.
- `{genderedTerm}` gives person.

The name keeps its own capital letters, also in `{Subject}`.
"iFiLLz" stays "iFiLLz".
If you write a line for these people with `{name}` once and `{subject}` after it, the name shows twice.
Use shorter lines to keep that readable.

## Examples

Each line below is the same reply for four kinds of viewer: he, she, they, and any or other.
The first line of each example is what you type.
For "any", the name stands where a pronoun would, so a line that already names the person shows the name more than once.

**A shoutout.**
Typed: `Shoutout to {target.displayname}! {Target.Subject} {target.presentTense} a great streamer. Go follow {target.object}!`

- he: `Shoutout to Alex! He is a great streamer. Go follow him!`
- she: `Shoutout to Alex! She is a great streamer. Go follow her!`
- they: `Shoutout to Alex! They are a great streamer. Go follow them!`
- any: `Shoutout to Alex! Alex is a great streamer. Go follow Alex!`

Without helpers, the same line written for "he" reads wrong for she and they.

**A verb that must agree.**
Typed: `{target.displayname} is live. Say hi to {target.object} and see what {target.subject} {target.verb:plays|play}.`

- he: `Alex is live. Say hi to him and see what he plays.`
- she: `Alex is live. Say hi to her and see what she plays.`
- they: `Alex is live. Say hi to them and see what they play.`
- any: `Alex is live. Say hi to Alex and see what Alex plays.`

**A possessive and a term.**
Typed: `{user} loves {user.possessive} cat. {user} is a good {user.genderedTerm}.`

- he: `Alex loves his cat. Alex is a good guy.`
- she: `Alex loves her cat. Alex is a good gal.`
- they: `Alex loves their cat. Alex is a good person.`
- any: `Alex loves Alex's cat. Alex is a good person.`

**A line that follows the stream.**
Typed: `{Target.Subject} {target.tense} streaming Tetris.`

- he, live: `He is streaming Tetris.`
- she, offline: `She was streaming Tetris.`
- they, live: `They are streaming Tetris.`
- they, offline: `They were streaming Tetris.`
- any, live: `Alex is streaming Tetris.`
- any, offline: `Alex was streaming Tetris.`

In a shoutout, the live state is the live state of the person you shout out. Everywhere else it is the live state of your channel.
