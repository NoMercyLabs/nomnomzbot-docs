---
title: Platform helpers
description: The 25 platform template helpers that give the link, live state, viewers and chat totals of each platform the channel streams to, with the platform names they take and what each one gives when a platform is offline.
---

Platform helpers read each platform the streamer [connected](/reference/getting-started/connect-platforms) separately, so one reply can name Twitch, Kick and YouTube side by side. You type them in a reply field that takes template braces, like the other [template helpers](/reference/templates/overview).

A platform that is not connected, or not live, gives an empty text. It never gives a 0 as if nobody were watching. Plan the words around the helper for that empty case, for example with `{default}`.

## Where they work

The helpers about the channel's platforms work in commands, event responses, timers, pipelines, Discord posts and webhook bodies.

The four helpers about the person's or the event's platform need a trigger that supplies one, so they work in fewer places. They are listed in [The platform of the person or the event](#the-platform-of-the-person-or-the-event).

No platform helper works in a viewer's own text, such as a reward input. There it stays as written.

## Name a platform

Some helpers take the platform as their input, after a colon, as [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates) explains. Write `twitch`, `kick` or `youtube`, or the names `Twitch`, `Kick` or `YouTube`, in any mix of capitals.

`{platform.isLive:kick}` and `{platform.isLive:Kick}` give the same answer. A helper given a name it does not know, or no name, stays in the reply exactly as written, braces and all.

Numbers come out as whole numbers with the thousands separator of the channel's language, such as `1,234` for an English channel. Lists of platforms are always in the order Twitch, Kick, YouTube.

## Links and connections

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{platform.connected}` | The names of the connected platforms, separated by commas | `Twitch, Kick, YouTube` |
| `{platform.count}` | How many platforms are connected | `3` |
| `{platform.link.twitch}` | The link to the Twitch channel | `https://twitch.tv/samplechannel` |
| `{platform.link.kick}` | The link to the Kick channel | `https://kick.com/samplechannel` |
| `{platform.link.youtube}` | The link to the YouTube channel | `https://youtube.com/channel/UCsample` |
| `{platform.links}` | The links to every connected channel, without `https://`, separated by a dot | `twitch.tv/samplechannel · kick.com/samplechannel · youtube.com/channel/UCsample` |

A link helper for a platform that is not connected gives an empty text. These helpers do not depend on being live, so they suit a timer or a `!socials` command that runs while you are offline.

## Live state and viewers on one platform

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{platform.isLive}` | `true` when that platform is live now, otherwise `false` | `{platform.isLive:youtube}` gives `true` |
| `{platform.live.list}` | The names of the platforms that are live now | `Twitch, Kick, YouTube` |
| `{platform.live.count}` | How many platforms are live now | `3` |
| `{platform.viewers.twitch}` | The viewers on Twitch now | `42` |
| `{platform.viewers.kick}` | The viewers on Kick now | `10` |
| `{platform.viewers.youtube}` | The viewers on YouTube now | `8` |
| `{platform.title}` | The stream title on that platform | `{platform.title:youtube}` gives `Building a bot` |
| `{platform.link.youtube.live}` | The link to the YouTube stream that is live now | `https://youtube.com/watch?v=sample` |

`{platform.isLive}` is the one helper here that never comes out empty: a platform that is not connected gives `false`. `{platform.live.count}` gives `0` when platforms are connected and none is live, and an empty text when none is connected.

A viewer helper, `{platform.title}` and `{platform.link.youtube.live}` give an empty text while their platform is offline or not connected.

Live does not always come with a viewer count. Sometimes the bot sees that a platform is live but cannot read its number of viewers. Then `{platform.isLive:kick}` gives `true` while `{platform.viewers.kick}` is empty, so a reply that shows a count still needs words for the empty case.

A multistream channel can give its Kick viewers their own line, with a fallback for the missing count:

```
Watch on Kick: {platform.link.kick} · Viewers there: {default:{platform.viewers.kick}|not available right now}
```

## Viewers across platforms

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{platform.viewers.total}` | The viewers on all live platforms together | `60` |
| `{platform.viewers.breakdown}` | The viewers on each live platform | `Twitch 42 · Kick 10 · YouTube 8` |
| `{platform.viewers.share}` | The share of all viewers on that platform, as a whole percentage | `{platform.viewers.share:kick}` gives `17%` |
| `{platform.top}` | The name of the live platform with the most viewers | `Twitch` |

These helpers add up only the live platforms that report a viewer count. A live platform without a count is left out of the total, the breakdown and the share, and it never counts as 0. When Twitch reports 42, Kick reports 10 and YouTube is live without a count, `{platform.viewers.total}` gives `52` and the breakdown shows only Twitch and Kick.

When no live platform reports a count, all four give an empty text. `{platform.viewers.share}` is also empty for a platform that has no count of its own.

## Followers and chat totals

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{platform.followers.twitch}` | The Twitch follower count | `1,234` |
| `{platform.messages.stream}` | The chat messages on that platform | `{platform.messages.stream:kick}` gives `120` |
| `{platform.chatters.stream}` | The different people who chatted on that platform | `{platform.chatters.stream:youtube}` gives `6` |

`{platform.followers.twitch}` gives an empty text when Twitch is not connected, or when the bot cannot read the count from Twitch at that moment.

The two chat totals count from the start of the day the stream went live until now. They give an empty text while the channel is offline, and for a platform that is not connected. A connected platform where nobody chatted gives `0`, which is a real answer, unlike the empty text.

## The platform of the person or the event

These helpers read where the trigger came from, not the channel's connections. A command typed on Kick gives `kick`, even when Twitch is also live.

| Helper | What it gives | Example | Use in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `{platform.user}` | The platform of the person who used the command or caused the event | `twitch` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{platform.user.name}` | The name of that platform | `Twitch` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{platform.user.icon}` | A colored dot for that platform: 🟣 Twitch, 🟢 Kick, 🔴 YouTube | `🟣` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{platform.event}` | The platform the event came from | `twitch` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, Discord |

A timer has no person and no event, so none of these works there. In an event response, the three `platform.user` helpers work only for events that name a person, such as a follow, a sub or a cheer.

When the trigger names no person, the three `platform.user` helpers stay in the reply exactly as written.

To show the counts of the platform a viewer is on, rather than a combined value, pass the person's platform as the input: `{platform.viewers.share:{platform.user}}` gives the share of viewers watching where that person is.
