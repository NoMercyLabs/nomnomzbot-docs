Helpers on this page: `{user}`, `{user.*}`, `{viewer.*}`, `{engagement.*}`, `{name}`, `{link}`, `{subject}`, `{object}`, `{possessive}`, `{tense}`, `{presentTense}`, `{genderedTerm}`, `{verb:*}`.

These helpers are about the person who used the command, such as `{user}`, `{user.points}` and `{user.followAge}`.
They also hold that person's pronouns and the words that go with them, such as `{user.subject}` and `{user.possessive}`.
The bare helpers `{name}`, `{link}`, `{subject}`, `{object}`, `{possessive}`, `{tense}`, `{presentTense}`, `{genderedTerm}` and `{verb:plays|play}` are here too, and they follow a different rule.
They are about the person the command names, which is the first word after the command, as in `!hug @someone`.
The bot takes any word typed after the command as the person named, so `!lurk getting food` names "getting".
If nothing is typed after the command, they are about the person who used the command.
Type the `user.` form, such as `{user.subject}`, to always get the person who used the command.
Use these helpers to greet a viewer, to show their stats, or to write a reply that fits their pronouns.

`{user}` is the display name, written the way the viewer writes it.
In a chat reply, the bot writes the display name only when it matches the login apart from letter case. Otherwise, for example for a name in another alphabet, it writes the login.
`{user.name}` is the login name, in lower case.

## Pronouns

The pronoun and grammar helpers sit in the first table below.
The reply uses the person's saved pronouns, which can come from their own choice or the external pronoun service.
To learn where the bot gets pronouns and how to write a reply that fits he, she and they, read [Pronouns in templates](/reference/templates/pronouns).

## Welcome the person who asked

Put `Welcome {user}!` in a command reply to greet its caller.
Use `{target.displayname}` instead when the greeting is for someone named after the command.
Keeping those two people distinct matters for replies such as `{user} sends a hug to {target.displayname}.`

The points helpers read the bot's [channel currency](/reference/economy/currency-and-balances), not a viewer's Twitch Channel Points balance.
They report values; putting `{user.points}` in a reply does not grant or spend currency.

The examples illustrate possible values; your results depend on the channel and trigger.
**Use in** lists the template fields that accept each helper.
A pipeline still needs a trigger that supplies the relevant person, message, or event; event-specific helpers need that event.
For viewer-written text, the [Viewer Templates setting and permissions](/reference/settings/features) also apply.

## When a value is not available

A `{user.*}` helper needs a person. If the reply has no trigger person, the helper stays in the reply as written.
The pronoun helpers are the exception. They print `they`, `them`, `their`, `are` and `person`, and `{user.verb:a|b}` prints the plural form. `{user.tense}` prints `are` or `were`.
`{user.pronouns}` has no fallback. When no pronoun is on record for the viewer, it stays in the reply as written.
`{user.lastmessage}` is the other exception and prints empty.
A viewer with no record in your channel reads as not a follower, not a subscriber, 0 for every count and empty for every date. `{viewer.firstseen}` prints `unknown` instead.
A detail that Twitch cannot give prints empty. This holds for the color, picture, bio and account date.
`{user.followAge}` prints `not following` when the channel has no follow record for the viewer. It prints `unknown` when the bot could not ask Twitch.
A viewer with no streak reads as 0.

The points helpers show 0 for a viewer with no balance.
The rank helpers print empty when the viewer is not on the currency ranking. The ranking exists only when the channel has a leaderboard on the balance metric. Without one, every rank helper prints empty for every viewer.
`{user.points.nextRank.name}` and `{user.points.nextRank.gap}` also print empty for the viewer in first place.
`{user.points.nextRank.gap}` is the balance difference to the viewer one place above. That balance only ties that place, so it is not enough to pass.

The `{user.stats.*}` and `{viewer.*}` helpers read the totals that the bot recorded for your channel.
`{user.stats.streak}` and `{user.stats.streak.best}` come from the watch-streak notifications of Twitch.
The `{engagement.*}` helpers carry a value in one event only.
`{engagement.streak}` comes with a watch-streak notification. `{engagement.daysSinceLastSeen}` comes with a returning chatter. `{engagement.months}` and `{engagement.years}` come with a moderator anniversary.
In any other reply, they carry no value.

| `{object}` | The object-case pronoun (e.g. them) | `them` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{possessive}` | The possessive pronoun (e.g. their) | `their` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{presentTense}` | The present-tense to-be form (e.g. is/are) | `are` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{subject}` | The subject-case pronoun (e.g. they) | `they` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{tense}` | Is or are while your channel is live, was or were when it is offline. | `{user.genderedTerm}` | A gendered term for the triggering user | `person` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, viewer text |
| `{user.object}` | The object-case pronoun for the triggering user | `them` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, viewer text |
| `{user.possessive}` | The possessive pronoun for the triggering user | `their` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, viewer text |
| `{user.presentTense}` | The present-tense to-be form for the triggering user | `are` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, viewer text |
| `{user.pronouns}` | The triggering user recorded pronouns | `they/them` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, viewer text |
| `{user.subject}` | The subject-case pronoun for the triggering user | `they` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, viewer text |
| `{user.tense}` | Is or are while your channel is live, was or were when it is offline. The verb agrees with the person who used the command. In a shoutout it follows the live state of the person shouted out instead | `are` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks, viewer text |
| `{user.verb:<sing\|plur>}` | A singular/plural verb agreeing with the triggering user (e.g. `{user.verb:plays\|play}`) | `{user.verb:has\|have}` gives `has` | Commands, event responses, pipelines, webhooks |
| `{verb:<sing\|plur>}` | The first form when the person takes is (he, she, any other single pronoun, or a name), and the second form for they.
