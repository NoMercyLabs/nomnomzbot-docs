---
title: Chat activity helpers
description: The activity helpers for chat speed, who is chatting now, stream and daily totals, and the top or a random chatter.
---

The `{activity.*}` helpers read your channel's chat at the moment the reply goes out.
They work in a command reply, an event response, a timer, a pipeline step, a Discord notification and a webhook body.
They do not work in a viewer's own text, even with **Viewer Templates** on in [Features](/reference/settings/features).
If you have not used braces in a reply yet, read [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates) first.

Pick the period first.
For someone who is chatting right now, use the helpers in [Who is chatting right now](#who-is-chatting-right-now), which read the last hour.
For the person who talked most all stream, use the helpers in [Your latest stream](#your-latest-stream).
The live counts and the daily totals come after those.

Counts use the digit grouping of your channel's language, so English gives `1,080`.
That is the **Language** setting on **Bot basics**, in the **Commands and language** card, and English is used when none is set.
Most counts give `0` when there is nothing to count, like `{activity.messages.today}` before the first message of the day.
The counts of your latest stream, the live counts and `{activity.top.today.count}` are the exception: each section below says when its helpers print as typed instead of `0`.

When a helper has nothing to show, it prints as you typed it, braces and all.
That happens when a helper that names a person, an hour or a day has nobody to name, when an input is missing or out of range, and in the cases that the sections below name.
One helper works differently: `{activity.top.list}` prints nothing at all when your latest stream has no counted chat.

The helpers that name the top chatter can name the person who set off the reply.
Only `{activity.last.chatter}` skips that person.
In a command, that is the person who used it, and in an event response it is the viewer the event is about, such as a new follower.
A timer has nobody behind it, so there `{activity.last.chatter}` names the last person who chatted.

## Who is chatting right now

These helpers read the last 60 minutes of chat, on every platform you connect.
A helper that takes minutes accepts a whole number from 1 to 60, as in `{activity.chatters.active:10}`.
The bot keeps this hour in memory.
After the bot restarts, the counts start again from `0`, and the helpers that name someone print as typed until people chat again.

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{activity.mpm}` | Messages in the last minute. With minutes, the average per minute over that many minutes, rounded to a whole number | `{activity.mpm:5}` gives `18` |
| `{activity.messages.hour}` | Messages in the last 60 minutes | `1,080` |
| `{activity.chatters.active}` | Different people who chatted in the last few minutes. It needs the number of minutes | `{activity.chatters.active:10}` gives `12` |
| `{activity.random.recent}` | One person picked at random from those who chatted in the last few minutes. Everyone has the same chance, however many messages they sent. It needs the number of minutes | `{activity.random.recent:5}` gives `ViewerTwo` |
| `{activity.top.hour}` | The person who sent the most messages in the last 60 minutes. On a tie, the one who chatted first in that hour | `ViewerTwo` |
| `{activity.last.chatter}` | The last person who chatted in the last 60 minutes, other than the person who set off the reply | `ViewerTwo` |

To name someone who is in chat now, rather than whoever talked most over a long stretch, pick from the last few minutes:

```
{activity.random.recent:5}, you have been chosen!
```

This gives `ViewerTwo, you have been chosen!`.
The person who used the command can be picked too, if they chatted in those minutes.
When nobody chatted in the last 5 minutes, the reply shows `{activity.random.recent:5}` as typed, so a short window suits a busy chat best.

## Your latest stream

These helpers count the saved chat of your latest stream: the one you are live in now, or the last one when you are offline.
People who are banned in your channel are left out.
Before your first stream, they print as typed.

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{activity.messages.stream}` | Messages in the stream | `2,430` |
| `{activity.top.stream}` | The person who sent the most messages, with their count in brackets | `ViewerOne (140)` |
| `{activity.top.list}` | The top chatters, each name followed by its count after a space, separated by commas. It needs how many people to list | `{activity.top.list:3}` gives `ViewerOne 140, ViewerTwo 96, ViewerThree 40` |

`{activity.top.stream}` puts the count in brackets and `{activity.top.list}` does not, so pick the one whose shape fits your sentence.
You can ask for up to 25, but the list never shows more than 5, so `{activity.top.list:6}` prints the same as `{activity.top.list:5}`.
When two people tie on messages, they go in order by name, with capital letters before lowercase letters, so `Zed` comes before `abe`.
`{activity.top.stream}` names the first of them.
When the stream has no counted chat yet, `{activity.top.stream}` prints as typed and `{activity.top.list}` prints nothing.

## What the bot has counted live

These helpers read what the bot holds right now, not the saved chat of a stream.
The chatter count comes from Twitch at the moment of the reply, and when Twitch does not answer, `{activity.chatters.now}` and `{activity.chatters.lurkers}` print as typed.

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{activity.chatters.now}` | People connected to your Twitch chat right now, as Twitch counts them | `51` |
| `{activity.chatters.unique}` | Different people who have chatted since your stream went live, on every platform you connect | `33` |
| `{activity.chatters.lurkers}` | People in chat who have not chatted: the Twitch count minus `{activity.chatters.unique}`, never below 0 | `18` |
| `{activity.commands.stream}` | Commands that ran successfully since the bot joined your chat | `88` |
| `{activity.last.message.ago}` | How long since the bot last saw a message that starts with your command prefix, even an unknown one such as `!typo` | `4s`, `3m 12s` or `1h 5m` |

Unlike the stream helpers, `{activity.chatters.unique}` counts everyone who chatted since your stream went live, banned people and messages after the stream ended included.
If the bot joined your chat after the stream started, it counts from that moment.
Because of that, `{activity.chatters.lurkers}` can come out lower than the real number, down to `0`: people who chatted on another platform, or chatted and then left, still count as chatters.

`{activity.commands.stream}` does not start again with a new stream.
It starts again when the bot joins your chat again, such as after a restart, and after the quiet time described below.
`{activity.last.message.ago}` also starts again when your stream goes live and when the bot joins your chat.

The bot lets go of these live counts when your stream is offline and more than 2 hours have passed since the last of these: your stream went live, the bot joined your chat, or a message started with your command prefix.
It starts them again with the next chat message or when your stream goes live.
Until then, `{activity.chatters.unique}`, `{activity.commands.stream}` and `{activity.last.message.ago}` print as typed.
This shows up in a Discord notification or a webhook body that goes out during that quiet time.

## Today and the last 90 days

These helpers read the bot's saved chat history, which it keeps in days that run from midnight to midnight UTC.
Your channel time zone is the **Timezone** setting next to **Language**, and UTC is used when none is set.

`{activity.messages.today}`, `{activity.top.today}` and `{activity.top.today.count}` read the UTC day that has today's date in your channel time zone.
Outside UTC, near the start or the end of your day, the newest messages can be missing from today.

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{activity.messages.today}` | Messages today, or `0` when nobody has chatted yet | `2,610` |
| `{activity.top.today}` | The person who sent the most messages today. On a tie, either person can be named | `ViewerOne` |
| `{activity.top.today.count}` | How many messages that person sent today. It prints as typed when nobody has chatted yet | `140` |
| `{activity.busiestHour}` | The hour of the day with the most messages over the last 90 days, in your channel time zone, on the 24-hour clock | `21:00` |
| `{activity.busiestDay}` | The day of the week with the most messages over the last 90 days, in your channel's language | `Friday` |

When two hours have the same number of messages, the earlier hour wins.
When two days tie, the day earlier in the week wins, with Sunday as the first day.
