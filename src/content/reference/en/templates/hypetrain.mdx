Helpers on this page: `{hypetrain*}`, `{hype_train*}`.

There are two kinds of hype train helper, and the spelling tells them apart.
`{hypetrain.*}` asks Twitch about the train that runs now, at the moment the reply goes out.
`{hype_train.*}`, with the underscore, carries the numbers of the start or end event that fired the reply.
None of them take arguments. A `{hypetrain.*}` helper given an argument shows exactly as you wrote it.

## Before you use them

A hype train is a Twitch feature, so these helpers read your Twitch channel only.

The `{hypetrain.*}` helpers work in commands, event responses, timers, pipeline steps, Discord notifications and webhook bodies.
They only have something to show while a train runs.
When no train runs, each of them shows exactly as you wrote it, braces and all, with these exceptions:

- `{hypetrain.isActive}`, `{hypetrain.isGolden}` and `{hypetrain.isShared}` show `false`.
- `{hypetrain.record.level}` still shows the highest level your channel ever reached. It shows as written only when your channel has never had a hype train.

When the bot cannot read the hype train from Twitch, the three flags still show `false`, and every other helper, `{hypetrain.record.level}` included, shows as written.

The `{hype_train.*}` helpers work only in an event response for **Hype train starts** or **Hype train ends**, or in a pipeline started by one of those events.

## Show the running train in a command

The live helpers in one reply share one read from Twitch, so their numbers always agree.

- `level` is the level the train is on now.
- `progress` is the points earned toward the next level, `goal` is the points that level needs, and `remaining` is the difference, never below `0`.
- `percent` is `progress` out of `goal`, rounded to a whole number, such as `60%`.
- `bar` draws 10 marks and adds the percent: `██████░░░░ 60%`.
- `total` is all the points the train has collected so far.
- `timeLeft` is the time before the train ends, such as `4m 10s`. From one hour on it reads like `1h 5m`, and under a minute like `45s`.
- `topContributor` is the display name of the person with the biggest contribution to the running train.

`progress`, `goal`, `remaining` and `total` use the thousands separator of the channel's **Language**, and English when it is not set.

A live helper with no train shows as written, so wrap a `!train` reply in `{hypetrain.isActive}` to give it a sentence for both cases:

`{if.hypetrain.isActive:Hype train level {hypetrain.level} is at {hypetrain.bar}, {hypetrain.remaining} points to go with {hypetrain.timeLeft} left!|No hype train right now. Our record is level {hypetrain.record.level}.}`

During a train at level 3 with 1,800 of 3,000 points, this gives `Hype train level 3 is at ██████░░░░ 60%, 1,200 points to go with 4m 10s left!`.

`{hypetrain.isGolden}` is `true` for a Golden Kappa train, and `{hypetrain.isShared}` is `true` for a train shared with other channels.

## Reply when a train starts or ends

An event response for **Hype train starts** or **Hype train ends** gets the event's own numbers:

- `{hype_train.level}` is the level, and `{hype_train.total}` is the points collected.
- `{hype_train.goal}` is the points needed for the next level. Only **Hype train starts** has it.
- `{hype_train.id}` is the train's Twitch ID.

These numbers have no thousands separator, so a total shows as `6400`.

To name a participant when the train starts, add `{hypetrain.topContributor}`, which reads the running train:

`A hype train started at level {hype_train.level}! {hypetrain.topContributor} got it rolling.`

In a **Hype train ends** response the train no longer runs, so use the `{hype_train.*}` helpers there and leave the live ones out.

## All hype train helpers

The live examples show a running train at level 3 with 1,800 of 3,000 points toward the next level and 5,400 in total. It is not a Golden Kappa train and not a shared train. The event examples show a train at level 3 with 6,400 points and a goal of 8,000.
