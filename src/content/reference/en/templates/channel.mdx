---
title: Channel helpers
description: The template helpers that name your channel and read its counts, profile, settings, raids and live status, whoever runs the command.
---

Every helper on this page reads your channel, never the person who triggered the reply.
When a moderator runs a command with `{channel}` in it, the reply names your channel, not the moderator's.
For how braces work and which fields take them, read [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates) first.

A link to your Twitch channel, in a command any moderator can use:

```
Follow {channel.display} at https://www.twitch.tv/{channel}
```

The example writes the Twitch address out, because `{channel.url}` works only in a Discord notification.
For Kick, replace the whole address with `{platform.link.kick}`, as in `Follow {channel.display} at {platform.link.kick}`.
It gives the full link to the Kick channel you connected, and nothing when no Kick channel is connected.

All of these helpers work in commands, event responses, timers, pipelines, Discord notifications and webhooks, unless a section says otherwise.
Only the two raid counts take an input.

The name and settings helpers read your channel in the bot.
The counts and profile helpers come from your Twitch channel, and `{channel.members}` comes from YouTube.
A helper with no value to give prints exactly as you typed it, braces and all, and so does a count or profile helper when Twitch gives no answer.
A few helpers give empty text instead, and their rows say so.

## Name and ID

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{channel}` | Your channel's login name, the one in your channel link | `example_channel` |
| `{channel.display}` | Your channel's display name, or the login name when it has none | `Example_Channel` |
| `{streamer}` | The same display name as `{channel.display}` | `Example_Channel` |
| `{channel.mention}` | Your display name with an `@` in front, to mention your channel in chat | `@Example_Channel` |
| `{channel.id}` | Your channel's Twitch ID, a number, not a name | `123456789` |

Use `{channel}` when a reply needs your login name. It works in every field listed above.

`{channel}`, `{channel.display}` and `{streamer}` also work in text that viewers type after a command or in a reward input.
Two things must both be true for that.
The **Viewer Templates** feature is on, in the **Chat** area of [Features](/reference/settings/features); it is on by default.
The viewer also holds the template permission, which subscribers and higher hold by default.
For anyone else, the text stays exactly as they typed it.

## Channel settings

These read your channel's settings in the bot.

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{channel.language}` | Your channel's language code, `en` when none is set | `en` |
| `{channel.timezone}` | Your channel's time zone, `UTC` when none is set | `Europe/Amsterdam` |
| `{channel.localTime}` | The time now in your channel's time zone, on a 24-hour clock | `15:30` |
| `{channel.personality}` | The personality the bot uses in your channel | `Informative` |
| `{channel.prefix}` | The prefix your commands start with | `!` |

## Counts

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{channel.followers}` | How many followers your channel has, with a thousands separator | `1,234` |
| `{channel.followers.compact}` | The same count, short, with one decimal at most: k for thousands, M for millions | `1.2k` |
| `{channel.subs}` | How many subscribers your channel has | `80` |
| `{channel.members}` | How many members your YouTube channel has; as typed when no YouTube account is connected or YouTube does not show its members | `12` |
| `{channel.editors.count}` | How many editors your channel has | `2` |
| `{channel.mods.count}` | How many moderators your channel has | `6` |
| `{channel.vips.count}` | How many VIPs your channel has | `4` |

The bot keeps the YouTube member count for 5 minutes, so `{channel.members}` can be up to 5 minutes old.

## Profile

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{channel.tags}` | Your channel's tags, separated by commas, empty when you have none | `English, Coding` |
| `{channel.ccls}` | Your channel's content labels (ccls is short for content classification labels), separated by commas, empty when you have none | `MatureGame` |
| `{channel.isBranded}` | `true` when your stream is marked as branded content, otherwise `false` | `false` |
| `{channel.team}` | The name of your Twitch team; with more than one team, the first one Twitch lists | `SampleTeam` |
| `{channel.vod.latest}` | The link to your latest past broadcast | `https://www.twitch.tv/videos/123` |
| `{channel.vod.latest.title}` | The title of your latest past broadcast | `Chill Saturday` |
| `{channel.age}` | How old your Twitch account is, in years and months | `6 years, 2 months` |

An account under a month old gets its age in days instead, and one under a day old in hours or minutes.

Content labels are the Twitch warnings on your stream, such as drugs, gambling or profanity.
`{channel.ccls}` gives them as Twitch names them, for example `DrugsIntoxication` or `ProfanityVulgarity`.
Twitch adds `MatureGame` itself, from your category.

`{channel.team}` prints as typed when your channel is in no team, and `{channel.vod.latest}` and `{channel.vod.latest.title}` do when you have no past broadcast.

## Raids

These read the raids the bot has on record for your channel, from the raids it saw come in and go out.

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{channel.raids.outCount:login}` | How many times your channel raided the channel you name | `3` |
| `{channel.raids.inCount:login}` | How many times the channel you name raided yours | `5` |
| `{channel.raids.lastOut}` | The display name of the channel you raided most recently | `OtherStreamer` |
| `{channel.raids.lastIn.at}` | The date of the most recent raid into your channel, as year-month-day in UTC | `2026-10-24` |

The two counts need the other channel's login name after a colon, as in `{channel.raids.inCount:otherstreamer}`.
An `@` in front of the name is fine.
A name with no raids on record gives `0`.
So does a name Twitch does not know, or cannot look up at that moment.
Without a name, the count prints as typed.

The counts are for the channel you name.
The last-raid helpers give your most recent raid in that direction, with any channel.
When there is no raid in that direction yet, they print as typed.

## Live status

| Helper | What it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{status}` | `live` while your stream is live, otherwise `offline` | `live` |

In a shoutout reply, `{status}` is about the channel you shout out, not yours.

## Discord notifications

These six are filled in only in a Discord notification, and some only in certain posts.

| Helper | What it gives | Which posts | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `{channel.url}` | The link to your channel on Twitch or Kick | Every post | `https://www.twitch.tv/example_channel` |
| `{channel.login}` | Your login name in lowercase, the name inside that link | Every post | `example_channel` |
| `{channel.avatar}` | The link to your profile picture | Every post | `https://static-cdn.jtvnw.net/jtv_user_pictures/example_channel.png` |
| `{channel.name}` | Your display name, the same name `{channel.display}` gives | Go-live and stream-end posts | `Example_Channel` |
| `{channel.title}` | Your stream title | Go-live posts | `Cozy coding night` |
| `{channel.game}` | Your stream category | Go-live posts | `Just Chatting` |

In a Discord post they are not listed for, `{channel.name}`, `{channel.title}` and `{channel.game}` print as typed.
A command, event response or timer that uses one of the six is not saved, and the error says where the helper works.

On a platform other than Twitch or Kick, `{channel.url}` is empty, and so is `{channel.avatar}` when you have no profile picture.
A link or picture field of the embed that ends up empty is left out of the post, so the rest of it still goes out.
