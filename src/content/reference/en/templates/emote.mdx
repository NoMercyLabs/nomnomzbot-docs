Helpers on this page: `{emote}`, `{emote.*}`.

Most of these helpers work in commands, event responses, timers, pipeline steps, Discord notifications and webhook bodies.
The ones that read a viewer or a message need a trigger that brings one, so they do not work in timers.
The **Use in** column of the table at the end lists the fields for each helper.

The emotes a helper can see are the ones your channel can show: your own Twitch emotes, the Twitch globals, and your 7TV, BTTV and FFZ sets with their globals.
When you name an emote in a helper, write its code the way viewers type it.
An exact match wins, and a code that differs only in capitals is used when there is no exact one.

## Look up one emote

`{emote:catJAM}` gives `catJAM` when your channel can show it, and nothing when it cannot.
Add a fallback after a pipe to send other text instead: `{emote:catJAM|hype}` gives `hype` when there is no `catJAM`.

`{emote.image:catJAM}` gives the picture link of the emote, and `{emote.provider:catJAM}` gives the network it comes from: `7TV`, `BTTV`, `FFZ` or `Twitch`.
Both give nothing when the emote is not there.

## Repeat an emote or build a wall

`{emote.repeat:catJAM|3}` gives `catJAM catJAM catJAM`.
The count is a whole number from 1 to 50.

`{emote.wall:catJAM|2|3}` gives 2 rows of 3, with a slash between the rows: `catJAM catJAM catJAM / catJAM catJAM catJAM`.
A wall has 1 to 10 rows and 1 to 20 columns.

A count outside those limits, or one that is not a plain number, leaves the helper in the reply exactly as you wrote it, braces and all.
An emote your channel cannot show gives nothing.

`{emote.repeat}` is the only emote helper a viewer can use in their own text, such as the words after a command, when the [Viewer Templates setting and permissions](/reference/settings/features) allow it.
There it takes a plain emote code only, not a link or another helper, and an emote your channel cannot show leaves the helper as the viewer wrote it.

## Pick, list or count a set

Each set has three helpers: a pick gives one random emote, a list gives every emote one space apart, and a count gives how many there are.

| Set | Pick | List | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Your own Twitch emotes | `{emote.random}` | `{emote.list.channel}` | `{emote.count.channel}` |
| Your 7TV set | `{emote.random.7tv}` | `{emote.list.7tv}` | `{emote.count.7tv}` |
| Your BTTV set | `{emote.random.bttv}` | `{emote.list.bttv}` | `{emote.count.bttv}` |
| Your FFZ set | `{emote.random.ffz}` | `{emote.list.ffz}` | `{emote.count.ffz}` |

The 7TV, BTTV and FFZ helpers in this table read only your channel's own set, not that network's globals.
Your own Twitch emotes are your subscriber, follower and Bits tier emotes, and you can pick from one kind with `{emote.random.sub}`, `{emote.random.follower}` or `{emote.random.bits}`.
`{emote.random.global}` picks a Twitch global, and `{emote.random.any}` picks from every emote your channel can show.

A pick is new each time the reply runs.
Pick, list and count helpers take no arguments, so they have no fallback text.
When you add an argument anyway, the helper stays in the reply as written, braces and all.
When a set is empty, a pick and a list give nothing and the count gives `0`.
So `Today's emote: {emote.random.7tv}` on a channel without a 7TV set sends `Today's emote:` and nothing after it.
A count uses the number style of your channel's language, such as `1,204` in English.

## Show a cheermote

`{emote.cheermote:100}` gives the cheermote for 100 Bits, at the biggest tier the amount reaches: `Cheer100`.
It uses the standard `Cheer` cheermote, and gives nothing when no tier fits.
The amount is a whole number of at least 1; anything else leaves the helper as written.

## Show chat's most used emote

These helpers count the emotes in the newest 5,000 chat lines of your channel.
Lines that ran a command are not counted.
A tie goes to the code that sorts first by character code, so a capital letter beats a lowercase one (KEKW beats catJAM).
When nobody used an emote, the helper gives nothing.

- `{emote.channel.top}` is the emote chat used most.
- `{emote.channel.top.stream}` counts only the lines of the stream that is live now, so it gives nothing while you are offline.
- `{emote.user.top}` is the emote used most by the viewer who triggered the reply, and `{emote.user.top.count}` is how many times they used it.

`{emote.user.favorite}` is a different thing: it does not look at chat.
It gives the emote you, the channel owner, starred most recently.
To star one, open **Chat**, open **Emotes and emoji** in the message box, and right-click the emote.

## Read the emotes in a message

`{emote.message.count}` gives how many emotes the triggering message holds, with repeats counted, and `{emote.message.first}` gives the first one.
For a command, that is the chat line that ran it.
Without a message, the count is `0` and the first emote is empty.
