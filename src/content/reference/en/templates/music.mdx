You type these helpers into a reply, such as a command reply, an event response or a timer message.
The bot fills them in from your channel's music when it sends the line.
Reading a value changes nothing.

`{music.now}` is the current track, and the queue helpers describe the song requests that wait after it.
So `Now playing: {music.now}` gives `Now playing: Song A by Artist A`, and `Up next: {music.queue.next}` gives `Up next: Song B by Artist B`.

The `music.now.*` helpers, `{music.volume}`, `{music.shuffle}` and `{music.repeat}` read the connected [music player](/reference/music/music-player).
The `music.requests.*` helpers read your saved request settings, and the history helpers read the requests the bot has stored, so both answer without a player.
No music helper works in text that viewers write. There it prints as written, even when the [Viewer Templates setting](/reference/settings/features) is on.

## When there is nothing to show

A helper that has nothing to read prints as written, braces and all.
When the player has no track, `Now playing: {music.now}` goes out exactly like that.
So test the reply once with the music stopped.
`{music.now.isPlaying}` always answers, with `false` when no track plays.
`{music.now.status}` always gives one of its five states.

An empty queue is a real answer, not a missing one.
`{music.queue.length}` and `{music.queue.requesters}` give `0`, and `{music.queue.isEmpty}` gives `true`.
The helpers that name or time songs in the queue, such as `{music.queue.next}`, `{music.queue.list:3}` and `{music.queue.duration}`, print as written when the queue is empty.

A track missing one detail behaves the same.
A track with no album leaves `{music.now.album}` as written, and a track nobody requested leaves `{music.now.requester}` as written.
When only the artist is missing, `{music.now}` gives the title alone.

## How far back the history reads

The history helpers read the newest 500 requests of your channel that have started playing.
A request counts only once it starts playing, and its time is the moment it started.
So a song that still waits in the queue is not in `{music.history.last}`, `{music.user.lastRequest}` or the stream counts.

The helpers ending in `.stream` keep only the songs in that set that started since the stream went live.
They print as written while you are offline.
`{music.user.requests.total}` reads the viewer's all-time count from their viewer profile instead, so the 500 limit does not apply to it.

## Values a pipeline step fills in

A [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines) still needs a trigger that supplies the person, message or event a helper reads.
Six helpers only work in a pipeline, and each group works in a different place.
The four `music.favorite.*` values work in the steps that come after the favorite step.
The two playlist values work only in the messages of the **Add to playlist** step itself.

The **Song request favorite** step (`song_request_favorite`) queues a viewer's most-requested song and fills in the four `music.favorite.*` values.
It tries up to 10 of the viewer's tracks, most-requested first, and `{music.favorite.outcome}` says how that went:

- `queued`: a favorite went into the queue, and the other three values describe it.
- `unavailable`: the music service could not take the track. The values still name the track it tried.
- `none`: the viewer has no request history, or each favorite was refused, for example because it was blocked or already in the queue. With no history at all, the track and artist are empty and the count is `0`.

The **Add to playlist** step fills in `{playlist_id}` and `{track_name}` for its own two messages.
One message goes out after the track is added, and the other when the track is already in the playlist.
A later step does not get these two values.
When the step adds the current track, `{track_name}` is that track's name. When the step adds a fixed track, `{track_name}` is that track's address, not a name.

## The queue

Some helpers look only at the first 10 songs in the queue: `{music.queue.position}`, `{music.queue.position.ordinal}`, `{music.queue.wait}`, `{music.queue.wait.at}`, the four `music.queue.mine.*` helpers and `{music.queue.target.count}`.
For a viewer whose song is at place 11 or later, they print as written, and the counts give a number that is too low.
`{music.queue.item}` and `{music.queue.list}` read the whole queue, so this limit does not apply to them.
