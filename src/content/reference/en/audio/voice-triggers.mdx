---
title: Voice triggers
description: Count a word you say on stream, and pop a sticker on your stream each time the bot hears it.
---

You set up voice triggers on the **Voice triggers** page, in the **Build** group of the sidebar.
A voice trigger is a word you say out loud on stream.
Each time the bot hears it, the bot adds one to its count and can show a sticker on your stream.

A voice trigger listens to your microphone, not to chat.
It does not speak: reading text aloud is [Text to speech](/reference/audio/text-to-speech).

## Who can use this page

A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page and change voice triggers.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

The **Broadcaster** may also let a **VIP** open the page.
A VIP then sees the list, the counts, **Popout listener** and **Copy listener link**.
**New voice trigger** and the editor do not show for a VIP.
The listener link holds the channel's overlay key, so allow a VIP only when you trust them with it.

## How the bot hears you

The bot hears you through the voice listener.
The voice listener is a web page that you keep open in Chrome while you stream.
It uses the speech recognition built into Chrome, set to US English.

1. Open the **Voice triggers** page in Chrome, and choose **Popout listener** in the page header.
   The listener opens in its own small window of the same browser, with the title **Voice Listener**.
2. Allow the microphone when Chrome asks.
   The listener shows **Listening**, and the words it hears show in its box.
3. Leave the window open while you stream.

Do not add the listener to OBS as a browser source.
Speech recognition does not run inside OBS.
When you close the listener, no voice trigger counts until you open it again.

**Copy listener link** on the **Voice triggers** list copies the same link.
Paste it into Chrome when the dashboard runs in another browser.
The link holds your channel's overlay key: the secret that lets a page act for your channel's overlays, so do not share it.

The listener shows each phrase it heard, newest first, with the last 20 kept.
Next to each phrase it shows the result.

| The listener shows | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Matched "word" (count N) | The phrase held a voice trigger word, and the count went up. |
| (no trigger word) | No voice trigger counted for this phrase. |
| (not sent: this listener link is no longer valid) | The link is old. Copy a new one from the **Voice triggers** page. |
| (not sent: the bot could not be reached) | The listener could not reach the bot. |

The listener restarts itself when Chrome stops listening.
When Chrome blocks the microphone, the listener shows **Stopped**.
A line under it says what to allow.

While your stream is live, the bot also keeps every phrase the listener sends as the spoken transcript of that stream.
When you are not live, the bot only looks for a trigger word in each phrase, and does not store the phrase.

## Create a voice trigger

1. Choose **New voice trigger** in the page header.
   The **New voice trigger** dialog opens.
2. Type the word in **Word to listen for**, for example `hello`.
3. Set **Start counting at**, or leave it at 0.
   Use it to carry over a count from another bot.
4. Set **Seconds between counts**, or leave it at 5.
5. Pick a **Sticker**, or leave **No sticker**.
6. Choose **Create voice trigger**.
   The dialog closes, and the new word shows in the list, switched on.

When the list is empty, the page shows "No voice triggers yet."
Its **Make the first voice trigger** button opens the same dialog.

When **Word to listen for** is empty, the dialog shows "Give the trigger a word to listen for."

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Word to listen for** | The word or words the bot listens for, up to 200 characters. |
| **Start counting at** | The count the trigger starts from. You set it only when you create the trigger. |
| **Seconds between counts** | After a count, the trigger waits this long before it counts again. From 0 to 3600. |
| **Sticker** | An image from your uploaded images. It pops up on your stream each time the word is heard. |

## How a word is matched

The bot checks each phrase the listener sends.
A trigger counts when its word is anywhere in the phrase, in upper or lower case.
So the word `cat` also counts in "category".

Only one voice trigger counts for each phrase.
A trigger that is switched off counts nothing.

When the word is heard again within **Seconds between counts**, it is not counted.
This stops one long word from counting twice.

## Show the sticker on your stream

The sticker shows through an **Alerts** overlay, as [Widgets and alerts](/reference/overlays/widgets-and-alerts) explains.
A new **Alerts** overlay does not show voice triggers, so you switch them on once.

1. Open the **Alerts** overlay on the **Overlays** page.
2. In **Settings**, add **Voice trigger** to **Alert types**.
3. Choose **Save settings**.
   The editor shows "Saved.", and open sources reload.

**Sticker on-screen time (ms)** in the same **Settings** sets how long the sticker stays.

Each time the word counts, the alert shows the word with the new count, such as "hello (3)", and the sticker image.

## Edit a voice trigger

1. Choose a word in the **Voice triggers** list.
   The editor opens on the right.
2. Change **Word to listen for**, **Seconds between counts** or **Sticker**.
   The footer shows how many changes are not saved.
3. Choose **Save changes**.
   The editor shows the saved values.

**Discard** puts the fields back to the saved values.

The top of the editor shows the sticker image and three facts.
They change on their own as the word is heard.

| Fact | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Heard so far** | The count now. |
| **Started at** | The count the trigger started from. |
| **Last heard** | When the word last counted, or "Not yet". |

The list shows each word with its count, such as "Heard 3 times".

## Turn a voice trigger off or on

Use the **Enabled** switch at the top of the editor.
It saves at once.
A trigger that is off stays in the list with an **Off** badge, keeps its count, and counts nothing.

## Delete a voice trigger

1. Open the voice trigger and choose **Delete**.
   A dialog asks "Delete hello?" with the word you chose.
2. Choose **Delete voice trigger**.
   The word leaves the list, and its count is lost.

The sticker image stays in your uploaded images.

## Check that the listener works

The page header shows when the bot last counted any word.

| The header shows | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| No match heard yet | No voice trigger has counted yet. |
| Last match: just now | A word counted in the last minute. |
| Last match: and how long ago | When the last count was. |

This shows a past count only.
It does not prove that the listener is open now.

::Troubleshooting
**I say the word, but the count does not go up.**
Check that the listener window is open and shows **Listening**.
Check that the trigger is switched on, and that **Seconds between counts** has passed.
Look at the listener: it shows what Chrome heard, which may be a different spelling.
::

::Troubleshooting
**The count goes up, but no sticker shows.**
Check that the trigger has a **Sticker**.
Check that **Voice trigger** is in **Alert types** of your **Alerts** overlay.
Check that the **Alerts** overlay is in OBS.
::
