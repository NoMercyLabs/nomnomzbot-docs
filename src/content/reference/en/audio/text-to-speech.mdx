Text to speech turns a written line into audio that your viewers can hear.
You might use it to read a channel-point reward, speak a command's reply or thank someone during an event response.
The **Text to speech** page sets the voice and rules shared by those features.

Turning speech on does not make every chat line speak by itself.
A command, reward, event response or pipeline must request the speech, and the browser source in OBS must be ready to play it.
Set up that path first, then use the voice and message settings to decide how it should sound.

## Hear your first spoken reply

1. In the dashboard, open the **OBS** page, choose **Overlays in OBS**, and find **TTS caption** in **Required overlays**.
2. Choose **Connect**. In the OBS app, add it as a **Browser** source using the dialog's settings. Keep **Control audio via OBS** on.
3. Open **Text to speech** and choose **Rules**. Check that **Read messages aloud** is on and **Moderators approve first** is off for this first test.
Also check that **Who may be heard** is **Everyone** and **Minimum bits** is 0, so the command in step 5 is read.
4. Choose a voice on **Voices** and use its play button to hear a sample.
5. On a [chat command](/reference/commands/create-a-command), enable **Read it aloud**, save it and run the command in chat.

You should hear the reply through OBS and see the caption when the source displays it.
The dashboard's voice sample checks the voice; running the command checks the path through to your stream.
For viewer-written speech instead, use **Rewards that speak** below.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | The **Spoken history** and a short summary of the **Settings**. |
| **Voices** | The voice list, where you pick the channel voice. |
| **Rules** | The **Speaking** settings, the **Message rules** and the **Rewards that speak**. |
| **Pronunciation** | Rules that make the bot say a word another way. |
| **Keys** | Your own voice provider keys, and how your channel name is spoken. |

The speech plays through **TTS caption**, which also displays the spoken words.
**Bot Audio** handles sound clips and redemption sounds; it is a different source.
[OBS browser source](/reference/overlays/obs-browser-source) shows how.
The **Speaker overlay** row on the **Overview** tab opens **Required overlays** on the **OBS** page, where **TTS caption** is listed.

## Who can use this page

A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page and change the settings.
By default, switching a reward on in **Rewards that speak** needs the **Editor** role.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

The **Broadcaster** may also let a **VIP** open the page.
A VIP then sees the settings, but cannot change them.
A VIP does not see the play buttons: playing a voice sample needs **Moderator** or higher.
The **Keys** tab shows only to someone who may change the settings.

## Which voice speaks

A line the bot writes, such as a command reply or an event response, is spoken in the channel voice by default.
A **Read aloud (TTS)** step in a [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines) sets the text it reads.
These settings on this page still apply to it: **Read messages aloud**, **Who may be heard**, **Minimum bits**, **Cooldown** for a viewer's lines, **Maximum characters**, **Censor profanity** and the pronunciation rules.
A command reply set to **Read it aloud** has no viewer role and no bits.
The bot counts it as **Everyone** with 0 bits.
So for a command reply, any **Who may be heard** above **Everyone**, or any **Minimum bits** above 0, stops that reply from being read.

On a **Read aloud (TTS)** step, the **As** field picks whose voice speaks.
Blank, `bot` or `channel` uses the channel voice.
`viewer` or `user` uses the voice of the viewer who triggered the step.
`broadcaster` uses the broadcaster's own saved voice, and a platform user id speaks as that person.
The **Rate** field sets the speed as a percent of normal, from -50% to +50%.

A viewer's own words, such as a reward's typed text, are read in the viewer's own voice.
A viewer picks it in chat with `!voice`, as [Built-in commands](/reference/commands/built-in-commands) shows.
A viewer with no voice of their own gets a voice the first time their words are read.
The bot picks an English voice, or one that speaks many languages, from the channel's provider.
It prefers a voice that matches the viewer's pronouns (he: male, she: female, others: neutral), and picks at random among those.
The viewer keeps that voice.

Some event replies contain both the bot's announcement and the viewer's own message, such as a resubscription message.
The bot can speak those parts in their respective voices within the same reply.
The person who triggered an event does not become the voice of all the bot's narration.

When the bot says a viewer's name, it reads a name such as `smirking_squiggly` as "smirking squiggly".
A number range such as `10-30` stays as written.

## Turn text to speech on or off

1. Open **Text to speech** in the sidebar and choose the **Rules** tab.
2. In **Speaking**, switch **Read messages aloud** on or off.
   The header badge changes to "Text to speech is on" or "Text to speech is off".

When it is off, the bot reads nothing aloud.
A new channel starts with it on.

## Pick the channel voice

1. Open the **Voices** tab.
2. Type in **Search voices**, or choose a language chip: **All**, **English**, **Dutch**, **German**, **French**, **Spanish** or **Other**.
3. Choose the play button on a row to hear a sample of the voice.
4. Click the row to make it the channel voice.
   The **Channel voice** panel shows the voice you picked.
   Picking a voice also sets **Voice provider** to that voice's service.

**Load more voices** at the bottom adds the next part of the list.

Until you pick a voice, the channel follows the platform voice.
The **Channel voice** panel then says "This channel follows the platform voice until you pick one."
To go back to it, switch **Follow the platform voice** on in **Message rules**.

## Speaking settings

The **Speaking** panel on the **Rules** tab sets when the bot reads aloud, and how much.
Each row shows its default under the row.
A change saves when you switch it, choose it, or leave the box.

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Read messages aloud** | Off: the bot stays silent. | On |
| **Voice source** | **Free bot voice**, or **My own key** (see [Use your own voice key](#use-your-own-voice-key)). | **Free bot voice** |
| **Voice provider** | The service that makes the speech. **Free bot voice** offers **Microsoft Edge**. **My own key** offers **Azure** and **ElevenLabs**. | **Microsoft Edge** |
| **Who may be heard** | The lowest role that is read aloud: **Everyone**, **Twitch subscribers**, **VIPs**, **Moderators** or **Broadcaster**. | **Everyone** |
| **Maximum characters** | The longest line, up to 500. | 500 characters |
| **Cooldown** | Seconds a viewer waits between their spoken lines, from 0 to 3600. 0 means none. | 0 seconds |

A bot line longer than **Maximum characters** is cut at the last whole word that fits.
A viewer's own words that are too long are not read at all.
A hosted plan can set a lower limit than **Maximum characters**.
The hosted Free plan allows 200 characters a line.
It also allows 25,000 characters a month on **Free bot voice**; **My own key** is not counted.
A self-hosted bot has no plan limit.

A number above or below the range is set to the nearest allowed value.
For example, 5000 in **Cooldown** becomes 3600.
Only **Minimum bits** on the **Rules** tab can be refused: a value above 1,000,000 shows "The minimum bits is between 0 and 1,000,000." in red.

## Choose who is heard

**Who may be heard** sits in the **Speaking** panel on the **Rules** tab; set it there to the lowest role that is read aloud.
It checks the role of the person whose own words are read.
A command reply has no such person, so it counts as **Everyone**; see [Which voice speaks](#which-voice-speaks).
The **Message rules** panel below it on the same tab decides what else the bot reads aloud.

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Follow the platform voice** | Use the platform voice until you pick your own. | On |
| **Viewers pick their own voice** | Viewers change their voice with `!voice`. Off: moderators only. | On |
| **Censor profanity** | Mask mild swearing before it is spoken. | On |
| **Minimum bits** | The bits a line needs to be read, from 0 to 1,000,000. 0 means none. | 0 |
| **Moderators approve first** | Holds otherwise accepted speech requests in an approval queue instead of playing them immediately. | Off |

**Who may be heard**, **Minimum bits** and **Cooldown** are bypassed when the reward's built-in **Rewards that speak** path reads its submitted text.
A **Read aloud (TTS)** step in that reward's pipeline still uses the normal checks; being triggered by a redemption does not give the step the same bypass.
The viewer already paid for that line with channel points.

When **Censor profanity** removes every word of a line, the bot reads nothing.

### Approval

**Moderators approve first** changes the speech path, including requests for bot-authored replies: an accepted request is held rather than played.
The dashboard has no screen to approve held requests, so leave this switch off when you want speech to play at once.

Turning the switch off lets new requests use the immediate playback path.
It does not itself approve or play requests already held in the queue.

### Reset the settings

**Reset to defaults** sits at the bottom of the **Speaking** and **Message rules** panels.
It puts every setting in both panels back to its default, except **Follow the platform voice**.
The button is grayed out while every setting already has its default.

The note beside it says "Keys, voice and pronunciation stay."
The reset keeps your keys, the channel voice, your pronunciation rules and the viewers' own voices.

## Rewards that speak

The **Rewards that speak** panel on the **Rules** tab lists your channel-point rewards, each with its cost.
Switch a reward on to have the bot read aloud the text the viewer types when they redeem it.
This switch needs the **Editor** role by default.
The line is spoken in the viewer's own voice.

Choose and configure the reward on [Channel-point rewards](/reference/automation/rewards), including **Viewer must type a message** so the viewer can supply the text.
With no rewards, the panel says "No rewards yet. Create a reward first."

## Add a pronunciation rule

A pronunciation rule makes the bot say a replacement whenever a phrase appears in a line.

1. Open the **Pronunciation** tab and choose **Add rule**.
   The **Add a pronunciation rule** dialog opens.
2. Type the phrase in **Phrase**, up to 100 characters.
3. Type what the bot should say in **Say instead**, up to 200 characters.
4. Optional: type the sound of the word in **Exact sound (IPA)**, up to 200 characters.
   IPA is the International Phonetic Alphabet, for example `vjuː` for "Vue".
5. Choose **Match**: **Whole word, any case**, or **Exact text**.
6. Choose **Add rule**.
   The dialog closes and the rule shows in the list.

A voice that reads IPA, such as an Azure voice, says the exact sound.
Every other voice says the text in **Say instead**, so always fill in both.
If the voice service refuses the sound, the bot speaks the line with **Say instead**.
The list shows the sound under the replacement.

**Add rule** stays grayed out until **Phrase** and **Say instead** both hold text.
A second rule with the same phrase and match is refused with "A rule with this phrase and match already exists."

Each row has a play button that reads the phrase in the channel voice, so you hear the rule at work.
The pencil button opens **Edit the pronunciation rule**, where **Save rule** keeps your change.

To delete a rule, choose its trash button.
The **Delete this rule?** dialog opens, and **Delete rule** removes the row from the list.

## Use your own voice key

Your own key lets the bot speak with a paid provider voice, billed to your own provider account.
Get the key from the provider's own site first.

1. Open the **Keys** tab.
2. Paste the key in **Azure speech key** or **ElevenLabs key**.
3. For Azure, type the region of your key in **Azure region**.
4. Choose **Save key**.
   A "The key is stored." message shows, the box empties, and the badge changes to **Key stored**.
5. Open the **Rules** tab and set **Voice source** to **My own key**.
   Then pick **Azure** or **ElevenLabs** in **Voice provider**.

The key is never shown again.
The box then says "A key is stored. Type to replace it."
To replace the key, paste the new one and choose **Save key**.

**Remove key** deletes the stored key, and the badge changes to **No key**.
For Azure, it also clears the region.

## Set how your channel name is spoken

The **Keys** tab also holds **How to say the channel name**.

1. Type the spelling that sounds right, for example with spaces between the parts of the name.
2. Choose **Preview** to hear it in the channel voice.
3. Choose **Save spelling**.
   A "The spelling is saved." message shows.

Leave the box empty to read the name as it is written.

## Read the speech history

The **Spoken history** on the **Overview** tab lists the lines the bot sent to be read aloud, newest first.
Each row shows the text, who asked for it, the voice and how long ago it was sent.
A row is saved before the line reaches OBS, so a row does not prove that the line played.
**Show older messages** at the bottom loads the next 50 lines.

A line spoken before the bot kept the text shows "Spoken before the bot kept the text."
With nothing spoken yet, the panel says "Nothing has been read aloud yet. Messages show here as the bot speaks them."

## Other places that speak

### Coordinate speech with other steps

A reward might lower the music, speak a line, then restore the volume.
By default, a **Read aloud (TTS)** step uses **Mode** `async`: the next step begins after the line is dispatched, while its audio may still be playing.
Choose `sync` when the next step should wait for that line. The bot waits for the synthesized clip's reported duration, capped at 60 seconds, plus a quarter-second startup allowance.
This is a timed wait, rather than confirmation from OBS that playback finished.
A request held for moderator approval does not run this playback wait.
Both modes set `{tts.durationMs}` and `{tts.voiceId}` for later steps.

For a sequence with several timed lines, add **Scene reserve** before its speech.
**Scene reserve** sets `{scene.token}`; put that variable in **Scene token** on each of the sequence's **Read aloud (TTS)** steps.
Use `sync` for lines that should finish before the next step, then add **Scene release** with the same token after the sequence.
Unrelated new speech waits for the reservation to clear. Speech already underway is not cancelled, and the usual TTS permission, bits, length and approval checks still apply.

**Max hold seconds** defaults to 60 and is clamped to 1–120 seconds.
The reservation expires even if the pipeline stops before reaching **Scene release**, so a failed sequence cannot hold other speech indefinitely.
Only one reservation is active per channel: another **Scene reserve** replaces its token, and an old token cannot release the replacement.

### Make speech without playing it

The **Tts synthesize** step makes the speech audio without playing it.
It gives `{tts.audioUrl}` and `{tts.durationMs}` to later steps, for example for a widget.

### Several voices in one line

The **Segments** field of a **Read aloud (TTS)** step speaks one line as a list of blocks.
When it is set, it replaces **Text to read**.
It takes a JSON list, and every value in it accepts [template variables](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates).
The whole list can also come from one variable, such as one a paired program hands over.

A text block has `text`, and optionally `voice`, `rate`, `pitch`, `volume`, `stress` and `breakAfterMs`.
`rate`, `pitch` and `volume` are a percent change from normal, such as `20` or `-10%`.
Each one is limited to -50 to 50; a bigger value counts as 50.
`stress` set to `true` marks the block that carries the stress; `breakAfterMs` is silence after the block, up to 5000 ms.
A pause block has only `pause`, in milliseconds, and adds silence after the block before it.
Blocks go in any order.

```json
[
  { "text": "Welcome back", "voice": "en-US-AriaNeural" },
  { "pause": 400 },
  { "text": "everyone", "stress": true }
]
```

Each voice provider reads the list its own way.
Azure speaks the blocks in one clip, and makes a stressed block slower, higher and louder.
Edge cannot stress a word, so it joins neighboring blocks that sound the same and cuts at a pause.
ElevenLabs speaks a run of blocks with the same voice as one text.
It skips pauses, `breakAfterMs`, `rate`, `pitch`, `volume` and `stress`; only the voice changes are heard.
The step fails when the list is empty or is not valid JSON.
It also fails when a block is not an object, or has neither `text` nor `pause`.
It fails when no block has `text`.
It fails when a `rate`, `pitch` or `volume` is not a number.
A pause block at the start of the list adds no silence.
A [code script](/reference/code/sdk-chat-music-voice) can pass the same blocks.

### Assign a viewer's voice from a pipeline

**Tts set voice** saves the catalog voice selected in **Voice id**, while **Tts clear voice** removes the saved assignment.
These steps are useful when a reward changes a viewer's voice for future messages; they do not change the channel voice.
To change only one spoken line, use the **Voice (optional)** field on that line's **Read aloud (TTS)** step instead.

Leave **Target variable** empty to use the person who triggered the pipeline. This self-service path requires **Read messages aloud** and **Viewers pick their own voice** to be on.
To name another viewer, enter the name of a variable containing their platform user id, such as `target.id`, without braces.
If that variable is missing or empty, the step falls back to the triggering person; make sure a targeted workflow supplies it before running the step.
The named-viewer path does not apply the self-service switches or check a separate invoking role. Limit who can trigger a pipeline that changes someone else's voice.
Clearing an assignment does not pin the viewer to the channel voice: the next viewer-speech request can assign a random voice again, as explained in [Which voice speaks](#which-voice-speaks).

### Commands, scripts and rewards

Text to speech also runs from other places in the bot:

- A command reply with **Read it aloud** switched on, on [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command).
- A **Read aloud (TTS)** step in a [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines).
- A code script, with the calls on [Chat, music and voice](/reference/code/sdk-chat-music-voice).

Each one goes through the same settings as a **Read aloud (TTS)** step, listed in [Which voice speaks](#which-voice-speaks).

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**A reward is redeemed, but nothing is read aloud.**
Check that **Read messages aloud** is on, and that the reward is switched on in **Rewards that speak**.
The viewer must type text when they redeem it.
Check that **TTS caption** is loaded in OBS and its audio reaches the mixer.
If **Moderators approve first** is on, the request is held and the dashboard cannot approve it. Switch it off to play new requests at once.

::

::Troubleshooting

**A viewer's long message is not read at all.**
The message is longer than **Maximum characters**.
A viewer's own words are not cut.
**Maximum characters** can go no higher than 500, so if it is already 500, ask the viewer for a shorter line.
If it is lower, raise it up to 500.
On a hosted plan, the plan's own line limit can also be lower than that.

::
