You set up text to speech on the **Text to speech** page in the sidebar.
Text to speech is how the bot reads a line aloud on your stream.
The page header shows "Text to speech is on" or "Text to speech is off".

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | The **Spoken history** and a short summary of the **Settings**. |
| **Voices** | The voice list, where you pick the channel voice. |
| **Rules** | The **Speaking** settings, the **Message rules** and the **Rewards that speak**. |
| **Pronunciation** | Rules that make the bot say a word another way. |
| **Keys** | Your own voice provider keys, and how your channel name is spoken. |

The speech plays through the speaker overlay.
Add it to OBS from **Required overlays** on the **OBS** page.
[OBS browser source](/reference/overlays/obs-browser-source) shows how.
The **Speaker overlay** row on the **Overview** tab opens that place.

## Who can use this page

A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page and change the settings.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

The **Broadcaster** may also let a **VIP** open the page.
A VIP then sees the settings, but cannot change them.
The **Keys** tab shows only to someone who may change the settings.

## Which voice speaks

A line the bot writes, such as a command reply or an event response, is spoken in the channel voice by default.
A **Read aloud (TTS)** step in a [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines) sets the text it reads.
These settings on this page still apply to it: **Read messages aloud**, **Who may be heard**, **Minimum bits**, **Cooldown** for a viewer's lines, **Maximum characters**, **Censor profanity** and the pronunciation rules.

A viewer's own words, such as a reward's typed text, are read in the viewer's own voice.
A viewer picks it in chat with `!voice`, as [Built-in commands](/reference/commands/built-in-commands) shows.
A viewer with no voice of their own gets a random voice the first time their words are read.
The bot picks an English voice, or one that speaks many languages, from the channel's provider.
The viewer keeps that voice.

## Turn text to speech on or off

1. Open **Text to speech** in the sidebar and choose the **Rules** tab.
2. In **Speaking**, switch **Read messages aloud** on or off.
   The header badge changes to "Text to speech is on" or "Text to speech is off".

When it is off, the bot reads nothing aloud.
A new channel starts with it on.

## Pick the channel voice

1. Open the **Voices** tab.
2. Type in **Search voices**, or choose a language chip: **All**, **English**, **Dutch**, **German**, **French**, **Spanish** or **Other**.
3. Choose the play button on a row to hear a sample of the voice.
4. Click the row to make it the channel voice.
   The **Channel voice** panel shows the voice you picked.

**Load more voices** at the bottom adds the next part of the list.

Until you pick a voice, the channel follows the platform voice.
The **Channel voice** panel then says "This channel follows the platform voice until you pick one."
To go back to it, switch **Follow the platform voice** on in **Message rules**.

## Speaking settings

The **Speaking** panel on the **Rules** tab sets when the bot reads aloud, and how much.
Each row shows its default under the row.
A change saves when you switch it, choose it, or leave the box.

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Read messages aloud** | Off: the bot stays silent. | On |
| **Voice source** | **Free bot voice**, or **My own key** (see [Use your own voice key](#use-your-own-voice-key)). | **Free bot voice** |
| **Voice provider** | The service that makes the speech. **Free bot voice** offers **Microsoft Edge**. **My own key** offers **Azure** and **ElevenLabs**. | **Microsoft Edge** |
| **Who may be heard** | The lowest role that is read aloud: **Everyone**, **Twitch subscribers**, **VIPs**, **Moderators** or **Broadcaster**. | **Everyone** |
| **Maximum characters** | The longest line, up to 500. | 500 characters |
| **Cooldown** | Seconds a viewer waits between their spoken lines, from 0 to 3600. 0 means none. | 0 seconds |

A bot line longer than **Maximum characters** is cut at the last whole word that fits.
A viewer's own words that are too long are not read at all.

A number outside its range is refused, and the row shows the reason in red, for example "The cooldown is between 0 and 3600 seconds."

## Choose who is heard

The **Message rules** panel on the **Rules** tab decides who is heard and what the bot reads aloud.

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Follow the platform voice** | Use the platform voice until you pick your own. | On |
| **Viewers pick their own voice** | Viewers change their voice with `!voice`. Off: moderators only. | On |
| **Censor profanity** | Mask mild swearing before it is spoken. | On |
| **Minimum bits** | The bits a line needs to be read, from 0 to 1,000,000. 0 means none. | 0 |

**Who may be heard**, **Minimum bits** and **Cooldown** do not apply to a channel-point reward.
The viewer already paid for that line with channel points.

When **Censor profanity** removes every word of a line, the bot reads nothing.

### Reset the settings

**Reset to defaults** sits at the bottom of the **Speaking** and **Message rules** panels.
It puts every setting in both panels back to its default, except **Follow the platform voice**.

The note beside it says "Keys, voice and pronunciation stay."
The reset keeps your keys, the channel voice, your pronunciation rules and the viewers' own voices.

## Rewards that speak

The **Rewards that speak** panel on the **Rules** tab lists your channel-point rewards, each with its cost.
Switch a reward on to have the bot read aloud the text the viewer types when they redeem it.
The line is spoken in the viewer's own voice.

A reward is made on [Channel-point rewards](/reference/automation/rewards).
With no rewards, the panel says "No rewards yet. Create a reward first."

## Add a pronunciation rule

A pronunciation rule makes the bot say a replacement whenever a phrase appears in a line.

1. Open the **Pronunciation** tab and choose **Add rule**.
   The **Add a pronunciation rule** dialog opens.
2. Type the phrase in **Phrase**, up to 100 characters.
3. Type what the bot should say in **Say instead**, up to 200 characters.
4. Choose **Match**: **Whole word, any case**, or **Exact text**.
5. Choose **Add rule**.
   The dialog closes and the rule shows in the list.

**Add rule** stays grayed out until **Phrase** and **Say instead** both hold text.
A second rule with the same phrase and match is refused with "A rule with this phrase and match already exists."

Each row has a play button that reads the phrase in the channel voice, so you hear the rule at work.
The pencil button opens **Edit the pronunciation rule**, where **Save rule** keeps your change.

To delete a rule, choose its trash button.
The **Delete this rule?** dialog opens, and **Delete rule** removes the row from the list.

## Use your own voice key

Your own key lets the bot speak with a paid provider voice, billed to your own provider account.
Get the key from the provider's own site first.

1. Open the **Keys** tab.
2. Paste the key in **Azure speech key** or **ElevenLabs key**.
3. For Azure, type the region of your key in **Azure region**.
4. Choose **Save key**.
   A "The key is stored." message shows, the box empties, and the badge changes to **Key stored**.
5. Open the **Rules** tab and set **Voice source** to **My own key**.
   Then pick **Azure** or **ElevenLabs** in **Voice provider**.

The key is never shown again.
The box then says "A key is stored. Type to replace it."
To replace the key, paste the new one and choose **Save key**.

**Remove key** deletes the stored key, and the badge changes to **No key**.
For Azure, it also clears the region.

## Set how your channel name is spoken

The **Keys** tab also holds **How to say the channel name**.

1. Type the spelling that sounds right, for example with spaces between the parts of the name.
2. Choose **Preview** to hear it in the channel voice.
3. Choose **Save spelling**.
   A "The spelling is saved." message shows.

Leave the box empty to read the name as it is written.

## Read the speech history

The **Spoken history** on the **Overview** tab lists the lines the bot read aloud, newest first.
Each row shows the text, who asked for it, the voice and how long ago it played.
**Show older messages** at the bottom loads the next 50 lines.

A line spoken before the bot kept the text shows "Spoken before the bot kept the text."
With nothing spoken yet, the panel says "Nothing has been read aloud yet. Messages show here as the bot speaks them."

## Other places that speak

Text to speech also runs from other places in the bot:

- A command reply with **Read it aloud** switched on, on [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command).
- A speak step in a [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines).
- A code script, with the calls on [Chat, music and voice](/reference/code/sdk-chat-music-voice).

Each one goes through the same settings as a speak step, listed in [Which voice speaks](#which-voice-speaks).

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**A reward is redeemed, but nothing is read aloud.**
Check that **Read messages aloud** is on, and that the reward is switched on in **Rewards that speak**.
The viewer must type text when they redeem it.
Check that the speaker overlay is in OBS.

::

::Troubleshooting

**A viewer's long message is not read at all.**
The message is longer than **Maximum characters**.
A viewer's own words are not cut, so raise the limit or ask for a shorter line.

::
