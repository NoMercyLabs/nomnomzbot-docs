You run NomNomzBot from a dashboard in your browser, at the web address of an installation.
An installation is one running copy of NomNomzBot.
You either sign in to an installation someone already runs, or you install your own.

## The bot, the dashboard and your channel

The bot is the part that stays connected to your channel and answers in chat.
The dashboard is where you set up what it does.
It is the website of your installation, and you sign in to it with your Twitch account.

The dashboard sidebar shows each person only the pages their role may open.
So a moderator sees fewer pages than the broadcaster.

On the sign-in page, the broadcaster chooses **Continue as broadcaster**.
That button sets up the bot on the channel of your Twitch stream, and the bot joins its chat.
One channel can hold more than one platform connection.
You add Kick and YouTube to it from the **Connections** panel on the dashboard's **Home** page, as [Connect platforms](/reference/getting-started/connect-platforms) shows.

To type in chat, the bot needs an account.
It uses your channel's own bot account when you connected one, and otherwise the shared bot account when the installation has one connected.
Without either, the bot sends its replies from your own Twitch account, so they show in chat under your name.
So you can start without a separate bot account, and [choose one later](/reference/getting-started/bot-account).

## Choose how to start

If someone already runs NomNomzBot for your community, ask them for its address and whether it runs the full or the lite setup.
The setup decides whose channel you get.
On full, each streamer who chooses **Continue as broadcaster** gets a channel of their own.
On lite, the installation serves one channel, and everyone who signs in after the first streamer lands on that streamer's channel.
If you cannot ask before you sign in, you find out from the channel the dashboard opens on.

If nobody runs one for you, install your own with Docker.
You need Docker and a Twitch app.
You either register your own app, or use the shared NomNomzBot app, as [First-run setup](/reference/self-hosting/first-run-setup#1-twitch-app) shows.
For your own channel, lite is enough.
[Install with Docker](/reference/self-hosting/install-with-docker) helps you pick a setup and walks you through it.

A moderator does not install a bot of their own.
Ask the broadcaster, or whoever runs the channel's installation, for its address.
The broadcaster signs in there first with **Continue as broadcaster**.
That sign-in copies the channel's moderator list from Twitch into the installation.
You are a moderator in the dashboard when you are on that list.

Then sign in with **Continue as moderator**.
Pick the channel in the channel switcher at the top of the sidebar, under **Channels you moderate**.

## Your first task

For a streamer, a chat command is the quickest thing to get working.
Here you make a `schedule` command that replies with your stream times.

1. Get the address of an installation from whoever runs it, or install your own with [Install with Docker](/reference/self-hosting/install-with-docker).
2. Open the address, choose **Continue as broadcaster**, and approve the sign-in on Twitch, as [Sign in](/reference/getting-started/sign-in) shows.
   The dashboard opens on your channel, and the bot joins its chat.
   On a lite installation that already serves another streamer, it opens on that streamer's channel instead, and you need another installation.
3. Open **Commands** in the **Build** group of the dashboard sidebar, and make a command named `schedule` whose reply is your stream times, as [Your first command](/reference/getting-started/first-command) shows.
4. In your Twitch chat, type `!schedule` and press Enter.
   The bot answers with your stream times.
   While you are offline, it answers only you and your moderators, unless you turn on **Keep commands and rewards on while offline**.
   Without a bot account, the answer shows under your own Twitch name, and that is the bot answering.

## Using NomNomzBot

You may use, change and share NomNomzBot for free, for yourself, your friends and your community, but you may not earn money with it, such as by selling it or by running paid hosting on it.
