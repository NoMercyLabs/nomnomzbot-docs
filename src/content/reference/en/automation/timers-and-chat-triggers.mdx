---
title: Timers and chat triggers
description: Make a timer that posts on a schedule and a chat trigger that answers a phrase, and set when each fires.
---

A timer posts in chat on its own, every few minutes.
A chat trigger answers when someone in chat types certain words.
You make both on the **Triggers and timers** page in the sidebar.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Timers** | Lines the bot posts on a schedule. |
| **Chat triggers** | Replies to words in ordinary chat lines. |

A chat trigger is not a command.
A command starts with the prefix, such as `!hello`, and is made on [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command).

## Who can use this page

A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page and change timers and chat triggers.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

The **Broadcaster** may also let a **VIP** open each tab.
A VIP then sees the list only, and the editor does not open.
For a VIP, the **New timer** and **New trigger** buttons show "You can view this, but your role cannot change it."

## Make a timer

1. Open **Triggers and timers** in the sidebar, then the **Timers** tab.
2. Choose **New timer**.
   The **New timer** dialog opens.
3. Type a **Name**, for example `Follow reminder`.
4. Under **Reaction**, choose **Simple reply** or **Pipeline** (see the table below).
5. Set **Minutes between posts**.
   The default is 30 minutes.
6. Choose **Create timer**.
   The dialog closes and the new timer shows in the list, switched on.

| **Reaction** | The field to fill in |
| --- | --- |
| **Simple reply** | **Messages**: one line per message. Blank lines are dropped. |
| **Pipeline** | **Pipeline**: a command, a [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines) or an action for the timer to run. |

A message can hold template helpers, such as `{channel}`.
They are explained on [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates).

When the list is empty, the tab shows "No timers yet."
Its **Make the first timer** button opens the same dialog.

The dialog stops with a reason in red when a field is wrong:

| Problem | What the dialog shows |
| --- | --- |
| No name | "Give the timer a name." |
| **Simple reply** with no message | "Write at least one message." |
| **Pipeline** with nothing picked | "Pick the command, action or pipeline the timer runs." |
| Minutes outside the limits | "Use a whole number from 1 to 1440 minutes." |

## When a timer posts

The bot checks its timers about every 30 seconds.
A timer posts when both of these are true:

- **Minutes between posts** have passed since its last post.
- Chat has at least **Chat lines needed** new lines since its last post.

A timer that has never posted is due at once.
A new timer starts with **Chat lines needed** at 0, so it posts at the next check, within about 30 seconds.

A copy made with **Duplicate** keeps the **Chat lines needed** of the timer it copies.
Once you switch the copy on, it posts at the next check after chat has had that many lines since the bot began watching your channel.
After the first post, it waits **Minutes between posts** between posts.

When **Chat lines needed** is 0, a timer posts on time alone.
The bot posts only in a channel it is connected to.

A timer with several messages posts them in turn, one per post.
After the last message, it starts again at the first.
A timer set to **Pipeline** has no messages, so it runs its command, pipeline or action each time.

A message line goes to Twitch chat in the channel's one bot voice.
When a line cannot be sent, the timer tries the same line again at the next check.

## Edit a timer

Choose a timer in the list to open it in the editor.
The list shows each timer's name and how often it posts.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Enabled** | Switches the timer on or off at once. A timer that is off stays in the list, marked **Off**, and posts nothing. |
| **Name** | The timer's name in the list. |
| **Reaction** | The messages, or what the timer runs. |
| **Minutes between posts** | A whole number from 1 to 1440. |
| **Chat lines needed** | A whole number from 0 to 10000. |
| **Post once** | The timer posts one time and then switches itself off. |
| **Read it aloud** | Each post also plays as speech on your stream. |

The footer counts your unsaved changes.
Choose **Save changes** to keep them, or **Discard** to go back to the saved timer.
After a save, the count goes away and the list shows the new values.

**Duplicate** makes a copy named after the timer with "copy" added, such as `Follow reminder copy`.
The copy is switched off, and it opens in the editor.

## Delete a timer

1. Open the timer and choose **Delete**.
   A box asks "Delete *name*?"
2. Choose **Delete timer**.

The timer is removed and stops posting.
The editor closes and shows "Pick a timer to edit it."

## Make a chat trigger

1. Open **Triggers and timers** in the sidebar, then the **Chat triggers** tab.
2. Choose **New trigger**.
   The **New chat trigger** dialog opens.
3. Type the **Words to watch for**, for example `good morning`.
4. Type the **Reply**, the line the bot sends.
   The reply may hold template helpers from [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates).
5. Choose **Create trigger**.
   The dialog closes and the new trigger shows in the list, switched on.

The dialog shows "Give the trigger some words to watch for." or "Write the reply." when a field is empty.
When the list is empty, the tab shows "No chat triggers yet." with a **Make the first trigger** button.

A new trigger starts with these settings, which you change in the editor:

| Setting | Starts at |
| --- | --- |
| **How chat must match** | **Contains the words** |
| **Match capital letters** | Off |
| **Seconds between replies** | 30 |
| **Who can set it off** | **Everyone** |

## How chat must match

**How chat must match** decides how the words compare to a chat line.

| Choice | The trigger fires when the chat line |
| --- | --- |
| **Contains the words** | Holds the words anywhere, even inside a longer word. |
| **Is exactly the words** | Is the words and nothing else. |
| **Starts with the words** | Begins with the words. |
| **Matches a pattern** | Matches a regular expression, a pattern language for text. |

With **Contains the words**, the words `hi` also match `this`.
Use **Is exactly the words** or **Matches a pattern** when that is too wide.

**Match capital letters** off means `Hello` and `hello` are the same.
A pattern that does not work as a regular expression is refused when you save.

## When a chat trigger fires

The bot checks chat triggers only on ordinary chat lines:

- A line that starts with the command prefix, or that runs a command, never sets off a chat trigger.
- A line that plays a sound from the soundboard never sets off a chat trigger.

One chat line sets off one chat trigger at most.
The trigger then checks **Who can set it off** and **Seconds between replies**.

**Who can set it off** is the lowest role that may fire the trigger.
The choices run from **Everyone** up to **Broadcaster**, as on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

**Seconds between replies** holds the whole channel.
After the trigger answers, it stays quiet for that many seconds.
The broadcaster and moderators are not held back by it.

The bot keeps the value between 0 and 86400 seconds, one day.

## Edit a chat trigger

Choose a trigger in the list to open it in the editor.
The list shows each trigger's words and its match choice.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Enabled** | Switches the trigger on or off at once. A trigger that is off stays in the list, marked **Off**, and answers nothing. |
| **Words to watch for** | The words the bot looks for. |
| **How chat must match** | See [How chat must match](#how-chat-must-match). |
| **Match capital letters** | Makes `Hello` and `hello` different. |
| **Reaction** | **Simple reply** sends the **Reply**. **Pipeline** runs a command, a pipeline or an action instead. |
| **Seconds between replies** | The wait before the trigger answers again. |
| **Who can set it off** | The lowest role that may fire it. |

The reply goes to Twitch chat in the channel's one bot voice.
Choose **Save changes** to keep your edits, or **Discard** to go back.
After a save, the unsaved count goes away and the list shows the new words.

## Delete a chat trigger

1. Open the trigger and choose **Delete**.
   A box asks "Delete *words*?"
2. Choose **Delete trigger**.

The trigger is removed for good and stops answering.
The editor closes and shows "Pick a trigger to edit it."

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**A timer never posts.**
Check these, in order:

1. **Enabled**. A timer marked **Off** posts nothing, and **Post once** switches it off after one post.
2. **Chat lines needed**. A quiet chat holds the timer until enough new lines arrive.
3. **Minutes between posts**. After a post, the next one can come up to 30 seconds later than the number you set.

**A chat trigger does not answer.**
Check these, in order:

1. **Enabled**. A trigger marked **Off** answers nothing.
2. The chat line. A line that starts with the command prefix never sets off a trigger.
3. **Who can set it off**. The viewer's role must be at least the role chosen.
4. **Seconds between replies**. The trigger stays quiet after an answer.

::
