---
title: Timers and chat triggers
description: Make a timer that posts on a schedule and a chat trigger that answers a phrase, and set when each fires.
---

Timers and chat triggers let the bot handle messages you would otherwise repeat during a stream.
A timer can remind viewers to take a break or explain a regular channel activity; a chat trigger can answer a phrase as it comes up in conversation.
You manage both on **Triggers and timers** in the sidebar.

The difference is what starts the response.
A timer waits for time and, optionally, enough chat activity.
A chat trigger watches ordinary messages for the words you choose.
Both can send a reply or run a command, action or [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines), so you can start with a message and add automation later.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Timers** | Lines the bot posts on a schedule. |
| **Chat triggers** | Replies to words in ordinary chat lines. |

A chat trigger is not a command.
A command starts with the prefix, such as `!hello`, and is made on [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command).

## Who can use this page

A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page and change timers and chat triggers.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

A **VIP** can only view these tabs, and only when the **Broadcaster** allows it.
See [What a VIP sees](#what-a-vip-sees).

## Make a timer

For a first timer, make a reminder that rotates through two useful messages.
Use a name such as `Break reminder`, keep **Reaction** on **Simple reply**, and enter one message on each line:

```text
Take a moment to stretch and get some water.
New here? You are welcome to join the conversation or just watch.
```

The bot posts one line each time the timer fires, then moves to the next.
This lets one timer vary its reminder without posting several messages at once.

A timer posts only while your channel is live.
Its first post comes one **Minutes between posts** after you go live.

1. Open **Triggers and timers** in the sidebar, then the **Timers** tab.
2. Choose **New timer**.
   The **New timer** dialog opens.
3. Type a **Name**, for example `Break reminder`.
4. Under **Reaction**, choose **Simple reply** or **Pipeline** (see the table below).
5. For a simple reply, type your lines in **Messages**, one on each line.
6. Under **Send the reply to**, keep **Twitch chat** or choose another place.
7. Set **Minutes between posts**.
   The default is 30 minutes.
8. Choose **Create timer**.
   The dialog closes and the new timer shows in the list, switched on.

| **Reaction** | The field to fill in |
| --- | --- |
| **Simple reply** | **Messages**: one line per message. Blank lines are dropped. |
| **Pipeline** | **Pipeline**: a command, a [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines) or an action for the timer to run. |

A message can hold template helpers, such as `{channel}`.
They are explained on [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates).

When the list is empty, the tab shows "No timers yet."
Its **Make the first timer** button opens the same dialog.

The dialog stops with a reason in red when a field is wrong:

| Problem | What the dialog shows |
| --- | --- |
| No name | "Give the timer a name." |
| **Simple reply** with no message | "Write at least one message." |
| **Pipeline** with nothing picked | "Pick the command, action or pipeline the timer runs." |
| Minutes outside the limits | "Use a whole number from 1 to 1440 minutes." |

## When a timer posts

Timers run only while your channel is live.
While you are offline, the bot checks no timer and posts nothing.

While you are live, the bot checks its timers about every 30 seconds.
A timer posts when all of these are true:

- **Minutes between posts** have passed since the later of its last post and the moment your stream went live.
- Chat has at least **Chat lines needed** new lines since its last post.

Going live starts a fresh time count: the first line of a stream waits one full **Minutes between posts**.
The **Chat lines needed** count does not start again.
Chat lines sent since the timer's last post still count, including lines sent while you were offline.
A new timer starts with **Chat lines needed** at 0, so once the stream has been live that long, it posts at the next check, within about 30 seconds.

A copy made with **Duplicate** keeps the **Chat lines needed** of the timer it copies.
Switch the copy on.

The copy posts at the first check while you are live, once two things are true.
First, one **Minutes between posts** has passed since the stream went live.
Second, chat has had as many lines as **Chat lines needed** says, counted since the bot began watching your channel.
After the first post, it waits **Minutes between posts** between posts.

When **Chat lines needed** is 0, a timer posts on time alone.
The bot posts only in a channel it is connected to.

A timer with several messages posts them in turn, one per post.
After the last message, it starts again at the first.
A timer set to **Pipeline** runs its command, pipeline or action each time instead of posting a line.
The run gets `{timer.name}`, and `{timer.message}` when the timer has lines.
A run that is skipped or fails still counts as a post, so the timer waits a full **Minutes between posts** before it tries again.

A simple reply goes to Twitch chat by default.
**Send the reply to** can send it to **Chat overlay**, **Both** or **Nowhere** instead; the shared delivery and appearance controls are explained on [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command).
When the requested delivery fails, the timer tries the same line again at the next check.

If the stream ends during a check, the timer posts nothing.

## Edit a timer

Choose a timer in the list to open it in the editor.
The list shows each timer's name and how often it posts, such as "Every 30 minutes" with the message count, or "Once".
The **Search timers** field narrows the list; when nothing fits, it shows "No timer matches your search."
You can also pause and resume a timer from the quick actions on Home. A paused timer stays saved and does not fire.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Enabled** | Switches the timer on or off at once. A timer that is off stays in the list, marked **Off**, and posts nothing. |
| **Name** | The timer's name in the list. |
| **Reaction** | The messages, or what the timer runs. |
| **Send the reply to** | Where a simple reply appears: **Twitch chat**, **Chat overlay**, **Both** or **Nowhere**. |
| **Minutes between posts** | A whole number from 1 to 1440. |
| **Chat lines needed** | A whole number from 0 to 10000. |
| **Post once** | The timer posts one time and then switches itself off. |
| **Read it aloud** | Speaks the reply after a successful chat delivery. An overlay-only reply or **Nowhere** does not trigger speech. |

The footer counts your unsaved changes.
Choose **Save changes** to keep them, or **Discard** to go back to the saved timer.
After a save, the count goes away and the list shows the new values.

**Duplicate** makes a copy named after the timer with "copy" added, such as `Break reminder copy`.
The copy is switched off, and it opens in the editor.
It copies the saved timer, so changes you have not saved are not in the copy.
Save first if you want them in the copy.

## Delete a timer

1. Open the timer and choose **Delete**.
   A box asks "Delete *name*?"
2. Choose **Delete timer**.

The timer is removed and stops posting.
The editor closes and shows "Pick a timer to edit it."

## Make a chat trigger

For example, you could welcome a morning greeting without asking viewers to remember a command.
Set **Words to watch for** to `good morning` and **Reply** to `Good morning! Settle in and enjoy the stream.`
With the default matching mode, a message such as `good morning everyone` starts the reply too.

1. Open **Triggers and timers** in the sidebar, then the **Chat triggers** tab.
2. Choose **New trigger**.
   The **New chat trigger** dialog opens.
3. Type the **Words to watch for**, for example `good morning`.
4. Type the **Reply**, the line the bot sends, up to 500 characters.
   The reply may hold template helpers from [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates).
5. Under **Send the reply to**, keep **Twitch chat** or choose another place.
6. Choose **Create trigger**.
   The dialog closes and the new trigger shows in the list, switched on.

The dialog shows "Give the trigger some words to watch for." or "Write the reply." when a field is empty.
When the list is empty, the tab shows "No chat triggers yet." with a **Make the first trigger** button.

A new trigger starts with these settings, which you change in the editor:

| Setting | Starts at |
| --- | --- |
| **How chat must match** | **Contains the words** |
| **Match capital letters** | Off |
| **Seconds between replies** | 30 |
| **Who can set it off** | **Everyone** |

## How chat must match

**How chat must match** decides how the words compare to a chat line.

| Choice | The trigger fires when the chat line |
| --- | --- |
| **Contains the words** | Holds the words anywhere, even inside a longer word. |
| **Is exactly the words** | Is the words and nothing else. |
| **Starts with the words** | Begins with the words. |
| **Matches a pattern** | Matches a regular expression, a pattern language for text. |

With **Contains the words**, the words `hi` also match `this`.
Use **Is exactly the words** or **Matches a pattern** when that is too wide.

**Match capital letters** off means `Hello` and `hello` are the same.
A pattern that does not work as a regular expression is refused when you save.

## When a chat trigger fires

The bot checks chat triggers only on ordinary chat lines:

- A line that starts with the command prefix, or that runs a command, never sets off a chat trigger.
- A line that is exactly a soundboard word never sets off a chat trigger, even when the sound does not play, for example on its cooldown.
- A viewer the bot has muted or shadowbanned never sets off a chat trigger.
- While a chat poll is open, a line that is only an option number is a vote and sets off no trigger.

One chat line sets off one chat trigger at most.
The bot looks for the first trigger whose words match and whose **Who can set it off** the viewer reaches.
A trigger the viewer's role does not reach is passed over, and the next matching trigger can answer.
If the trigger that is found is on its **Seconds between replies** cooldown, no other trigger answers that line.

**Who can set it off** is the lowest role that may fire the trigger.
The choices run from **Everyone** up to **Broadcaster**, as on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

**Seconds between replies** holds the whole channel.
After the trigger answers, it stays quiet for that many seconds.
The broadcaster and moderators are not held back by it.

The bot keeps the value between 0 and 86400 seconds, one day.

## Edit a chat trigger

Choose a trigger in the list to open it in the editor.
The list shows each trigger's words and its match choice.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Enabled** | Switches the trigger on or off at once. A trigger that is off stays in the list, marked **Off**, and answers nothing. |
| **Words to watch for** | The words the bot looks for. |
| **How chat must match** | See [How chat must match](#how-chat-must-match). |
| **Match capital letters** | Makes `Hello` and `hello` different. |
| **Reaction** | **Simple reply** sends the **Reply**, up to 500 characters. **Pipeline** runs a command, a pipeline or an action instead. |
| **Send the reply to** | Where a simple reply appears; the default is **Twitch chat**. |
| **Seconds between replies** | The wait before the trigger answers again. |
| **Who can set it off** | The lowest role that may fire it. |

The default reply appears in Twitch chat from the channel's bot account.
To use the trigger for an on-stream response, select **Chat overlay** or **Both** and keep that overlay loaded in OBS.
Choose **Save changes** to keep your edits, or **Discard** to go back.
After a save, the unsaved count goes away and the list shows the new words.

## Delete a chat trigger

1. Open the trigger and choose **Delete**.
   A box asks "Delete *words*?"
2. Choose **Delete trigger**.

The trigger is removed for good and stops answering.
The editor closes and shows "Pick a trigger to edit it."

## What a VIP sees

The **Broadcaster** may let a **VIP** open each tab.
A VIP opens the page only when the timers view is opened to VIPs.
To show the **Chat triggers** tab to a VIP, open both the timers view and the chat triggers view to VIPs.
To open a view to VIPs, go to the **Permissions** settings page, open the **Actions** tab and use **Search actions** to find **See timers** or **See chat triggers**.
Choose **VIP** in the action's picker; both actions start at **Moderator**.
The steps are on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions#set-the-lowest-role-for-an-action).
A VIP then sees the list only, and the editor does not open.
For a VIP, the **New timer** and **New trigger** buttons show "You can view this, but your role cannot change it."

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**A timer never posts.**
Check these, in order:

1. **Enabled**. A timer marked **Off** posts nothing, and **Post once** switches it off after one post.
2. Your stream. A timer posts only while the channel is live, and the first post comes one **Minutes between posts** after you go live.
3. **Chat lines needed**. A quiet chat holds the timer until enough new lines arrive.
4. **Minutes between posts**. After a post, the next one can come up to 30 seconds later than the number you set.

**A chat trigger does not answer.**
Check these, in order:

1. **Enabled**. A trigger marked **Off** answers nothing.
2. The chat line. A line that starts with the command prefix, or that is exactly a soundboard word, never sets off a trigger.
3. **Who can set it off**. The viewer's role must be at least the role chosen.
4. **Seconds between replies**. The trigger stays quiet after an answer.
5. The viewer. A viewer the bot has muted or shadowbanned sets off no trigger.
6. A poll. While a chat poll is open, a line that is only an option number is a vote, not a trigger.

::
