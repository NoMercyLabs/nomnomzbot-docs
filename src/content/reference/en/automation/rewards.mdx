title: Channel-point rewards
description: Edit, copy, switch off and delete channel-point rewards, choose what the bot does when a viewer buys one, and work the queue of waiting requests.

A channel-point reward can run a bot action when a viewer buys it, such as reading the viewer's message aloud or playing a sound.
You manage the bot's reaction and the requests waiting for attention on **Channel points**, in the **Loyalty** group of the sidebar.

The two panels serve different jobs.
**Rewards** defines what viewers can request and what the bot does; **Pending rewards** is the list you work through during the stream.
Twitch channel points are separate from the bot's own [channel currency](/reference/economy/currency-and-balances).

## Start with a reward you can manage

Before trying this, check [what the bot needs from Twitch](#what-the-bot-needs-from-twitch) and [who can use this page](#who-can-use-this-page).
Changing a reward requires the **Editor** or **Broadcaster** role by default, and the reward must be available on Twitch before you can redeem it.

Rewards get into the **Rewards** list when you make them on Twitch: the bot imports them from your channel.
A reward made on Twitch is imported as one another app made, so the bot cannot change it, but it can copy it.
The bot imports your Twitch rewards when you open the list, at most once every six hours, so a new reward can take up to about six hours to show up.
The first reward you can change is therefore a copy:

1. Make the reward on Twitch, and wait for it to appear in **Rewards**.
2. Choose **Edit** on its row, then choose **Duplicate**.
   The copy belongs to the bot, and it starts switched off.
3. Choose **Edit** on the copy.
   For a simple first reaction, select **Simple reply**, enter `Thanks for redeeming this reward!` in **Chat reply**, and leave **Send the reply to** on **Twitch chat**.
4. Choose **Save**, switch the copy on, then redeem it from Twitch chat to check that the bot posts the reply.

To make more rewards, use **Duplicate** on any reward, change the copy's name and settings, then switch it on when it is ready.
A **Not on Twitch** badge means viewers cannot redeem that reward yet.

For a reward that reads the viewer's own message, enable **Viewer must type a message**, then switch the reward on in **Rewards that speak** on [Text to speech](/reference/audio/text-to-speech).
For a sound or a series of actions, choose **Pipeline** and pick a saved [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines), a command or an action.
You can also choose **Create a new pipeline** to make an empty one named after the reward, then **Open in the pipeline editor** to build it.
These approaches make the redemption the trigger; you do not need a separate chat command.

## What the bot needs from Twitch

Channel-point rewards exist only on Twitch.
The channel must be connected to Twitch, as [Connect platforms](/reference/getting-started/connect-platforms) explains.

Twitch gives channel points only to an Affiliate or Partner channel, a status that Twitch itself grants.
For any other channel, the page shows "Channel points need Twitch Affiliate or Partner. These rewards are saved here and go live on Twitch when the channel is affiliate."
Then only the **Rewards** panel shows, and its on and off switches cannot be used.

## Who can use this page

A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page and read it.
The **Broadcaster** may also let a **VIP** open it, on the **Permissions** settings page.

An **Editor** or the **Broadcaster** may change the reward definitions by default.
A **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may complete or reject pending requests; those are separate permissions from editing rewards.
Someone with read access alone sees the lists without the relevant switches, **Edit** button or queue actions.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

## Read the reward list

The **Rewards** panel lists every reward of the channel, with the count at the top, for example "4 rewards".
When there is none, it shows "This channel has no rewards yet."
Rewards arrive from Twitch: make the reward there first.

Each row shows the reward's name, its cost in points, an on and off switch and an **Edit** button.
The line under the name shows the reward's pipeline or its reply.
The dashboard uses "redeem" for spending Twitch points on a reward.

| Line under the name | What the bot does when a viewer buys the reward |
| --- | --- |
| A pipeline name | Runs that [pipeline](/reference/commands/pipelines). |
| A line of text | Sends that chat reply. |
| **Only lands in the queue** | See the text under this table. |

**Only lands in the queue** shows for every reward that has no saved pipeline and no reply text.
A command or an action picked under **Pipeline** is not a saved pipeline, so such a reward shows this line too.

With a command or an action picked, the bot runs it.
With nothing picked, the bot sends the channel's **Reward redeemed** line from [Event responses](/reference/automation/event-responses), until you save the reward once in the **Edit** dialog.
After that save, an empty **Chat reply** sends nothing.

A row can also carry a badge.

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Not on Twitch** | The reward is saved in the bot, but it does not exist on Twitch. Viewers cannot buy it. |
| **Paused** | The reward shows on Twitch, but viewers cannot buy it now. |

## Edit a reward

1. Choose **Edit** on the reward's row.
   The **Edit** dialog opens with the reward's name in its title: "Changes go to Twitch when you save."
2. Change the fields you want (see the table below).
3. Choose **Save**.
   The dialog closes and the row shows the new values.

If the name is empty, the dialog shows "Give the reward a name." and saves nothing.
If Twitch or the bot refuses the change, the dialog stays open with the reason.
The list shows the new value at once.
If Twitch refuses it, the list goes back to the value it had before.

| Field | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | "The name viewers see in the channel points list." Up to 45 characters. |
| **Cost in points** | "How many channel points one redeem costs." |
| **Description** | "Viewers see this under the reward on Twitch." Up to 200 characters. |
| **Color** | The reward's color on Twitch, as a hex code such as `#9146FF`. An empty box keeps the color it has. |
| **Viewer must type a message** | "The viewer types a message with each redeem." |
| **Enabled** | At the top of the dialog. "Viewers can redeem it only while it is on." |
| **Paused** | At the top of the dialog. "A paused reward stays visible but viewers cannot redeem it." |

### Choose what the reward does

The **Reaction** group in the dialog has two choices.

| Choice | What you fill in |
| --- | --- |
| **Simple reply** | **Chat reply**: "What the bot says in chat when someone redeems it." Up to 500 characters. **Send the reply to** chooses **Twitch chat**, **Chat overlay**, **Both** or **Nowhere**. |
| **Pipeline** | **Pipeline**: the thing to run, which is a saved pipeline, a command or an action. Until you pick one, the dialog shows "This answers nothing until you pick a pipeline. The pipeline has its own reply step." |

The **Reaction** setting chooses either the simple reply or the selected command, pipeline or action.
When you save, the unused reaction is cleared.
Reading the viewer's text through **Rewards that speak** and starting **Timer in seconds** are separate behaviors, so they can accompany the reaction.

The reply is a line in the channel's one bot voice.
It can use helpers in braces, listed on [Variables and templates](/reference/commands/variables-and-templates).

**Send the reply to** works as it does for a command, on [Create a command](/reference/commands/create-a-command).

### Set limits

The **Limits** group is collapsed each time the dialog opens.
Its header shows the limits that are set, and **Paused** when the reward is paused, for example "5 per stream · 30s cooldown".
With none of these, it shows "No limits".

| Field | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Limit per stream** | "Most redeems of this reward in one stream." |
| **Limit per viewer per stream** | "Most redeems per viewer in one stream." |
| **Cooldown in seconds** | "Seconds to wait between any two redeems." |
| **Timer in seconds** | A countdown that starts when a viewer buys the reward. The longest timer is 86,400 seconds (24 hours); a longer value is cut to that. |

An empty box keeps the limit the reward has now. Type 0 to clear a limit.
For a **Not on Twitch** reward, **Color** and the per-stream, per-viewer and cooldown limits do not save. The timer and the other fields do.

<details>
<summary>A reward another app made</summary>

Twitch lets only the app that made a reward change it.
For such a reward, the dialog shows "Another app made this reward, so Twitch only lets that app change it."
Its fields and its switch in the list are off.

**Delete** and **Duplicate** still work for it.
**Delete** removes it only from the bot. It stays on Twitch, and it does not come back in the list when the bot imports your Twitch rewards again.
**Duplicate** makes a copy that the bot owns and can change.

</details>

## Switch a reward on or off

Each row in the list has a switch.
It sets the same thing as **Enabled** in the dialog, and it saves at once.
For a reward that is on Twitch, the switch changes it on Twitch too; a **Not on Twitch** reward changes only in the bot.

**Paused** is a different setting: the reward stays on, and Twitch shows it but lets no viewer buy it.

A reward that is off cannot be bought, and the bot runs none of its reply, pipeline or timer.

## Copy a reward

1. Choose **Edit** on the reward's row.
2. Choose **Duplicate** at the bottom of the dialog.
   The dialog closes, and a copy named after the reward with " copy" at the end joins the list.

The copy has the saved settings of the reward.
It starts switched off, so viewers cannot buy it until you switch it on.

Twitch does not allow two rewards with the same name.
If a reward with the copy's name exists, the dialog stays open with the reason.

## Delete a reward

1. Choose **Edit** on the reward's row.
2. Choose **Delete** at the bottom of the dialog.
   The dialog asks "Delete" and the reward's name: "The reward is removed here and on Twitch. This cannot be undone." (For a reward another app made, it is removed only here.)
   Under it, a line names what the reward takes with it, or says "No viewer has redeemed this reward."
3. Choose **Delete reward**.
   The dialog closes and the reward leaves the list.

## Work the queue

The **Pending rewards** panel lists the requests that wait for you, with the count of the open list at the top, for example "3 waiting".
On the left, **All requests** and each reward show their own count.
Choose one to see only its requests; the page remembers your choice.

Each request shows the reward, the time since the viewer bought it, the viewer, the cost and the viewer's message.
When nothing waits, the panel shows "No requests are waiting."

| Button | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Complete** (check mark on the row) | The request is marked done and leaves the list. |
| **Reject** (cross on the row) | A dialog asks you to confirm: "Twitch cancels each request and gives the points back." Its **Reject 1** button, named for the count, confirms it, and the request leaves the list. Reject only changes the request's status: the reaction that already ran stays done, and so does any timer that already started. |

To act on many requests at once, tick their boxes, or tick **Select all requests**.
The buttons at the bottom, such as **Complete 3** and **Reject 3**, act on the ticked requests.
With nothing ticked, they act on every request in the open list.

A bulk **Complete** or **Reject** can partly succeed.
If some fail, they stay in the list, and the list reloads so you can see which ones are left.

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**A viewer buys the reward and the bot does nothing.**
Check these, in order:

1. The switch on the reward's row. A reward that is off runs nothing.
2. The **Reaction** in the **Edit** dialog. A **Pipeline** with nothing picked answers nothing. A saved empty **Chat reply** sends nothing: type a reply and choose **Save**.
3. **Send the reply to**. **Chat overlay** and **Nowhere** send nothing to Twitch chat.

::
