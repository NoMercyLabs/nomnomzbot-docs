Analytics helps you look back on a stream or a longer period and understand how your community took part.
Use **Overview** for the broad picture, **Streams** to compare individual broadcasts, **Chat** to see when conversation was busiest, and **YouTube** for your YouTube channel's numbers.
**Recaps** lets you save a stream's end-credit picture to share afterward.

Open **Analytics** in the **Live** group of the sidebar.
Its numbers come from the channel activity the bot recorded, so read missing data as a gap in those records rather than as a measured zero.

## Who can use this page

By default, a **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page.
The sidebar shows **Analytics** only to these roles.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

## Pick a range

1. Open **Analytics** in the sidebar.
   The **Overview** tab opens.
2. Choose a value in **Range** at the top of the page.
   On a narrow screen, **Range** sits in the bar at the bottom of the screen.
   The **Overview**, **Streams**, **Chat** and **YouTube** tabs show that range.

| **Range** | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| **30 days** | The last 30 days. This is the default. |
| **90 days** | The last 90 days. |
| **12 months** | The last 365 days. |

The browser remembers the range.
The tab is part of the page address, so a link or the back button opens the same tab.
The **Recaps** tab does not use the range.

## Read the overview

The **Overview** tab starts with a row of numbers for the range.

| Number | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **Streams** | The streams in the range, with the total time live under it. |
| **Chat lines** | The chat lines, with the lines per live hour under it. |
| **New follows** | The new follows. |
| **Twitch subs** | New, renewed and gifted subs. |
| **Bits cheered** | The Bits cheered. |
| **Peak viewers** | The highest viewer count. |

A number the bot has no record of shows "No data".

Below the numbers are three panels:

- **Follows** shows the new follows per day as a bar chart.
- **Chatters** shows how many different people chatted, per day.
- **Highlights** shows the average stream length, the top category, and how many commands, rewards and song requests chat used.

With **12 months**, both charts show one bar per month.

## Read every stream

The **Streams** tab lists **Every stream** in the range.
The line above the table gives the average stream length and the day with the busiest chat.

When comparing broadcasts, consider duration as well as message totals.
A longer stream gives viewers more time to chat, so the total alone does not explain whether conversation was more active.
The overview's chat-lines-per-live-hour figure provides another way to read that activity.

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Started** | The day and time the stream started. |
| **Length** | How long the stream ran. |
| **Category** | The category of the stream. |
| **Chat lines** | The chat lines in the stream. |
| **Chatters** | The different people who chatted. |
| **New follows** | The follows during the stream. |
| **Peak viewers** | The highest viewer count, or "Not known". |

On a narrow screen the table hides **Category**, **Chatters**, **New follows** and **Peak viewers**.
The category, chatters and new follows then show under the start time.
Peak viewers is not shown.

## See when chat is busy

The **Chat** tab has two panels:

- **When chat is busy** is a grid of weekdays and hours. Its legend runs from **Fewer** to **More** chat lines.
- **Busy days and hours** shows the chat lines **By day of the week** and the **Busiest hours**.

The line under **When chat is busy** names the time zone of the grid, for example "Times are in UTC."
The grid groups recorded chat messages by weekday and hour in the channel's time zone, including messages recorded while the channel was offline.
The current count also includes stored Twitch notices, held-message records and moderation notices, such as chat being cleared.
The **Busy days and hours** panel counts the same way, so it also includes offline chat, even though its on-screen line says chat follows your stream days.
It is therefore a count of recorded feed activity rather than a count of viewer-written messages alone.
It shows when this channel's chat was active during the chosen range, not a forecast of how many viewers a different schedule would bring.

## Read your YouTube numbers

The **YouTube** tab shows your own YouTube channel per day, newest first, and follows the **Range**.
A line above the table adds up the range, for example "1,200 views, 40 hr watched, 15 YouTube subscribers net."
The numbers come from YouTube and can be two days behind.

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Day** | The day. |
| **Views** | The views. |
| **Watch time** | The time watched. |
| **YouTube subscribers gained** and **YouTube subscribers lost** | Hidden on a narrow screen. |
| **Estimated revenue** | Shown only when YouTube reports revenue for the channel. A day without a value shows "None". |

Without revenue, the tab says "YouTube gives no revenue for this channel. Revenue needs the YouTube Partner Program."
When YouTube is not connected, the tab says so. Choose **Open connected services** and connect YouTube.
When the bot may not read your analytics yet, choose **Open connected services**, open **Details** on the YouTube row and allow **See your YouTube analytics**.
See [YouTube](/reference/integrations/youtube) for connecting.

## Save a stream recap

A stream recap is a shareable end-credit picture made with the raid recap overlay's layout.
The bot keeps one recap per stream when it goes offline, and can refresh it when the raid recap is shown at the end of a [raid](/reference/community/stream-page).
Saving a new recap for the same stream replaces the earlier one.

1. Open the **Recaps** tab.
   The **Stream recaps** table lists every kept recap, newest first.
2. Choose **Open** on a row.
   A dialog shows the recap as the overlay draws it, with its size under the title.
3. Choose **Save as PNG**.
   The browser saves the picture as a file named `recap-` with the stream date.

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Stream** | The day the stream started. |
| **Title** | The stream title. |
| **Length** | How long the stream ran. |
| **Peak** | The highest viewer count, or a dash when the bot has no count. |
| **Chat** | The chat lines in the stream. |

Choose a column name to sort by it.
The table shows 12 recaps to a page; use **Previous** and **Next** to move between pages.

The picture has the size of the stream it was made for.
A recap that stored no stream size is saved at 1920 x 1080, and the dialog says "(assumed, this recap stored no stream size)".

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**The Recaps tab says "No recap was kept yet. One is kept each time a raid starts."**
The bot has no kept recap for the channel.
The bot also keeps a recap when a stream goes offline, so a recap appears after the next stream goes offline or the next raid recap shows.

::

::Troubleshooting

**The PNG could not be made. Try again.**
The picture failed in the browser.
Choose **Save as PNG** again.

::

::Troubleshooting

**A panel says "This could not load."**
The dashboard could not read the numbers.
Choose **Try again** on the panel.

::
