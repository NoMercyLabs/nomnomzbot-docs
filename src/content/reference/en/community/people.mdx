title: People
description: Browse the real viewers of your channel, find anyone by name, and open a person to see their profile and act on them.

The **People** page gives you context about someone beyond the message they just sent.
You can find a returning viewer, check earlier moderation, leave a note for your team or change how the bot treats that person.
The list combines people the bot has seen with current Twitch lists such as subscribers, moderators and VIPs.

Choose a person to open their profile beside the list.
It is the same profile you open from a name in chat, so you can move from a live conversation to a broader view without learning a second set of controls.
The available information and actions depend on what the bot knows about the person and your permissions in the channel.

## Who can use this page

By default, a **Moderator**, **Lead moderator**, **Editor** or **Broadcaster** may open the page.
The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).
Some actions on a profile need a higher role (see [Act on a person](#act-on-a-person)).

## Browse the list

1. Open **People** in the sidebar.
   The **All** tab shows everyone the bot has seen in your chat.
2. Choose a tab above the list to see one group.
   The line under **People** shows the count and what the tab holds.
3. Choose **Last seen** or **A to Z** to change the order.
   The page keeps your choice the next time you open it.
4. Scroll down.
   The next part of the list loads when you reach the end.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **All** | Everyone the bot has seen in your chat. |
| **Moderators** | People who moderate your channel. |
| **VIPs** | People who hold the VIP badge on Twitch. |
| **Twitch subscribers** | People who subscribe to your channel on Twitch. |
| **Regulars** | People with at least 50 chat messages, first seen 30 days ago or more. |
| **New** | People first seen in the last 90 days, from the part of the list loaded so far. |
| **YouTube members** | People who pay for a membership of your YouTube channel. |

The **New** tab has no count on the tab itself, because it counts only what is loaded.
The line under **People** shows how many new people are loaded so far.
Scroll to load more people into it.

Each row shows the avatar, the name, and **Last seen** with the time, or **Not seen yet**.

| Mark on a row | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A gift icon | **Gifted Twitch subscription** |
| A **Banned** badge | The person is banned. |
| A shield icon | **Moderator** |
| A gem icon | **VIP** |

### See your YouTube members

The **YouTube members** tab is a table of **Member**, **Level**, **Member since** and **Months**, with a count such as "3 members".
The tab has no search and no sort.
When there are more members than fit, choose **Show more** at the bottom to load the next part.
On a phone the **Member since** column is hidden.
A YouTube member is a YouTube channel, not someone the bot has a profile for, so a row opens no profile.
The tab needs YouTube connected and the permission **See your channel members** allowed on the YouTube row of your connected services (see [YouTube](/reference/integrations/youtube)).
Google must also give the bot access to the member list.

| What the tab says | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| "This channel is not connected to YouTube." | Connect YouTube first. **Open connected services** takes you there. |
| "The bot may not read your YouTube members yet." | Allow **See your channel members** under **Details** on the YouTube row. |
| "Your YouTube channel has no channel memberships." | Memberships are not switched on for the channel. |
| "YouTube lists no members for your channel." | The list is empty. |

## Find a person

1. Type a name in **Search people**.
   The list changes to the matches, with name, avatar and login.
2. Press Enter to open the first match, or choose a match.
   The profile opens beside the list.

The search looks at every person the bot knows, also from other channels.
A match that your channel has never seen shows **Not in this channel**.
On most tabs, when nothing matches, the list shows "Nobody matches" with your text.
On the **Twitch subscribers** and **Regulars** tabs, the list shows that tab's empty text instead, so choose **All** before you search.

### Look up a name on Twitch

The bot may not know a person yet.
While you type, choose **Look up on Twitch** under the list.
The bot asks Twitch for that exact name and opens the person.

| Result | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Twitch has no account with that name | "Twitch has no account named" with the name. |
| Twitch did not answer | "Twitch could not be asked. Try again." |

## Open a profile

Choose a row to open the person's profile.
On a wide screen it opens beside the list.
On a phone it fills the screen, with **Back to the list** above it.

To close it, choose the close button, press Escape, or go back in the browser.
The address holds the person, so you can share a link that opens them.

The same profile opens when you choose a name elsewhere in the dashboard, in a dialog.
The dialog has **Open full page**, which opens the person on this page.

| Profile tab | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | The person at a glance. |
| **Chat** | Their chat messages. |
| **Emotes** | The emotes their own channel offers. |
| **Moderation** | Notes, history, **Actions taken** counts, and **Ban history**. |
| **Points** | Their points. Shows once the bot has a stored profile for them. |
| **Data** | What the bot stores about them. Shows once the bot has a stored profile for them. |
| **Settings** | How the bot treats them, their **Trust tier** and **Trust score**, and their text to speech voice. On your own profile it shows only the voice. |

**Ban history** lists each ban with its reason, who banned, the date, and the date it was lifted.
With no bans, it shows "No bans stored for this person."

### Leave context for other moderators

Use **Moderator notes** on the **Moderation** tab when the next moderator needs information that a ban reason or a message alone would not explain.
For example, a short note can record that the team has already spoken to someone about a recurring problem.
By default, moderators and higher roles may add, edit and delete these notes.

1. Type in **Add a note for the other moderators**.
2. Select **Pin to the top** if the note needs to stay prominent.
3. Choose **Add note**.

The list records the author and date.
Use **Edit** to correct a note and **Save note** to keep the change, or **Delete note** to remove one after confirming.

### Change bot standing or Twitch monitoring

**Bot standing** on the **Settings** tab changes how NomNomzBot responds to a person in this channel.
It is separate from a Twitch ban: choosing **Muted**, for example, makes the bot ignore their commands and other bot interactions while their chat remains visible.
By default, a lead moderator or higher may choose **Change standing**, select the treatment and an optional **Reason**, then **Save standing**.

The choices are **Normal**, **Muted**, **Shadowbanned** and **Blacklisted**.
The dialog explains each choice before you save it; **Normal** clears the special treatment.
Use Twitch's **Ban** or **Timeout** controls when the goal is to prevent someone chatting on Twitch.

The **Moderation** tab also offers **Twitch suspicious-user flag** to a lead moderator or higher.
Choose **Watch closely**, **Hold messages** or **Clear flag** to change Twitch's monitoring of that person.
The panel shows only Twitch's answer to your last change in the current session, so a blank status is not confirmation that Twitch has no flag.

### Review personal settings and saved data

The **Points** tab shows the person's wallet, total earned and spent, frozen state, giveaway participation and leaderboard visibility.
Use [Currency and balances](/reference/economy/currency-and-balances) when you need to adjust or freeze the wallet; the profile is a summary of those records.

The **Data** tab brings together their consent information, whether **Viewer statistics** count them or are turned off by their choice, active permits, command and song-request use, saved quotes, and data stored by the channel.
A permit is a grant of access to a role or action, possibly with an expiry.
Someone allowed to manage permits can choose **Revoke** beside an active permit; see [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions) for how access is granted.

The same tab contains **Own shoutout line**, where you can write a personal introduction for this streamer.
[Shoutouts](/reference/moderation/shoutouts) explains which introduction wins when several are available.

To change how this person's own messages sound, open **Settings** in their profile and choose **Change voice** beside **TTS voice**.
Search the available voices, listen to a sample, select one and choose **Save voice**.
Selecting **Channel voice** clears the person's saved voice; it does not permanently select the channel voice for them.
On their next spoken viewer message, the bot can assign and save another random English or multilingual voice, as [Text to speech](/reference/audio/text-to-speech#which-voice-speaks) explains.
By default, moderators and higher roles may change this setting; it affects the person's words, while the bot's own announcements use the channel voice.

## Act on a person

The action buttons sit at the bottom of the profile.
You see only the buttons your role may use.
Your own channel's account has no action buttons.

| Button | What it does | Who may use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shout out** | Sends a shoutout for the person. | A moderator and up |
| **Warn** | Asks for a reason, then sends a Twitch warning. The person must accept it before they chat again. | A moderator and up |
| **Timeout** | Opens **Time out for** with four lengths: 1 minute, 10 minutes, 1 hour and 1 day. | A moderator and up |
| **Ban** | Asks you to confirm, then bans the person. | A moderator and up |
| **Unban** | Lifts the ban. Shows in place of **Ban** for a banned person. | A moderator and up |
| **Make VIP** or **Remove VIP** | Gives or takes the VIP badge on Twitch. | The broadcaster |
| **Make moderator** or **Remove moderator** | Gives or takes the moderator role on Twitch. | The broadcaster |

A message at the bottom of the screen confirms **Shout out**, **Warn**, **Timeout**, **Unban** and the moderator change, such as "is now a moderator."
**Ban** and the VIP change show no message.
The profile updates instead: **Ban** turns into **Unban**, and the VIP button changes its label.
When one of them fails, an error shows.
**Warn**, **Timeout** and **Ban** never show on your own profile.
On a moderator's profile they show only to the broadcaster.
**Make VIP** is grayed out with **VIP status unknown** when Twitch could not tell the bot.

### Ban a person

1. Choose **Ban** on the profile.
   A dialog asks "Ban" with the name, and says the person cannot chat until you unban them.
2. Choose the **Ban** button with the name in the dialog.
   The bot bans the person on Twitch.

## Troubleshooting

::Troubleshooting

**The profile pane says "Nobody in the loaded lists has the login".**
The person in the link is not in any loaded list.
Type the name in **Search people**, or choose **Look up on Twitch**.

::

::Troubleshooting

**The Twitch subscribers tab is empty.**
It shows "Twitch lists no subscribers for your channel."
This means Twitch returned an empty subscriber list.
The tab shows the same text when a search finds nobody, so clear **Search people** first.
An error loading the list is a different result; use the page's retry control when the list could not load.

::
