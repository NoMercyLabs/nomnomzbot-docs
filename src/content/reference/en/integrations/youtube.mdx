YouTube has two different roles in NomNomzBot: it can be a streaming-platform connection for your channel, and it can supply videos for music requests.
Connecting your YouTube account lets the bot work with that channel through Google sign-in.
Once it is connected, the bot reads your YouTube live chat while you are live and feeds those messages into the same chat stream as your Twitch chat.
It also notes when your YouTube channel goes live, and it counts your viewers while you stream.
While you are offline, the bot only checks every 2 minutes whether you went live.
Playing a YouTube song request uses the **Music Player** source in OBS and does not require that account connection.

If you stream on YouTube, follow [Connect platforms](/reference/getting-started/connect-platforms), then check its status on **Stream** or **Integrations**.
If you only want YouTube music, go straight to [Music player](/reference/music/music-player) and [Song requests](/reference/music/song-requests).
This keeps music setup independent of permissions to manage your YouTube channel.

## Who can use this

| Role | What they may do |
| --- | --- |
| **Moderator**, **Lead moderator** | See the YouTube row and its status. |
| **Editor**, **Broadcaster** | Connect, reconnect and disconnect YouTube. |

The roles are explained on [Permissions](/reference/settings/permissions).

## What the connection asks for

**Connect** opens Google's own sign-in page in a new window.
It asks for one permission set, **Manage your channel**.
When you accept, the window closes and the row shows **Connected** with the YouTube account name.

## The YouTube row on the Stream page

The [Stream page](/reference/community/stream-page) has a panel **Where it goes**.
It lists Twitch, Kick, YouTube and X Live.
The YouTube row shows **Connected** with the account name, or **Not connected**.

A person who may change connections sees **Connect** beside **Not connected**.
The panel asks "Open YouTube to connect?" before the sign-in window opens.

## The YouTube row on the Integrations page

Open **Settings** in the sidebar, then **Integrations**.
The **Connected services** list has a **YouTube** row.

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Connected** | The connection works. The row shows "as" and the account name. |
| **Not connected** | No YouTube account is connected. |
| **Sign in again** | The saved YouTube login no longer works. |
| **Sign in again (the saved login is unreadable)** | The bot cannot read the saved login. |

Choose **Details** on the row to see the permission sets the channel allowed, such as **Permission sets: Manage your channel**.
After you allow the analytics, the list also shows **Read your channel**, because the analytics need it.
Choose **Reconnect** to sign in to Google again.

### Allow the member list and the analytics

**Connect** asks only for **Manage your channel**.
Two more permissions are separate, so a channel that does not need them never grants them.
When you may connect services, **Details** shows **You can also allow:** with one button for each permission not yet granted.

| Button | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| **See your channel members** | The **YouTube members** tab on [People](/reference/community/people), the **\{channel.members\}** template value and the YouTube member count on the Supporters page. |
| **See your YouTube analytics** | The **YouTube** view on [Analytics](/reference/community/analytics): views, watch time, subscribers gained and lost, and estimated revenue per day. |

A button opens Google's consent page for that one permission.
What the channel already allowed stays allowed.
**Reconnect** on a connected channel asks for **Manage your channel** and both extra permissions on one consent page, so use it when you want all three.
Until a permission is allowed, the **YouTube members** tab on People and the **YouTube** view on Analytics say so and point you back to **Details** on the YouTube row.
The Supporters page leaves out the YouTube members count.
Google must also give the bot access to the member list.
Until it does, the members stay hidden even after you allow **See your channel members**.
Revenue also needs the YouTube Partner Program; without it the analytics show no revenue.
A channel without channel memberships shows no members.

The YouTube member count is kept for 5 minutes, because every read costs Google quota.
When the bot cannot read the members, a template prints the placeholder as written, not as 0.

## Upload a clip to YouTube

The **Clips** panel on the [Stream page](/reference/community/stream-page) lists your Twitch clips, newest first.
Each clip can go to your own YouTube channel as a video.
This uses the YouTube connection, so connect YouTube first.

1. On the clip, choose **Upload to YouTube**.
2. Fill in **Title**, **Description** and **Who can see it**.
   The title starts as the clip's Twitch title.
3. Choose **Upload**.

**Who can see it** has three choices.
The first one, **Private: only you**, is the starting choice.

| Choice | Who sees the video |
| --- | --- |
| **Private: only you** | Only you. |
| **Unlisted: anyone with the link** | Anyone who has the link. |
| **Public: everyone** | Everyone. |

Google caps the uploads per day.
The panel shows how many are left, for example "1 YouTube upload left today".
When the upload works, the panel says "The clip is on your YouTube channel." and offers **Open the video**.

When an upload is refused, the dialog stays open and shows the reason.

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| This channel is not connected to YouTube. | Connect YouTube under **Connected services** first. |
| YouTube refused the upload for your account. | Connect YouTube again and allow the bot to manage your account. |
| The YouTube upload limit for today is used up. | Try again tomorrow. |

## What the music player does with YouTube

The [music player](/reference/music/music-player) can play YouTube videos.
The video plays in a music player overlay in your streaming software, not on your YouTube account.
That overlay tells the bot what it plays, and whether the video plays, is paused, has ended or failed.

| Report from the overlay | What the bot does |
| --- | --- |
| The video plays or is paused | The bot updates what the music player shows as now playing. |
| A song request ended or failed | The bot starts the next song request. |

Only one overlay plays the channel's YouTube music at a time.
Song requests from YouTube do not need the channel's YouTube connection.

## Disconnect YouTube

1. On the **YouTube** row of **Connected services**, choose **Disconnect**.
   A dialog **Disconnect YouTube?** opens.
   It lists related [pipelines](/reference/commands/pipelines) that use music actions.
2. Choose **Disconnect** in the dialog.
   A message says "YouTube is disconnected."
   The row shows **Not connected**.

After you disconnect, clip uploads to YouTube stop until you connect again.

The disconnect dialog's music list is broader than the account's actual role.
YouTube song requests do not need the channel's YouTube sign-in, so disconnecting this account is not the same as disabling YouTube music or removing its OBS player.
